On-Policy Distillation: what it buys and how it breaks

Qwen3-8B-Base, AIME'24, distilled from Qwen3-32B (Qwen3 Technical Report, Table 21). On-policy distillation reaches a higher score than RL at ~1/10 the GPU-hours — but it has characteristic failure modes.
On-policy distillation: 74.4% at 1,800 GPU-hours vs RL's 67.6% at 17,920 — +6.8 points for ~10× less compute. (And 9–30× cheaper than scaling SFT data to match; 50–100× for self-distillation.)
Height = AIME'24 score; the chip under each bar is GPU-hours. The win comes from density (O(N) teacher bits/token vs RL's O(1) scalar) plus shorter contexts and smaller batches — not from a better final objective.

Entropy collapse: unlike RL's gradual climb, on-policy distillation often shows a sudden reward jump that "corresponds with a drastic collapse in entropy" (nrehiew) — the student snaps onto a narrow mode. Compounding it, style tokens ("wait", "alright") carry far higher per-token KL than task tokens, so the loss can be hijacked by discourse phrasing. Per-token clipping caps any single token's contribution, keeping the update on task-relevant signal and slowing the collapse. Watch entropy as your early-warning gauge.
