What you should notice. At a 12-token budget (out of 32), H2O keeps the tokens with the largest cumulative attention mass — the "heavy hitters" — and tracks the diagonal + spike columns that actually carry the signal. Attention Sinks blindly keeps the first 4 tokens (the soft anchors that models trust by training accident) and the last 8 tokens (recency). For long-context Q&A this loses middle-of-context evidence; for chat continuation, sinks usually win because the model just needs to keep generating coherently. Random eviction is the floor — it tells you what your workload would look like if you cared nothing about which tokens matter. Drag the budget slider: watch H2O degrade gracefully while Random collapses fast.
