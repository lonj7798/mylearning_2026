# Excerpt: HelpSteer2 5-attribute RM — the core of Nemotron-4

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/model-reports/nemotron.md`
**Paper:** NVIDIA, *Nemotron-4 340B*, 2024.

---

## Why this source anchors ch-35

The Nemotron model-report is the single clearest public disclosure of a production reward-model recipe. From the source header:

> **Core Insight:** A reward model that outputs a vector of attribute scores (helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity) is a stronger judge than a scalar-only RM, and enables >98% of the alignment data to be synthetic.
>
> **Guideline:** Train a multi-attribute regression RM on HelpSteer2, then use it both for rejection sampling and for synthetic data filtering.

Ch-35 §1.1 extracts the 5-dim rubric verbatim; this excerpt records the architecture, training data, and aggregation logic behind it.

---

## The 5 attributes — what the RM actually predicts

From the source:

> HelpSteer2 dataset: 10K high-quality human preferences labeled with five attributes (helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity).

| # | Attribute | Learner-facing meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **helpfulness** | does the response address what was asked |
| 2 | **correctness** | are the facts, logic, or code right |
| 3 | **coherence** | is the response internally consistent and well-structured |
| 4 | **complexity** | does it match the intellectual depth the prompt needs |
| 5 | **verbosity** | is the length calibrated to the task |

Ten thousand examples. Each example has five scalar scores, not a preference winner/loser. The paper's argument for this labeling schema: a single preference bit collapses the full signal that a human annotator emitted; five scalars preserve more of it.

## The RM architecture

From the source, "Reward Model" section:

> - **Base:** Nemotron-4-340B-Base.
> - **Head:** replaced LM head with a regression MLP that outputs 5 floats (one per attribute).
> - **Training data:** HelpSteer2 — 10,000 human-labeled preferences with attribute scores.
> - **Loss:** per-attribute L2 regression.
> - **Aggregation for preference use:** weighted sum of attributes (weights documented in the NeMo-Aligner config).

The key architectural note: **a shared trunk with five linear heads**. The backbone is the full 340B base; only the output layer is replaced. Per-attribute L2 regression means each head is trained to predict its own scalar independently, with no cross-attribute coupling in the loss. The 5-vector is reduced to a preference scalar only at RL time via a weighted sum whose weights can be tuned without retraining the RM.

## Why the multi-attribute design amortizes 10K labels

The claim is that 10K preferences with 5-dim labels extract more training signal than 50K preferences with 1-dim labels. Three mechanisms:

1. **Per-head supervision density.** Each of 10K examples contributes 5 floats -> 50K scalar targets. A scalar RM on the same 10K examples gets 10K binary targets. The per-parameter signal-to-noise is ~5x higher.
2. **Reweighting without retraining.** If you decide verbosity should matter less at RL time, you change the aggregation weights. A scalar RM would require a new round of labeling with the new preference criterion.
3. **Failure-mode visibility.** If the policy starts hacking verbosity (writing longer to score higher), helpfulness/correctness scores diverge — a scalar RM would hide this.

The source notes the RM **ranks #1 on RewardBench at time of release**, which validates the design choice empirically.

## The self-bootstrapping synthetic loop

From the source, "Synthetic data pipeline (>98% of alignment data)":

> Three synthetic data types:
> 1. **Synthetic prompts** — generated by Nemotron-4-340B-Instruct itself across tasks.
> 2. **Synthetic responses** — two responses per prompt from the Instruct model (or earlier checkpoint).
> 3. **Synthetic preferences** — the multi-attribute RM scores response pairs; the scored pair becomes a preference datum.
> The pipeline is self-bootstrapping: each alignment round uses the previous round's Instruct model to generate data for the next round.

The three layers — prompts, responses, preferences — are all synthesized. The RM is the only non-learned component in the preference-label step; it grounds the whole loop. Self-bootstrapping iteration is what makes this pipeline *cheap* after the first round; it is also what opens the compounding-error risk ch-23 will make precise.

## What the source does *not* disclose

Two gaps that ch-35 has to flag:

- **The 5 aggregation weights.** The source says they are "documented in the NeMo-Aligner config" — not in the paper. A reader reproducing the recipe must read source code, not the paper alone.
- **The per-attribute annotation rubric.** The five attribute names are disclosed; the exact scoring rubric (0-4 Likert? 1-5? what do the endpoints mean?) is in the HelpSteer2 dataset card, not the main paper.
