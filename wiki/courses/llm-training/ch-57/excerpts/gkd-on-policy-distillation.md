# Excerpt: On-Policy Distillation of Language Models — Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes (GKD)

**Artifact.** Rishabh Agarwal, Nino Vieillard, Yongchao Zhou, Piotr Stanczyk, Sabela Ramos, Matthieu Geist,

## Problem statement (abstract)

"current KD methods for auto-regressive sequence models suffer from distribution mismatch between output
sequences seen during training and those generated by the student during inference. To address this issue, we
introduce Generalized Knowledge Distillation (GKD). Instead of solely relying on a fixed set of output
sequences, GKD trains the student on its self-generated output sequences by leveraging feedback from the
teacher on such sequences."

## The divergence (§2, Eq. 1)

  D_JSD(β)(P ‖ Q) = β · D_KL(P ‖ βP + (1−β)Q) + (1−β) · D_KL(Q ‖ βP + (1−β)Q)

- P — teacher distribution; Q — student distribution; β ∈ (0, 1) the bounded interpolation coefficient.
- §2: "Huszár (2015) show that lim_{β→0} D_JSD(β)(P ‖ Q)/β = D_KL(P ‖ Q). As such, gradients of JSD(β) behave
  similarly to forward KL and reverse KL when β is close to 0 and 1 respectively."
- Applied per token position and averaged over the sequence (§2, following Eq. 2).

## The objective (§3, Eq. after Algorithm 1)

  L_GKD(θ) := (1 − λ) · E_{(x,y)∼(X,Y)} D(p_T ‖ p_θS)(y|x) + λ · E_{x∼X} E_{y∼p_S(·|x)} D(p_T ‖ p_θS)(y|x)

- λ ∈ [0, 1] — student data fraction, "the fraction of on-policy student-generated outputs". λ = 1 is fully
  on-policy; λ = 0 is supervised KD on a fixed dataset.
- Algorithm 1 hyperparameters (L226-L232): student data fraction λ, divergence D, learning rate η. Each step
  draws u ~ U(0,1) and samples on-policy when u ≤ λ.
- §3: supervised KD and ImitKD-style approaches are recovered by setting D to forward KL with λ = 0 and λ = 1
  respectively.

**TRL mapping.** `GKDConfig.lmbda` is λ (default 0.5); `GKDConfig.beta` is β (default 0.5).
`DistillationConfig.beta` defaults to 1.0 (reverse KL), and that trainer is always on-policy.

## Task-specific results (§1, Fig. 1; §4.1-§4.3)

Teacher and students are T5 models; students T5-Small (77M), T5-Base (250M), T5-Large (800M).
Averaged across student sizes, on-policy GKD improved on the initial student by **2.1× on summarization
(XSum), 1.7× on machine translation (WMT), and 1.9× on arithmetic reasoning (GSM8K)**, relative to the
improvements the baseline KD methods achieved over the same student (§1, Fig. 1).

§4.2 ablation (T5-Small and T5-Base students, WMT): "generalized JSD divergences perform better than forward or
reverse KL but their performance gap reduces when using a larger student. Moreover, using purely on-policy and
mixed data distributions consistently outperform GKD variants only using a fixed supervised dataset."

## Task-agnostic result — the one the chapter uses for breadth (§4.4, Fig. 10)

- Teacher: FLAN T5-XL. Student: FLAN T5-Base.
- Data: FLAN2021 instruction-tuning dataset, 5.36 million examples spanning 62 tasks.
- Evaluation: two **held-out** suites — MMLU (57 tasks) and BBH (23 tasks) — by few-shot prompting, averaged
  across tasks.
- Checkpoints after 50K training steps.
- Result (§1): on-policy GKD gives "2% and 1% absolute accuracy improvement on the held-out BBH and MMLU
  benchmark suites (Figure 10)". The paper does not state which baseline that improvement is measured against;
  Fig. 10's caption gives the teacher FLAN T5-XL at 52.4% (MMLU) and 41% (BBH) and the student at 35.6% and
  31.25%. (The caption names the student "T5-large" while §4.4 names FLAN T5-Base.) §4.4: "on-policy GKD with reverse KL substantially outperforms supervised KD
  and ImitKD. Notably, in the context of instruction tuning, we find that using reverse KL performs much better
  than forward KL."
- The explanation given is an **Interpretation**, not a measurement: "We hypothesize that the efficacy of
  reverse KL in instruction tuning may stem from its mode-seeking nature as it ensures that the model zeroes in
  on the main intent or behavior specified by the instruction."

## Divergence versus diversity (§4, Fig. 4 caption)

"Transitioning from forward KL to reverse KL, through generalized JSD, leads to decreased diversity, attributed
to the enhanced mode-seeking characteristic of the divergence. Mode-seeking divergences often yield superior
quality, especially at high temperatures (γ = 1). Reducing the temperature curtails diversity while narrowing
performance differences among divergences." Diversity is quantified by Self-BLEU (Zhu et al., 2018), where 100
indicates deterministic outputs and 0 maximum diversity.

## Combination with RL (§1, Fig. 5)

The paper reports that on-policy GKD can be combined with RL fine-tuning (RLAIF), "a combination that has not
been previously explored".

## Conditions and limits

Encoder-decoder T5 models up to 800M parameters as students; four task families; one instruction-tuning corpus.
No decoder-only or multi-billion-parameter student is evaluated. **Result (single study)** for every number
above.
