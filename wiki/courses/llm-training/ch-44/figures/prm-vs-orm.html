Monte-Carlo step labels and solution-score aggregation

Companion to ch-44 read.md §2.2 (panel A) and §1.4 with §3.5 (panel B).

A. Error rates of a hard Monte-Carlo step label

Curves are the two binomial expressions in read.md §2.2: P(false negative) = (1 − p)^N on a correct step, P(false positive) = 1 − (1 − q)^N on an incorrect step, under the hard label "correct if any completion reaches the gold answer" (threshold 0, as recommended in arXiv:2501.07301v2 §3.1.4). p and q are set by the reader; they are not measured values. The measured anchor is Math-Shepherd (arXiv:2312.08935v3 §5.2, Fig. 4a): hard labels from a LLaMA2-70B completer are 86% accurate against 160 human-labelled GSM8K steps at N = 4, and accuracy declines for larger N, which the authors attribute to false positives.

Scores are arbitrary reader input. The three aggregators are the ones used in the literature: the product of per-step P(positive) with neutral counted as positive is the PRM800K default (arXiv:2305.20050v1 App. F.2, where best-of-1860 gives 78.2 for the product and 77.6 for the minimum, and the paper notes a slight bias against solutions with more steps); the minimum is used by Math-Shepherd (arXiv:2312.08935v3 §3.4); the last-step score is the one recommended for Monte-Carlo-trained PRMs, whose per-step values already estimate future success (arXiv:2501.07301v2 §3.2.4, Fig. 9).
