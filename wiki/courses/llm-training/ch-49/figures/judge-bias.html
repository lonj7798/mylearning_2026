Judge position consistency and length control

Two panels for chapter 49. Panel 1 applies the conservative swap rule of Zheng et al. 2023 §3.4 to a chosen number of evaluation pairs, using the measured consistency rates of Table 2. Panel 2 evaluates the AlpacaEval length-controlled GLM (Dubois et al. 2024, Eq. 1 and Eq. 2) and shows how much of a predicted win rate the length term accounts for. Every preset number is quoted from the source named in the caption below each panel.

Panel 1 — how many pairs survive the swap rule

QuantityValue
Judge calls (two orders per pair)400
Pairs with a consistent verdict (decided)130
Pairs counted as ties by the conservative rule70
Half-width of a 95% interval on a 50% win rate, from decided pairs±8.6 pp

The interval uses the normal approximation 1.96 · sqrt(p(1−p)/n_decided) at p = 0.5. It is shown to make one point: the swap rule removes inconsistent pairs from the sample, so a judge with lower consistency produces a wider interval at the same evaluation size. It is not a substitute for the bootstrap procedure used by the benchmarks in §1 of the chapter.

Source: Zheng et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.05685, Table 2 (consistency by judge and prompt), Table 12 (few-shot judge, GPT-4 65.0% → 77.5%), §3.4 (conservative swap rule: a win counts only if the verdict is unchanged after swapping; otherwise the pair is a tie).

Panel 2 — the length term in the AlpacaEval GLM

Predicted preference for one instruction: logistic( θ_m − θ_b + φ · tanh(d) ), where d is the length difference in units of its standard deviation. The length-controlled value sets the length term to zero: logistic( θ_m − θ_b ).

QuantityValue
Raw predicted preference71.3%
Length-controlled preference57.4%
Points attributed to the length difference13.9 pp

Reported effect of this control on the whole benchmark: the normalized standard deviation of a model's win rate across three verbosity prompts fell from 25% to 10%; gpt4_1106_preview's spread narrowed from 22.9–64.3% to 41.9–51.6%; the Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena rose from 0.94 to 0.98 over the 38 models present on both leaderboards. A constant 205-token response still reached 86.5% length-controlled win rate, so the control removes a confounder rather than preventing manipulation of the annotator.

Sources: Dubois et al. 2024, arXiv:2404.04475, Eq. 1, Eq. 2, §4.1, §4.2, Table 1; Zheng, Pang et al. 2024, arXiv:2410.07137, Table 2 (null-model result). The slider values θ_m − θ_b, φ and d are illustrative inputs, not fitted values from the paper.
