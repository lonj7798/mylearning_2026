# Excerpt: RL's Razor — Why Online Reinforcement Learning Forgets Less

**Primary source:** Idan Shenfeld, Jyothish Pari, Pulkit Agrawal (Improbable AI Lab, MIT).
arXiv:2509.04259 v1, 2025-09-04. Read on 2026-09-17.
**Source type:** paper.

---

## Core claim

Fine-tuning a model π on a new task τ causes forgetting in proportion to E_{x∼τ}[KL(π₀ ‖ π)] — the KL
divergence between the base policy π₀ and the fine-tuned policy π, evaluated on the *new task*
distribution. The paper calls this an empirical forgetting law and shows it holds across training
algorithms, so the SFT-versus-RL difference is a consequence of where each algorithm lands in KL, not of
the optimizer. "RL's Razor" is the claim that on-policy policy-gradient updates are implicitly biased
toward the KL-minimal solution among those that solve the new task (§1, §5).

---

## Setting (§3.1)

- Model for the LLM results: **Qwen 2.5 3B-Instruct**.
- New tasks: math questions from the Open-Reasoner-Zero dataset; the Chemistry L-3 subset of
  SciKnowEval; tool use on ToolAlpaca. A robotics arm uses OpenVLA 7B in SimplerEnv.
- RL method: **GRPO with a binary success indicator as the reward and no explicit KL regularization**.
- Forgetting is measured on HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, MMLU, IFEval, Winogrande and HumanEval.
- Dozens of models were trained per method across hyperparameters; results are reported as Pareto
  frontiers of new-task score against prior-task score.

---

## Results used by ch-56

1. **RL retains, SFT trades (§3.1, Fig. 2).** As RL raises new-task accuracy, prior-benchmark scores stay
   nearly unchanged; SFT reaches comparable new-task accuracy while prior scores fall, most sharply on the
   math task.
2. **KL predicts forgetting (§4, Fig. 3 middle).** In a controlled ParityMNIST/FashionMNIST setting with a
   3-layer MLP, forgetting plotted against KL collapses onto a single curve for both SFT and RL; a
   quadratic fit gives **R² = 0.96**. In the LLM experiments the same fit gives **R² = 0.71** (Fig. 11),
   with mean-zero residuals the authors attribute to approximate KL and accuracy estimation.
3. **The algorithm is not the cause (§4, "Optimal SFT Distribution").** An oracle SFT distribution
   constructed to minimize KL while reaching 100% accuracy on ParityMNIST retained *more* prior knowledge
   than RL. SFT trained on data generated by an RL-trained model matched RL's trade-off (Fig. 9).

---

## Conditions and limits

One model family at 3B for the LLM results, six benchmarks, one RL algorithm, and no explicit KL penalty
in the RL runs. The paper does not test whether adding a KL penalty to RL moves the same curve, and it
introduces no training algorithm. The KL it measures is KL(π₀ ‖ π) on the new-task distribution, which is
the reverse direction of the log π_θ − log π_ref estimate that RL frameworks log on their own rollouts;
the framework metric is a correlated proxy, not the paper's quantity.
