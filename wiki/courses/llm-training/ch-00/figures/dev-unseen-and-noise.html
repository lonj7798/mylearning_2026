Development versus unseen changes, and whether a difference exceeds its error

Companion to ch-00 §4 and §6.1. Panel 1 shows, for each Tülu 3 8B SFT training-data source, how much the development benchmark and the unseen benchmark of each skill change when that source is removed. Panel 2 computes the standard error of a difference between two scores so the reader can check whether a reported change is larger than its sampling error.

Panel 2 — Is the difference larger than its standard error?

Scores are treated as 0/1 per item. For fractional per-item scores such as F1 or pass@10 estimates, the 0/1 formula gives an upper bound on the standard error, so a difference that fails Condition 1 here is not shown to be measured, but it is not shown to be noise either. Enter the per-item correlation between the two models when both were scored on the same items; 0 gives the unpaired result.

Unpaired 95% interval Paired 95% interval

    Formulas: SE = sqrt(p(1 − p)/n); SE_unpaired = sqrt(SE_A² + SE_B²); SE_paired = sqrt(SE_A² + SE_B² − 2·SE_A·SE_B·ρ); interval = difference ± 1.96·SE (Miller, arXiv:2411.00640v1 §2.1, §4). Presets reproduce ch-00 §6.1: HumanEval (164 items) Tülu 3 8B SFT 86.2 vs DPO 83.9 (Table 31) with an assumed ρ = 0.6 gives SE 2.69 and 2.87, unpaired SE 3.94, paired SE 2.49, z = 0.92, interval (−2.6, 7.2), all upper bounds because pass@10 per-item scores are fractional; BigCodeBench-Hard (148 tasks) 11.5 vs 7.4 (Table 32) gives unpaired SE 3.39, z = 1.21. The MMLU-size preset uses hand numbers (65.0 vs 64.0) on 14,042 items; its SE of A is 0.40 points, as in §6.1. For reference, seed SDs for ten Llama-2-7B-architecture runs, averaged over 21 checkpoints from 10B to 210B tokens, are 0.80 (ARC-C), 1.11 (HumanEval), 0.21 (HellaSwag) (Madaan et al., arXiv:2406.10229v1 §3.1, Table 1); the Tülu 3 70B SFT average has a seed SD of 1.33 over three seeds (Table 14). A seed SD from one setting does not transfer to another model size or benchmark.
