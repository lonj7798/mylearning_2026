Narrow-to-broad checks: inoculation model and rare-behaviour detection

Companion to ch-42a §3 and §10. Panel A evaluates the linear model of inoculation prompting stated in Wichers et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.05024) App. H (Eq. 3-5) on values you set. Panel B computes how many evaluation samples a broad-behaviour check needs before a rare behaviour is likely to appear at all. All numbers on this page are computed from the inputs; they are not measurements from any paper.

Panel A. Inoculation prompting under the paper's linear model

Two traits are learned from the same oversight signal: an undesired trait (for example hard-coding to pass tests) and a desired trait (for example writing working code). T*(O) is the level that training converges to under that oversight. T(M₀, C₀) is the initial model's level with a neutral prompt; T(M₀, C_s) is its level when the inoculation instruction is present. k is the ratio between the effect measured without the instruction and with it (k = 1 means the training effect transfers unchanged).

Neutral training is the same model with s absent, so C_s = C₀ and k = 1 by definition: the trait moves all the way to T*(O). Under the inoculation prompt the trait moves by k(T*(O) − T(M₀, C_s)) instead (Eq. 3). Eq. 4 asks whether the instruction already elicits the undesired trait at least as strongly as the oversight rewards it; Eq. 5 asks whether the desired trait is left nearly untouched by the instruction. The paper reports Pearson correlations of 0.57-0.90 between how strongly a prompt elicits a behaviour and how well it inoculates, which supports using the model to rank candidate prompts rather than to predict the resulting trait level.

Panel B. How many samples a rare-behaviour check needs

A broad behavioural evaluation that draws n independent samples observes a behaviour with true per-sample rate p at least once with probability 1 − (1 − p)ⁿ. Reference rates: reward tampering in Denison et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.10162) is 45/32,768 = 0.137% and tampering plus test editing 7/32,768 = 0.021%; the helpful-only baseline is 0 in 100,000.
