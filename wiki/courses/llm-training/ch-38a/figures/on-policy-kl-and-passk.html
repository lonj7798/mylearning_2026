On-policy updates, KL to the base model, and pass@k

Three panels for ch-38a. Panel 1 computes the KL cost of two ways to reach the same task accuracy. Panel 2 computes pass@k from sample counts and shows when an RL model that is ahead at k = 1 falls behind at larger k. Panel 3 shows measured diversity across three post-training stages of OLMo 2.

1. Two distributions that both solve the task

Base distribution π₀ over five candidate answers for one prompt. Mark the answers a verifier accepts. The rejection-sampling projection q_RS is π₀ restricted to the accepted set and renormalized; this is the distribution policy gradient converges to with a binary reward (Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 of arXiv:2509.04259v1 §5.2). The SFT target q_SFT places all mass on one accepted answer chosen by an external annotator. Both reach 100% accuracy. The reported quantity is the reverse KL D(q ‖ π₀) in nats, which equals −ln π₀(accepted set) for q_RS and −ln π₀(target) for q_SFT.

2. pass@k from sample counts

Unbiased estimator of arXiv:2504.13837v5 App. A.2: pass@k = mean over problems of 1 − C(n − c, k) / C(n, k), with n samples per problem and c correct. The default numbers are constructed for this course, not taken from a source; they reproduce the shape reported in §3.2, where the RL model leads at k = 1 and the base model leads at larger k.
