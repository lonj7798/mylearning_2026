# Excerpt: RL's Razor: Why Online Reinforcement Learning Forgets Less

**Authors:** Idan Shenfeld, Jyothish Pari, Pulkit Agrawal (Improbable AI Lab, MIT).
**Version read:** arXiv:2509.04259v1 (4 Sep 2025).
**Status:** no library card existed for this slug on 2026-09-15; quotes and numbers read at the stated loci in the v1 PDF. A shorter excerpt for ch-30a exists at `ch-30a/excerpts/rls-razor.md`; this one adds §5 and §6.

## Forgetting law (Abstract, §4)
"the degree of forgetting is determined by the distributional shift, measured as the KL-divergence between the fine-tuned and base policy evaluated on the new task." The predictor is E_{x∼τ}[KL(π₀ ‖ π)] — the forward KL, with inputs x drawn from the new task τ, π₀ the base policy and π the fine-tuned policy.

## Tasks and models (§3.1)
Qwen 2.5 3B-Instruct on (a) math from Open-Reasoner-Zero, (b) the Chemistry L-3 subset of SciKnowEval, (c) ToolAlpaca; OpenVLA-7B on SimplerEnv pick-and-place. Prior-task suite for LLMs: HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, MMLU, IFEval, Winogrande, HumanEval, run with the Language Model Evaluation Harness. RL is GRPO with a binary success reward and no explicit KL regularization. SFT targets for math come from DeepSeek R1 (up to 16 samples per prompt, one correct response kept; valid for 96% of the dataset) and for science from GPT-4o (App. B.1).

## Pareto protocol (App. B.1)
Dozens of runs per method over the Table 2 sweep; models within 2 accuracy points of the Pareto frontier are kept; an exponential curve is fitted to those points.

## Fit quality (§4, §6)
ParityMNIST toy setting (3-layer MLP pretrained jointly on ParityMNIST and FashionMNIST): a quadratic fit of forgetting against forward KL reaches R² = 0.96. LLM experiments: R² = 0.71 (Fig. 11); "the residuals are mean-zero and can be attributed to noise from approximate KL and accuracy estimation". Table 1 (MNIST, R² of a second-degree polynomial): forward KL 0.96 ± 0.01; reverse KL 0.93 ± 0.01; total variation 0.80 ± 0.01; distribution change L2 0.56 ± 0.02; weight change L1 0.34 ± 0.02; Fisher-weighted L2 0.58 ± 0.02; spectral norm 0.58 ± 0.02; activation change L1 0.52 ± 0.02, L2 0.55 ± 0.02.

## Which difference matters (§5.1, Fig. 4)
Four objectives on the Science Q&A task, arranged by (on-policy vs offline) × (negative gradient vs none): GRPO (on-policy, negatives), 1–0 REINFORCE (on-policy, A = 1 for correct and 0 otherwise, "equivalent to sampling from the model and performing SFT on correct answers only"), SFT (offline, no negatives), SimPO (offline, negatives sampled from an external model). Result: "the critical factor is not the presence of negative gradients but the use of on-policy data"; GRPO and 1–0 REINFORCE reach the same task performance at smaller KL than SFT and SimPO.

## Theory (§5.2)
Lemma 5.1: for a distribution p over a finite set Y and binary reward R, rejection sampling from p accepting R(y) = 1 yields q_RS = argmin_q D_KL(q ‖ p) subject to E_{y∼q}[R(y)] = 1. Theorem 5.2: under stated regularity conditions, policy gradient on a binary reward converges to π† = argmin_{π ∈ P* ∩ Π} D_KL(π ‖ π₀), where P* is the set of policies with E[R] = 1 and Π the representable family. Note the direction: the lemma and theorem are about reverse KL(π ‖ π₀), while the empirical forgetting law uses forward KL(π₀ ‖ π).

## Oracle-SFT control (§4)
In ParityMNIST the KL-minimal labeling consistent with 100% accuracy can be computed in closed form (q* = argmin_q D_KL(π₀ ‖ q) s.t. qᵀR = 1, App. B.3). SFT on that oracle distribution "retained more prior knowledge than RL, achieving the best trade-off observed". SFT on data generated by an RL-trained model matched RL's trade-off (Fig. 9).

## Alternative hypotheses ruled out (§6)
Weight-level, representation-level, and rank measures track forgetting weakly. On update sparsity: "we found that the reason for the observed sparse updates was the use of bfloat16 for model training … Performing the same training with float32 resulted in models with identical performance but without any sparsity in their weight updates." All algorithms produced full-rank weight changes. Representation similarity: CKNNA to the base model 0.94 after RL and 0.56 after SFT (App. C.1, Fig. 7). Scale: Qwen 2.5 Instruct at 3B, 7B and 14B show the same SFT trade-off (App. C.2, Fig. 8).

## Hyperparameters (App. B.1, Table 2)
Base model Qwen2.5 3B-Instruct. SFT/SimPO: LR sweep {1e-5, 3e-5, 5e-5, 7e-5, 9e-5}, AdamW, scheduler {constant with warmup, cosine with warmup}, warmup 50 steps, epochs {1, 2}, batch {16, 32, 64, 128}, max grad norm 1, bfloat16, weight decay 0. RL: LR sweep {1e-5, 2e-5, 3e-5, 4e-5, 5e-5}, constant with warmup, warmup 50, 1 epoch, KL regularization 0, group size 64, prompts per generation 8, num iterations μ ∈ {1, 2}, Dr. GRPO loss.

## Stated limits (§7)
"we still lack a mechanistic account of why larger KL shifts on the new task disrupt prior knowledge"; behaviour "at frontier scales and in more diverse generative domains remains unknown"; online but off-policy algorithms were not studied.

## Conflict to record
[[retaining-by-doing]] App. A.5 reports Pearson correlation 0.52 between KL and non-target drop and a non-monotone Self-SFT-versus-SFT comparison, and states that the KL–forgetting connection "does not always hold in our setting".
