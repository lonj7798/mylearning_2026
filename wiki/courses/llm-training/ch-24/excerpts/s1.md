# Excerpt: s1 — 1K selected long traces, budget forcing, and the sequence-length ablation

**Checked on 2026-09-15 against arXiv:2501.19393v3 (full PDF including appendices).** The library card `papers/s1.md` had no verification section at that date; this excerpt is the checked extract used by ch-24 §4, §8, and the Recipe.

## Data selection (§2)

- Pool: 59,029 questions from 16 sources; traces and answers from Google Gemini Flash Thinking API; decontaminated against MATH500, GPQA Diamond, AIME24 with 8-grams (§2.1).
- Quality: remove API errors (54,116 left) and formatting problems such as ASCII art or missing image references (51,581 left); 384 samples from sources judged high quality are included directly (§2.2).
- Difficulty: remove questions solved by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, graded by Claude 3.5 Sonnet against the reference (24,496 left) (§2.2).
- Diversity: choose a Mathematics Subject Classification domain uniformly, then a question with probability favoring longer traces; repeat to 1,000 questions in 50 domains (§2.2).
- 53.6% of s1K traces are graded correct; incorrect traces are kept (§2.2).

## Budget forcing and results

- To cap thinking, append the end-of-thinking delimiter (optionally "Final Answer:"); to extend, suppress the delimiter and append "Wait" (§3.1).
- s1-32B: AIME24 56.7, MATH500 93.0, GPQA Diamond 59.6; without budget forcing 50.0 / 92.6 / 56.6; Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct 26.7 / 84.0 / 49.0 (Table 1, temperature 0).
- Extension raises AIME24 from 50% to 57% and flattens at six forced continuations; too much suppression causes repetitive loops (Abstract, §4.2).
- Data ablations with a ≈30,000-token cap (Table 2, AIME24 / MATH500 / GPQA): 1K-random 36.7 / 90.6 / 52.0; 1K-diverse 26.7 / 91.2 / 54.6; 1K-longest 33.3 / 90.4 / 59.6; 59K-full 53.3 / 92.8 / 58.1; s1K 50.0 / 93.0 / 57.6. The 59K run took 394 H100 GPU-hours vs 7 (§5.1).
- The authors state that the context window limits further scaling with budget forcing (§6.2). In a separate step-conditional run prompted to use up to 512 steps, 12 of 30 AIME24 responses exceeded the context window (§6.2).
- s1.1 (traces regenerated with DeepSeek r1): AIME 2024 56.7, AIME 2025 50.0 without forcing; s1 without forcing: 50.0 and 26.7 (App. A, Table 5; the caption attributes AIME 2025 values to Ye et al., 2025b).

## Training (App. D) and the sequence-length ablation (App. D.1)

- Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct; 5 epochs; batch 16; 315 steps; LR 1e-5, linear warmup 5% (16 steps), cosine decay to 0; AdamW β1 0.9, β2 0.95; weight decay 1e-4; bfloat16; loss only on reasoning traces and solutions; sequence length set so no sample is cut; 26 minutes on 16 H100.
- Table 8: training length 4,096 cut 74% of samples → AIME24 30.0% with 20,721 average thinking tokens, MATH500 90.0% / 5,324, GPQA 52.5% / 6,841; length 32,768 cut 0% → 50.0% / 6,984, 91.0% / 3,268, 53.0% / 3,568.
- The authors' explanation: with a shorter length "the answer section of the training sample is more commonly cut off", so the answer section gets fewer gradient updates and test-time reasoning becomes longer.
- Evaluation note: vLLM results changed with batch size and continuation settings even with greedy decoding and fixed seeds (App. B).
