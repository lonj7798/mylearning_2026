# Excerpt: s1 — 1K curated traces plus "Wait"-token budget forcing

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/papers/s1.md`
**Paper:** Muennighoff et al. 2025, "s1: Simple test-time scaling" (Stanford / UW / Anthropic alumni)

---

## Why this source anchors ch-24 §4

s1 is one of the two 2025 papers (with [[limo]]) that demolished the volume-first orthodoxy for reasoning SFT. Its specific contribution beyond LIMO is **budget forcing** — an inference-time trick that extends reasoning compute post-SFT without re-training.

---

## The data recipe — three filters against a 59K pool

From the source (§Data Recipe):

- Candidate pool: 59K questions.
- Selected: **s1K = 1,000 question-trace pairs**.
- Curation criteria: **difficulty** (strong baselines still miss), **diversity** (topic spread), **quality** (trace hand-check).
- Traces: generated by Gemini, paired with answers, filtered to the final 1K.
- Base: Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct.
- Training: **26 minutes on 16 H100 GPUs** with PyTorch FSDP.

The 26-minute training run is the operational punchline. A 32B reasoning model from zero to SOTA-on-competition-math in less time than it takes to launch an OMI-2-scale training job's first optimizer step.

---

## Budget forcing — the inference-time hack

From the source (§Technical Details):

> Budget forcing is implemented by ignoring the end-of-thinking stop and repeatedly appending `"Wait"`.

The mechanism is minimal. The model is trained with thinking/answer delimiters. At inference, whenever the model tries to emit the end-of-thinking token, the decoder:

1. Suppresses the token.
2. Appends the literal string `"Wait"` to the context.
3. Resumes sampling.

The model, conditioned on its prior reasoning plus a trailing `"Wait"`, produces more thinking. Repeat until budget exhausted (or context limit hit). Exit, then generate the answer.

The source's framing matters: this is **inference-time compute reallocation, not new capability**.

> Inference hack, not new capability: budget forcing mostly reallocates compute already latent in the model.

Ch-24 §4 quotes the reported AIME24 curve: 50% → 57% on the same checkpoint purely by extending the forced budget. That delta is *not* from re-training; the weights are frozen. The model had the capability; budget forcing prevents it from stopping early.

---

## Numbers that matter

From the source (§Technical Details):

- s1-32B: **56.7 AIME24, 93.0 MATH500, 59.6 GPQA-Diamond**.
- Dataset: 1K samples, math-heavy.
- Training cost: 26 min × 16 H100 ≈ 7 H100-hours.

For comparison, OpenMathInstruct-2 (ch-24 §2) reports 67.8 MATH on OpenMath2-Llama3.1-8B from 14M samples and ~650K H100-hours of teacher compute alone. The s1 result trades 6× more parameters (32B vs 8B) for *six orders of magnitude* less teacher compute — because it's not distilling, it's activating.

## Caveats that scale with the approach

From the source (§Risks + Gotchas):

- **Teacher dependence**: traces are Gemini-generated, so quality is bounded by what Gemini can produce. Activation, not installation, but the template matters.
- **Context limits**: aggressive budget forcing runs into the context window and repetition issues. Authors' reports max out around 32K reasoning tokens before quality degrades.
- **Dataset narrowness**: 1K math-heavy samples may not transfer to non-math reasoning. LIMO has some evidence of broader transfer; s1 is cautious about the claim.
- **Base-model dependence**: a 32B-class base is essential. Weak bases (7B non-reasoning-tuned) do not activate from 1K.

## The curation philosophy — why this belongs in ch-24 §4

The data-pipeline description is the key to the thesis. From the source (§Data Recipe):

> Difficulty, diversity, and quality, each validated through ablations.

Ch-24 §4 frames this as "trace style does the work." The ablation detail: random 1K samples from the same 59K pool score substantially lower on AIME24 (~24) than the s1K selection (56.7). The 32× gain is purely curation — same base model, same total training compute, same total traces, different filter.

The s1 curation operators are not as cleanly separable as MetaMath's four operators. They are *hand policies* applied to a Gemini-generated candidate set. Reproducibility depends on matching the three criteria, not on a mechanical pipeline. This is simultaneously the method's strength (it works) and its weakness (curator subjectivity is a real confound).
