Agreement and Label Noise Calculator

Companion to ch-15 read.md §2, §3, and §7. Panel A converts raw agreement into Cohen's κ. Panel B converts per-annotator accuracy into pairwise agreement and majority-vote label noise for a mix of clear and split items. Panel C shows how random label flips shrink the reward margin a Bradley-Terry model learns, and Panel D shows the loss gradient for pairs the model ranks with or against their label. All panels compute model quantities from the formulas in read.md; they do not reproduce a source experiment.

A. Raw agreement to Cohen's κ (two annotators, two labels)

pe = p1(A)p2(A) + p1(B)p2(B); κ = (po − pe)/(1 − pe). Agreement values: InstructGPT, arXiv:2203.02155 §3.4 (72.6 ± 1.5%); HH-RLHF, arXiv:2204.05862 §2.1 (about 63%). The sources report raw agreement only; label shares of 50% assume randomized response order. Some combinations of po and shares are not jointly possible; the panel flags them.

B. Per-annotator accuracy to agreement and majority-vote noise

Each annotator gives the label that the annotator population prefers with probability q, independently. Clear items use qc; split items use qs (0.5 means the population is divided evenly).

clear itemssplit itemsall items

Pairwise agreement a = (1 − s)[qc2 + (1 − qc)2] + s[qs2 + (1 − qs)2]. Majority of n (n odd) matches the population label with probability Σk>n/2 C(n,k) qk(1 − q)n−k. With two labels and odd n a majority always exists. On split items with qs = 0.5 there is no correct label, so "noise" there means disagreement with a coin-flip population preference.

C. Random label flips and the learned reward margin

Recorded win probability p' = (1 − ε)σ(Δ) + εσ(−Δ); a flexible model fit to these labels learns Δ' = logit(p'). The ordering is preserved (Δ' increases with Δ) and |Δ'| < |Δ| for ε > 0. Systematic label bias, such as a preference for length, is not modeled here.

D. Bradley-Terry loss gradient by current margin

L = −log σ(Δ), |∂L/∂Δ| = σ(−Δ), with Δ = r(x, yw) − r(x, yl) measured in the direction of the recorded label. Δ = 3: 0.047 (easy, already separated). Δ = 0: 0.5. Δ = −3: 0.953 (the model confidently disagrees with the label, which includes mislabeled pairs).
