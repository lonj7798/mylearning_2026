# Excerpt: HelpSteer2: Open-source dataset for training top-performing reward models

Authors: Zhilin Wang, Yi Dong, Olivier Delalleau, Jiaqi Zeng, Gerald Shen, Daniel Egert, Jimmy J. Zhang, Makesh Narsimhan Sreedhar, Oleksii Kuchaiev (NVIDIA). Source type: paper. Read in the v1 PDF text on 2026-09-15 for ch-15 §1, §2, §3, §6, §7 and Recipe.

## Prompts and responses (§2.1)
- "Most of the prompts (over 95%)" come from ShareGPT user turns; a small share of proprietary prompts covers summarization, closed QA, and extraction.
- Non-English prompts were removed because the annotators were US-based and "not expected to be competent in other languages"; prompts containing code snippets were removed because "our annotators lacked expertise in the coding domain".
- Prompts were clustered with BERTopic into about 1000 topics and sampled uniformly per topic; prompt complexity was rated on Likert-5 by Nemotron-2-43B and sampled uniformly per level, with the highest level given twice the weight.
- Multi-turn prompts are about 29% of samples; their assistant turns were regenerated by an internal model.
- Two responses per prompt ("instead of four as in HelpSteer ... to minimize annotators' cognitive load"), always from two different sources: Nemotron-2 18.9%, Nemotron-3 40.4%, Nemotron-4 26.9%, Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct 7.9%, human annotators from Scale AI 5.9%.

## Annotation (§2.1)
- Five attributes (helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity), each on a Likert-5 scale.
- "Each sample is initially annotated by three annotators. If these annotators demonstrate a high level of disagreement (i.e. the difference in helpfulness among them is greater than 2), two additional annotators are recruited"; average 3.41 annotators per sample.
- The two responses to a prompt are rated sequentially, which the authors report gives more calibrated scores.
- "approximately 1,000 US-based annotators through our vendor Scale AI compared to 200 annotators engaged in HelpSteer".
- "We used up to five annotators per sample but only retained annotations from the three most in agreement." Each annotation had at least two human reviews plus automated checks; annotations from "untrusted" annotators or those with consistently low agreement were removed.
- Guideline clarifications addressed edge cases, "e.g., whether coherence should consider previous turns, and how helpfulness should be evaluated if prompt instructions are unclear".

## Agreement (§2.1, Table 1; quadratic weighted Cohen's κ)
| Attribute | Initial collection | After improvements | Post-processing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Helpfulness | 0.465 | 0.706 | 0.791 |
| Correctness | 0.472 | 0.715 | 0.793 |
| Coherence | 0.169 | 0.387 | 0.428 |
| Complexity | 0.293 | 0.416 | 0.427 |
| Verbosity | 0.342 | 0.536 | 0.548 |
- Cohen's κ was chosen over Krippendorff's α or Fleiss' κ because, with about 1,000 annotators, "multiple individual annotators were rarely allocated common sets of samples"; the quadratic weighting was chosen because the attributes are ordinal.

## Disagreement filter and final size (§2.1)
- "we retained only responses for which the differences in helpfulness attribute among annotators were 2 points or below on a Likert-5 scale (for both responses to a common prompt), resulting in the removal of about 10% of the samples." The threshold balances retained data against noise, "recognizing that differences among annotators can also stem from inherent subjectivity or individual preferences rather than misunderstandings of the annotation task."
- About 50% of all annotations were excluded across all filtering stages. Final: 21,362 samples = 10,681 prompts × 2 responses; 95% train, 5% validation.

## Dataset statistics (§2.2)
- Mean verbosity rose from 1.53 (HelpSteer) to 2.00 and mean response length from 497.3 to 1492.6 characters. Pearson's R with helpfulness: correctness 0.9430; response length 0.0845; verbosity 0.0600.

## Use (§3, §4.3)
- Nemotron-4 340B RM trained with HelpSteer2 scored 92.0 on RewardBench's primary dataset (Table 2); the Llama 3 70B RM scored 88.8. The authors state that multi-attribute RMs, by predicting verbosity explicitly, can disambiguate verbosity from overall quality (§3).
- DPO data: higher-helpfulness response as chosen, identical-helpfulness pairs discarded; 7,221 training pairs; 7 epochs, constant LR 2e-7, KL penalty 1e-3, global batch 128 (§4.3).
