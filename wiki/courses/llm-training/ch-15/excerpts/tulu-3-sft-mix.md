# Excerpt: Tülu 3 SFT Mix — skill-targeted annotation at 939K

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/papers/tulu-3-sft-mix.md`
**Year / authors:** 2024 / Lambert, Morrison, Pyatkin, Brahman et al. (Allen AI).

## Why this source anchors ch-15

Tülu 3 is the 2024 reference point for **multi-skill annotation at operational scale**. 939,344 prompts, 18 component sources, 57% public + 43% in-house synthetic, decontaminated against every eval set that matters. It is where the ch-15 operational story ends up when every lesson from InstructGPT, HH-RLHF, OASST, UltraFeedback, and PRM800K is applied simultaneously. For ch-15 §6 (operational reality), the Tülu-3 recipe is the longest worked example available in the open literature.


The structure is not "general chat at scale." Every line is a skill: math, code, IF, safety, multilingual, table, science, hardcoded-identity. The ch-15 §1 rubric-as-product observation manifests here as **one rubric per skill**, with each skill's submix built and ablated independently before being merged.

The blog post's operational description:

> Ai2 starts with public datasets that have clear provenance and licenses, then manually reviews each candidate source for diversity, hard-skill coverage, and decontamination.
> The team builds skill-specific data mixtures and models first, keeps the mixes that perform best on individual skills, and then combines them into a preview mix.
> The final mix is iteratively adjusted by adding and removing datasets, decontaminating against the evaluation suite, and downsampling oversized sources.

This is the InstructGPT labeler-guidelines workflow scaled to 18 rubrics running in parallel. Each submix has its own quality bar ("keeps the mixes that perform best on individual skills"); failed submixes are discarded without polluting the mother-ship dataset.

## The 57/43 public-synthetic split — what it says about the rubric

43% in-house synthetic is the load-bearing number for ch-15 §5 (when human overrides judge). The in-house synthetic is generated by GPT-4o or Claude 3.5 Sonnet, with the understanding that:

- **Persona-MATH, Persona-GSM, Persona-Algebra**: math problems have verifiers (exact match, sympy) → synthetic is safe.
- **Persona-Python**: code has unit tests → synthetic is safe.
- **Persona-IF**: format constraints are mechanically checkable → synthetic is safe.
- **WildJailbreak**: adversarial safety — *synthetic generation followed by human review* — because the rubric for "jailbreak attempt" is complex and cultural.
- **Hardcoded**: 240 prompts for model identity — hand-authored, no synthesis.

The 57% public half is correspondingly where synthetic would fail: real-user chat (WildChat, OpenAssistant), genuine tabular reasoning (TableGPT), science (SciRIFF), multilingual (Aya). These domains either have rubric problems the judge doesn't solve (chat fluency, cultural nuance) or come from human sources that cannot be cheaply resynthesized.

The ch-15 §5 decision table is visible in the source allocation: verifier → synthetic, ambiguous rubric → human, hybrid → synthetic-generated-then-human-reviewed.

## Decontamination as an annotation step

> Decontamination is part of the data recipe, not an afterthought;
> Ai2 explicitly removes overlap with more than 2% of the eval suite.

The companion blog ([[allenai-tulu-sft-recipe]]) specifies:

```
8-gram overlap ≥ 50% against every eval set → drop.
Embedding similarity > 0.9 to eval-set items → drop.
Documented "surviving overlap" rates per eval.
```

This is the ch-15 §6 "prompt-leak prevention" generalized: annotators (here, synthetic-generators-as-annotators) can unintentionally produce items that overlap with the eval suite, and a decontamination step is the only defense that composes across sources. The "surviving overlap" documentation is the audit trail — a downstream consumer reading the Tulu-3 dataset card can see exactly how much residual overlap each eval retains.

For a team building its own mix, this is a 5-filter pipeline (drop + document + spot-check + rebuild + re-measure) that runs every time a new source is added. Under-budgeting this step is one of the most common failure modes ch-15 §6 warns against.

## The ablation-driven assembly

The companion blog reports:

> Removing Persona-Math drops GSM8K by 15 pts.
> Removing code drops HumanEval by 12.
> Removing safety data barely moves capability evals but tanks WildJailbreak from 98% → 52%.
> 2 epochs > 1 epoch > 3 epochs at this mix size.

The structural lesson: **skill submixes are approximately additive on their target eval, orthogonal to the others**. Removing math hurts math evals and is neutral on IF/code/safety; removing safety is neutral on capability and destroys safety. This additivity is what makes the "build skill-specific first, then merge" recipe work. If submixes interacted strongly, the merge would not be tractable — you would have to re-ablate every combination.

For ch-15 §4 (sampling policy), the Tulu-3 evidence is that the *per-skill* rubric matters more than the *inter-skill* balance: a well-rubric'd math submix contributes +15pp whether you have 50K math prompts or 150K. The implication for budget allocation: spend rubric-iteration budget on each skill individually; do not spend it on tuning the global mix weights.

## The downstream chain — what the SFT mix feeds

> The SFT mix is designed to support later DPO and RLVR stages,
> so its job is broad skill coverage rather than single-task specialization.

Ch-15 is in Track 2 (data); the SFT/DPO/RLVR stages are in Tracks 4 and 5. But the annotation choices here bind those later stages. DPO needs preference pairs generated on top of the SFT model's distribution; RLVR needs prompts with verifiers. Tulu-3 plans for both at SFT-mix construction time: the in-house synthetic skills are exactly the ones with verifiers (math, code, IF), so the same prompts can later be sampled for RLVR. Skill submixes without verifiers (chat, safety) will route to DPO or to judge-based preferences later.

This is the ch-15 §6 audit-trail discipline paying off across the entire post-training pipeline: because provenance and rubric-version are preserved, the downstream stages can selectively reuse slices.
