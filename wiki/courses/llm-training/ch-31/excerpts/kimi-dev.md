# Excerpt: Kimi-Dev: Agentless Training as Skill Prior for SWE-Agents

- **Authors:** Zonghan Yang, Shengjie Wang, Kelin Fu, Wenyang He, Weimin Xiong, Yibo Liu, et al. (Moonshot AI; Tsinghua University; Peking University; others)
- **Year:** 2025 (arXiv v1 2025-09; v3 2025-12-08)
- **Source type:** paper (model released as moonshotai/Kimi-Dev-72B)
- **Used in:** ch-31 §6.2, §7, Negative samples and negative feedback, Recipe, Generalization lens

## What the paper does

Agentless methods split GitHub issue resolution into single-turn steps with verifiable rewards; SWE-Agent frameworks let the model act over many turns (§2.1). The paper trains a 72B model in the Agentless format and then tests whether that training works as a starting point ("skill prior") for multi-turn SWE-Agent fine-tuning (Abstract, §4).

## Agentless recipe (§3)

- **Roles.** BugFixer writes patches; TestWriter writes tests that fail before and pass after the fix. Both need file localization and code editing (§3.1).
- **Mid-training (§3.2).** Qwen2.5-72B-Base, about 150B tokens: ~50B Agentless-format data from diff patches, ~20B curated PR commit packs, ~20B synthetic reasoning and agentic data upsampled 4×; repositories in SWE-bench Verified excluded. Figure 2: more mid-training tokens (50B, 100B, ~150B) gave higher BugFixer pass@1 after a light SFT on 2,000 pairs (§3.5.2). Mid-training settings: global batch 256, maximum sequence length 32K, learning rate 2e-5 with cosine decay to 2e-6 and about 3B warm-up tokens (App. A).
- **Cold start (§3.2).** SFT on reasoning trajectories generated by DeepSeek-R1 (20250120) for SWE-Gym and SWE-bench-extra tasks.
- **RL (§3.3).** Only the code-edit stage; Kimi k1.5 policy-gradient method with the mean reward of multiple rollouts as baseline; outcome reward 0 or 1 from test execution, no format or process reward. Prompts with pass@16 = 0 are discarded initially, leaving 1,200 problems; once success on the current set passes a threshold, 500 previously excluded prompts are added every 100 RL steps (§3.3; App. C.1 lists SWE-Gym, SWE-bench-extra, and R2E-gym as pools).
- **Positive example reinforcement (§3.3, App. C.2).** In late training, positive samples from recent RL iterations are added to the current batch; Figure 8 shows better BugFixer RL than without it; the authors state positive examples help "especially when its exploration ability diminishes". No numeric table.
- **RL setup (§3.5.3).** 5 training steps per RL iteration; 10 rollouts for each of 1,024 problems; prompt set adjusted every 20 iterations; 64k maximum context. Performance and response length rise together (Fig. 3). TestWriter showed occasional false positives from limited reproduction coverage (§3.5.3).
- **Test-time self-play (§3.4, §3.5.4).** 40 patches and 40 tests per issue, first sample greedy and 39 at temperature 1; score S_i = Σ_j FP(i, j) / Σ_j F(j) + Σ_j PP(i, j) / Σ_j P(j) (Eq. 1). SWE-bench Verified rises from 48.0% at 1 × 1 to 60.4% at 40 × 40 (Fig. 4; Table 1).

## SWE-Agent adaptation (§4)

- **Data and settings (§4.1).** 5,016 SWE-smith trajectories collected with Claude 3.7 Sonnet in synthetic environments; SFT at 64K context; inference up to 128K tokens and 100 turns.
- **Result (§4.1, Table 2).** 48.6% pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified; SWE-agent-LM (32B) trained on the same data: 40.2%; Claude 3.5 Sonnet (241022) with SWE-Agent: 49.0%. pass@10 of the adapted model is 74.0%, above Agentless pass@30 of 73.8%.
- **Prior comparison (§4.2, Fig. 5).** Four starting points (Base, mid-trained MT, long-CoT SFT, RL) fine-tuned with nested subsets of 100–5,016 trajectories (token budgets 0, 2²¹, 2²³, 2²⁴, 1.1 × 2²⁵, 1.1 × 2²⁶, 1.1 × 2²⁷, 1.5 × 2²⁸). The RL prior is best in nearly all settings and reaches the Base prior's best pass@1 with 2²³ tokens instead of 1.5 × 2²⁸. The SFT prior is better than RL at zero-shot, which the authors attribute to the RL prior fitting the Agentless format; the SFT prior shows what the authors call "a significant degradation" at 200 trajectories (2²⁴), which the authors hypothesize is memorization of a single data mode.
- **Turn limits (Fig. 6 left).** After adaptation, the RL prior keeps improving beyond 70 turns; SFT, MT, and Base show diminishing returns around 70, 60, and 50 turns.
- **Skill counts (Fig. 6 right).** Cases resolved at the Stage-3 cutoff within three passes: 484 (Base) to 605 (RL); gain from Stage 3 to the end: +94 (Base) to +113 (RL).
- **End-to-end RL on priors (§4.2, Fig. 7).** After a single SFT step (2²¹ tokens), problems with pass@8 > 0: 260 of 6,202 for MT; 2,062 for SFT and RL priors. MT pass@1 fell below 2% within 10 RL steps; after 300 steps the RL prior slightly outperforms the SFT prior at pass@1, 3, and 5. Outcome reward only, no KL or entropy regularization, batch 256.
- **Generalization (App. G.4, Figs. 11–12).** SWE-bench-Live (300 tasks, Oct 2024–Mar 2025) and SWE-bench Multilingual (300 tasks, 42 repositories, 9 languages): SFT and RL priors generalize better than Base with few adaptation trajectories; with more trajectories, Base and MT approach them.
