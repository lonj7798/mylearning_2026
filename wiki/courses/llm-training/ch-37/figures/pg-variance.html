Baseline, sign, and bias explorer

Companion to ch-37 §2, §4 and "Negative samples and negative feedback". All numbers are computed exactly in this page (no sampling, no training). Panel A shows which samples receive a negative advantage under each baseline. Panel B enumerates every group of G samples from a four-response policy and reports the expected gradient and its variance. Panel C applies one policy-gradient step to the logits of a softmax and shows where probability mass moves.

B. Exact expected gradient and variance for one prompt

baselinescale of E[ĝ] relative to ∇Junbiased?total variance of ĝvariance ÷ scale²

Policy: softmax over four fixed responses with logits z; reward rm ∈ {0, 1}. Estimator ĝ = (1/G) Σi Ai (ey_i − p), the gradient of (1/G) Σ Ai log p(yi) with respect to z. True gradient ∂J/∂zj = pj(rj − J). For binary reward every baseline in the table gives an expected gradient parallel to ∇J; the "scale" column is the factor. A scale different from 1 that depends on J is a per-prompt reweighting, which is the bias argued in [REINFORCE++ App. A.1] and [Dr. GRPO §3.1]. The group-mean scale (G − 1)/G follows from ri − mean = ((G − 1)/G)(ri − mean−i). "variance ÷ scale²" compares estimators at a matched expected step.

C. One update on the logits of a softmax

Update: zk ← zk + η A (1[k = y] − pk). First-order change: Δpj ≈ η A pj (1[j = y] − pj − py + Σk pk²). For A < 0, an unsampled response j gains probability only if pj > Σk pk² − py; lower-probability alternatives lose mass as well. Uses the probabilities set in panel B.
