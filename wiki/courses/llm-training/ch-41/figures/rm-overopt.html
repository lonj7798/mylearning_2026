Over-optimization functional forms and Bradley–Terry gradients

Companion to ch-41 read.md §2 (panel A) and §1 plus "Negative samples and negative feedback" (panel B).

A. Gold score versus √KL for a proxy reward model

Functional forms, the best-of-n KL formula KL = log n − (n−1)/n, and the definition d = √KL are from Gao, Schulman, Hilton (2022), arXiv:2210.10760 §1–§2 (source card: reward-model-overoptimization). The peak formulas d* = α/(2β) and d* = exp(α/β − 1) are derived by setting dR/dd = 0. The paper plots α and β but does not print them; all coefficient values here are user-set and illustrative. The paper's Fig. 3 shows α_bon increasing and β_bon, β_RL decreasing as proxy RM size grows, with α_RL held constant. The RL form likely does not hold near d = 0 (paper footnote 1), and RL and best-of-n use KL at different rates, so the two curves are not comparable at equal d (§3.5).

B. Bradley–Terry loss and gradient for one preference pair

Loss L = −log σ(Δ) (DPO arXiv:2305.18290 §3 Eq. 2; source card: bradley-terry-rm). The gradient on the rejected score is ∂L/∂r(y_l) = σ(−Δ) = 1 − σ(Δ), and the gradient on the chosen score has the same magnitude with opposite sign. Pairs the reward model already separates (large Δ) contribute little gradient; near-miss pairs contribute most of it, which also makes a mislabelled near-miss pair the most costly. Adding the same constant to both scores leaves Δ, the loss, and the gradient unchanged.
