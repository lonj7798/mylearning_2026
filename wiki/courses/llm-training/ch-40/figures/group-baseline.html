Group baselines: advantages, dead groups, and loss normalizers

Companion to ch-40. Panel 1 recomputes the advantage every rollout in a group receives under RLOO, GRPO (two standard-deviation conventions) and Dr. GRPO. Panel 2 shows how often a group carries no signal. Panel 3 compares loss normalizers on responses of different lengths.

Panel 1 — Advantages inside one group

Formulas: RLOO r_i − mean of the other G−1 (Ahmadian et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.14740 §2.3); GRPO (r_i − mean)/std (DeepSeekMath, arXiv:2402.03300 §4.1.2), with the population std and with torch.std (divisor G−1), which is what verl and TRL compute; Dr. GRPO r_i − mean (Dr. GRPO, arXiv:2503.20783 §3.2). The RLOO column is always G/(G−1) times the Dr. GRPO column (Dr. GRPO, arXiv:2503.20783 App. A).

Panel 2 — How often a group carries no signal

With independent samples, the chance that a prompt's group is all-correct or all-wrong is p^G + (1−p)^G; such a group has advantage exactly 0 and contributes no policy gradient (verl core_algos.py@753aed3 L319-329). DAPO resamples until the batch contains only groups with a non-zero advantage (DAPO, arXiv:2503.14476 §3.2).

Panel 3 — Loss normalizers and response length

GRPO divides each response's token losses by its own length |o_i|, so the total contribution of a response does not depend on its length; Dr. GRPO divides by a constant equal to the generation budget, and DAPO divides by the total token count of the group, so a longer response of the same advantage contributes proportionally more (Dr. GRPO, arXiv:2503.20783 §3.1; DAPO, arXiv:2503.14476 §3.3). Group size is fixed at 8 here.
