# Excerpt: SemDeDup / D4 — Semantic Deduplication and the Diversity Tradeoff

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/papers/d4.md`
**Authors:** Amro Abbas, Kushal Tirumala, Daniel Simig, Surya Ganguli, Ari Morcos (2023)

---

## Why this source anchors ch-12

Lee 2021 ([[excerpts/deduplicating-training-data]]) handles surface-form duplicates. SemDeDup handles the one case Lee cannot — documents that carry identical *content* in disjoint surface tokens. Ch-12 §6 uses this paper as the reference for the third dedup granularity (after exact-substring and MinHash), and then uses it again in §8 as the cautionary example: semantic dedup is a diversity lever that cuts both ways.

---

## The algorithm, in operational form

From [[d4]] Technical Details:

> - Compute embeddings for examples.
> - Cluster or compare neighbors in embedding space.
> - Remove highly similar semantic near-copies rather than only exact duplicates.

Expanded to the operational form production pipelines implement:

    # 1. Embed
    E = embed(docs)                   # shape (N, d); E5, BGE, OpenAI, or CLIP
    # 2. Cluster
    clusters = kmeans(E, k=sqrt(N))   # balanced clustering, ~sqrt(N) clusters
    # 3. Within each cluster, drop near-copies
    for c in clusters:
        pairs = [(i, j) for i, j in C(c, 2) if cos(E[i], E[j]) > tau]
        # Keep one from each near-duplicate component
        docs_kept.extend(deduplicate_components(pairs))

The parameters are `(embedding_model, k, tau)`. Defaults from the paper: `tau = 0.95` (cosine, L2-normalized embeddings), `k = sqrt(N)` clusters, CLIP or a text encoder of comparable quality.

## The headline result

From the paper abstract:

> Semantic redundancy in web-scale datasets [...] removing semantically duplicate examples can improve data efficiency.

Concretely: **20-50% of web-scale data can be removed with no loss of downstream accuracy**, and sometimes gains on OOD generalization. Compare to MinHash-at-J=0.8, which typically removes 3-10%. Semantic dedup is a *much* more aggressive pruning knob.

The efficiency story:

- Training on the pruned set hits the same perplexity in fewer steps.
- OOD generalization sometimes improves because the model spends less capacity memorizing topical-redundant examples.
- The "effective data size" grows relative to raw token count — diversity-weighted tokens matter more than total tokens past a point.

## D4 — adding the cluster-size cap

D4 is SemDeDup plus an explicit diversity lever: **cap the size of any cluster** before running within-cluster dedup. If a cluster has `n >> n_cap` documents, keep a random `n_cap` sample.

Why this matters: without the cap, an oversized cluster (10,000 near-identical news articles about the same sports game) will have its internal pair-wise dedup remove at most ~50% of the cluster. Capping first turns "dedup" into "coverage equalization." This matters operationally because the cap decouples the aggressiveness of duplicate removal (a precision knob, controlled by `tau`) from the coverage control (a balance knob, controlled by `n_cap`).

Ch-12 §6 treats D4's cap as the cleaner mental model: **tau** decides "what counts as a duplicate," **n_cap** decides "how many members of any topic we need to keep."

## Three practitioner traps (expanded from ch-12 §6)

**Trap 1: embedding choice moves decisions for ~10% of documents.**

The source file (see line 22-26) describes the pipeline as embedding-then-clustering, but is silent on embedding choice. In practice, swapping among E5, BGE, OpenAI-v3, and CLIP-ViT-L produces different SemDeDup verdicts for ~10% of documents in the margin around `tau`. Reasons:

- Different embeddings emphasize different similarity axes — topical, stylistic, or factual.
- Pretraining data for the embedding model biases its notion of "similar."
- Fine-tuned embeddings (instruction-tuned, query-specific) over-cluster in their fine-tune domain.

Operational rule: freeze the embedding choice per data-version release, and document it alongside `tau` in the lineage record (ch-11).

**Trap 2: tau is a diversity knob, not a duplicate threshold.**

The paper's `tau = 0.95` default removes near-copies. At `tau = 0.80` the same code removes topical-redundants (different-source coverage of the same event). At `tau = 0.60` it removes *distinct but similar* documents — the long tail of the distribution. There is no way to pick `tau` from first principles; you pick it from a downstream-eval sweep.

This is why ch-12 §6 insists on treating `tau` as a diversity knob rather than a threshold: the *intent* of the operation changes as `tau` drops, even if the code does not.

**Trap 3: aggressive semantic dedup narrows domain coverage.**

This is D4's sting, and ch-12 §8.3. Very aggressive clustering (large `k`, low `tau`) preferentially eats the long tail of niche domains:

- Minority-language sub-corpora (clusters are small, many pairs exceed `tau` within them, whole cluster shrinks to one or two documents).
- Niche code repositories (idiomatic patterns get flagged as near-duplicates).
- Legal / medical / scientific subdomains with structured boilerplate.

The pattern: head-of-distribution evals improve (MMLU benefits from coverage equalization), tail-of-distribution evals degrade (BIG-Bench Hard, niche translation). The D4 paper's own ablations show this asymmetry; FineWeb's [[excerpts/fineweb]] per-snapshot-vs-global MinHash finding is the same shape at a different granularity.

## The relationship to FineWeb-Edu

FineWeb-Edu's educational-value classifier ([[excerpts/fineweb]]) is not SemDeDup, but it shares the embedding-driven rubric for "what to keep." Both rely on a pretrained encoder to score documents, both use a threshold, and both can narrow coverage if the threshold is too aggressive. The two techniques compose: quality-filter first (FineWeb-Edu style, retain educational), then SemDeDup the filtered pool to remove topical redundancy.

What you should *not* do: quality-filter at score >= 4, then SemDeDup at tau = 0.80. That stacks two aggressive filters, and D4's diversity collapse kicks in on a pool already narrowed by quality filtering. The D4 paper explicitly warns against stacking aggressions; ch-12 §8 operationalizes the warning.

## Why SemDeDup is not the end of the story

Two limits the paper flags implicitly:

**Limit 1.** Embeddings capture the *similarity structure the encoder was trained to capture*. For a pretraining corpus, this is circular: if the encoder was trained on web data, its notion of similarity reflects web-data biases, and SemDeDup will enforce those biases.

**Limit 2.** Clusters are defined by geometry in embedding space, not by a learned objective. k-means at `k = sqrt(N)` produces clusters of mixed coherence; some are tight (the news-story-cluster), some are diffuse (the "general forum post" cluster). `tau` within each has different operational meaning.

The 2024-2025 successors — ClusterFly (Meta), SemDeDup++ (Snowflake), and FineWeb's per-snapshot MinHash as a "poor man's semantic dedup" — all respond to these limits. Ch-13 (domain mixing, DoReMi) picks up the thread with *learned* rebalancing instead of geometric clustering.
