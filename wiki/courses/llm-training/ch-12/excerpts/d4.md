# Excerpt: D4: Improving LLM Pretraining via Document De-Duplication and Diversification

Kushal Tirumala, Daniel Simig, Armen Aghajanyan, Ari S. Morcos (Meta AI Research). Library card: [[d4]].

## Source data (§3.1, App. A.1.2, A.1.3)
- CC-dedup: 5 CommonCrawl dumps (2017–2020) processed with the CCNet pipeline used for LLaMA, then document-level MinHash; about 600 million documents.
- MinHash parameters as stated: "20 hashes per signature, 20 buckets, and 1 row per bucket", the Spark MinHashLSH defaults, not swept (App. A.1.2). The same paragraph describes Lee et al. and Penedo et al. as using "20 buckets, 450 hashes per bucket, and 9000 signatures per hash"; Lee et al.'s own App. A states 450 buckets of 20 hashes (see [[deduplicating-training-data]]).
- App. A.1.2 also reports that SemDeDup and the aggressive MinHash of Lee et al. performed comparably at a fixed 3.9% selection on C4 (citing Abbas et al.).

## Method (§3.4)
- Embedding: last-layer embedding of the last token from OPT-125M, L2-normalized; faiss spherical K-Means with 11,000 clusters and 20 iterations on about 100M documents.
- SemDeDup: remove points within epsilon-balls of each other inside a cluster (epsilon not stated in D4).
- SSL Prototypes (Sorscher et al. 2022): remove points in increasing order of distance to the nearest centroid, so the most prototypical points go first.
- D4: (1) SemDeDup with ratio R_dedup → D′; (2) re-cluster D′; (3) SSL Prototypes with ratio R_proto. Overall R = R_dedup × R_proto; R_dedup = 0.75 in main experiments.
- Motivation: after MinHash, dense duplicate-driven clusters of templated text remain in embedding space (§3.4, App. A.9).

## Fixed-compute results (§4.1, Fig. 1–2)
- 6.7B, 100B tokens, R = 0.25: 22.18% faster on non-web-snapshot perplexity, 18.08% faster on Instruct + Answers perplexity, 2.04% higher mean 0-shot accuracy over 16 NLP tasks at the end of training.
- 1.3B, 40B tokens, 3 seeds: SemDeDup, SSL Prototypes and D4 all improve Instruct + Answers perplexity; D4 most.
- Web-snapshot validation sets (C4, CC-dedup, CommonCrawl) always get worse perplexity under selection; they lie closest to the training data in embedding space (§4.4.1, Fig. 5). Lower web-snapshot perplexity correlates slightly with worse Instruct + Answers perplexity (Pearson −0.368) and lower accuracy (−0.188) (Fig. 6).
- Cost (§4.3): about 4,300 GPU hours saved at 6.7B; embedding 400B tokens costs about 888 A100 GPU hours; net about 3,412 GPU hours.

## Repeated tokens (§4.2, Table 1, App. A.6)
1.3B OPT models, 40B training tokens, source 40B tokens, 3 seeds:

| Selection | Tokens selected | Epochs | Non-web-snapshot PPL | Instruct + Answers PPL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random | 40B | 1 | 16.27 | 14.19 |
| Random | 20B | 2 | 16.39 | 14.37 |
| D4 | 20B | 2 | 16.10 | 13.85 |

- D4 repetition also beat random new tokens at 125M and 6.7B and at R = 0.5 and 0.25 (App. A.6, Fig. A15).
- For a fixed selection method, "it is usually preferable to select new tokens" (App. A.6, Fig. A13).

## Limits (§5)
One data distribution (web), not source mixtures; largest model 6.7B on 100B tokens.
