MinHash LSH candidate probability, and why repetition can be memorized

Panel A computes the probability that two documents with Jaccard similarity s share at least one LSH bucket, for any number of hashes per bucket and any number of buckets. Presets load published settings, including the swapped reading of the Lee et al. setting discussed in ch-12 §4. Panel B recomputes the loss comparison that Hernandez et al. use to explain why a model can trade general capacity for memorizing a repeated subset (ch-12 §8.1).

A. LSH candidate probability

P(s) = 1 − (1 − sh)B    h = hashes per bucket, B = number of buckets, k = h·B
QuantityValue

Sources: Lee et al. NearDup, "r buckets, with b hashes per bucket", b = 20, r = 450, k = 9,000, candidates verified at Jaccard > 0.8 and edit similarity > 0.8; alternative b = 20, r = 40, k = 800 at 0.9 (Lee et al., arXiv:2107.06499v2 §4.2, App. A). FineWeb 14 buckets of 8 hashes, bucket match treated as duplicate, reported P = 56%, 77%, 92%, 98.8% at s = 0.7, 0.75, 0.8, 0.85 (arXiv:2406.17557v2 §3.4, App. E.1). Textbook Example 3.12, 20 bands of 5 rows, 0.99965 at s = 0.8 (Leskovec, Rajaraman, Ullman, Ch. 3 §3.4.2, as recorded in the minhash-lsh library card). Half point s½ is computed as (1 − 2−1/B)1/h; the approximation (ln 2 / B)1/h is derived for large B.

B. Memorize-or-generalize loss arithmetic

memorize: (1 − f)·Lsmall + f·Lmem  vs  generalize: Lfull
QuantityValue

Defaults from Hernandez et al., arXiv:2205.10487v1 §5.1: an 800M model at about 2.0 nats/token, a 400M model at about 2.2, fully memorized data at 0, 10% repeated tokens, giving 0.9 × 2.2 + 0.1 × 0 = 1.98 < 2.0. Lsmall is the loss on unique data after capacity is used for memorization (in the paper's example, the loss of a model with half the parameters). The break-even share f* = (Lsmall − Lfull) / (Lsmall − Lmem) is derived (9.1% at the defaults). Status: Interpretation by the authors. The arithmetic does not say whether the model has the capacity or enough repeats to memorize; the paper locates the worst degradation at intermediate repeat counts (Figures 2–4).
