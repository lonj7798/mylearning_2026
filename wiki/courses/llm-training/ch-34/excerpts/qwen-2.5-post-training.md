# Excerpt: Qwen 2.5 — 1M SFT + 150K DPO + GRPO as three named stages

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/model-reports/qwen-2.5.md`
**Report:** Qwen Team (Alibaba), Dec 2024, "Qwen2.5 Technical Report".

---

## Why this source anchors ch-34 §2

Qwen 2.5 is the clearest public origin of the **two-stage SFT context curriculum** and the **Online Merging Optimizer**. Later Qwen models (Qwen 3, Qwen 3.5) inherit this skeleton; the 2026 Qwen 3.5 refresh notably does **not** re-document post-training, so the Qwen 2.5 report is still the primary algorithmic reference for the whole family.

---

## The three post-training stages as written

From the source (lines 30-50):

> ### SFT
> - **Total examples:** 1,000,000 across SFT + DPO + GRPO stages.
> - **Two-stage context curriculum:**
>   - **Phase 1:** short instructions, max 32,768 tokens. Strong performance on typical tasks.
>   - **Phase 2:** mix of short (<=32K) and long (up to 262,144) instructions. Maintains short-task quality while teaching long-context instruction following.
> - Standard completion-masked loss.
>
> ### DPO
> - **Preference data:** ~150,000 pairs. The SFT model resamples responses for new queries; quality-check-pass responses = chosen, quality-check-fail = rejected.
> - **Optimizer:** Online Merging Optimizer (keeps a running merged checkpoint to stabilize DPO).
> - **Learning rate:** 7e-7.
> - **Epochs:** 1.
> - **Beta:** not explicitly reported in the public tech report text (standard DPO beta ~0.1 assumed).
>
> ### GRPO
> - **Prompt ordering (novel):** the sequence in which queries are processed during training is determined by the variance of their response scores, as evaluated by the reward model. Queries with higher variance — where the RM can discriminate strongly between good/bad samples — are prioritized.

Three things stand out.

1. **"1M examples across SFT + DPO + GRPO."** The 1M count is the *total post-training budget*, not SFT alone. Within that total, 150K is allocated to DPO preference pairs, and an unspecified remainder carries the GRPO prompts. That means the SFT stage itself is in the ~700K–850K range — still very large, and Qwen splits it across Phase 1 and Phase 2.
2. **Two-stage context curriculum inside SFT.** Phase 1 teaches instruction-following at ≤32K so short-task quality locks in; Phase 2 mixes ≤32K with up to 262K-token long-instruction examples. This is the *SFT-internal* context curriculum — distinct from the pretraining-stage context extension (4K native → 128K with YARN) described separately. The mix in Phase 2 is what prevents long-context SFT from degrading short-task behaviour; without the mix, short-task quality regresses.
3. **Preference data is on-policy, filtered by quality.** The DPO pairs are not collected from human raters; they are generated by resampling the SFT model and partitioning by a **quality filter**. Pass-the-filter responses become chosen, fail-the-filter become rejected. This is lower-cost than RLHF human labeling but creates a dependency on the quality-filter model — whose design Qwen does not document.

## Online Merging Optimizer — what it actually stabilizes

From the source (line 42):

> - **Optimizer:** Online Merging Optimizer (keeps a running merged checkpoint to stabilize DPO).

The mechanism: during DPO training, maintain a *merged* checkpoint that is a time-averaged (or periodically re-merged) version of the trainable checkpoint. The DPO gradient is applied to the trainable checkpoint, but inference / reference is against the merged version. This is structurally similar to EMA (exponential moving average) weight averaging used in diffusion / self-supervised training, adapted to the DPO regime.

**Why DPO needs this.** DPO at LR 7e-7 for 1 epoch on 150K pairs is on the tight edge of the single-pass preference regime. With a small preference set and a small LR, the gradient signal is noisy, and the policy can drift in directions that help the loss but hurt downstream evals (classical "DPO degrades instruction-following" failure). Online merging acts as a low-pass filter on the trajectory — the merged checkpoint moves only in the directions the DPO gradient has consistently pushed, not the directions of single noisy pairs.

The raw-data source does not disclose the merge coefficient α or the merge frequency, so this is a named but not quantitatively attested knob.

## Variance-ordered GRPO — the discriminative-prompt-first principle

From the source (line 48):

> - **Prompt ordering (novel):** the sequence in which queries are processed during training is determined by the variance of their response scores, as evaluated by the reward model. Queries with higher variance — where the RM can discriminate strongly between good/bad samples — are prioritized.

The argument: GRPO's advantage signal is `r_i - mean(r)` within a group of G rollouts per prompt. If the reward model scores all G rollouts similarly — because the prompt is trivially easy (all good) or trivially hard (all bad) — the advantage is ≈0 and the update contributes no learning signal. The variance of `{r_i}` across the group is the proxy for "how discriminative is this prompt right now?"

Ordering prompts by variance front-loads the learning: the first GRPO passes spend compute on prompts that actually move the policy, and low-variance prompts either become discriminative later (as the policy changes) or are de-prioritized. This is a GRPO-specific curriculum choice that plays the same role as "boundary of base-model capability" filtering in Phi-4-reasoning.

## Benchmark deltas

From the source (lines 57-61):

> - MMLU: 86.1%
> - HumanEval: 85.4%
> - MATH: 83.1%
> - IFEval: 86.1%

These are the Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct final numbers after the full SFT + DPO + GRPO stack. The tech report does not break out per-stage deltas (SFT-only → +DPO → +GRPO) publicly, which is a gap — without those deltas you cannot tell whether DPO or GRPO is the source of the IFEval 86.1.

## What the report does NOT disclose

- GRPO group size G, clip ε, KL coefficient, LR, number of rollouts per prompt, total GRPO steps (Qwen2.5-Math report at arXiv 2409.12122 discloses math-specific RL details; the general-purpose GRPO numbers are not in the public extract).
- DPO β (inferred ~0.1 from cousin Tülu defaults).
- The quality-filter model identity and threshold for the DPO preference construction.
- The Online Merging Optimizer merge coefficient and merge frequency.
