Effective tokens under repetition

Accompanies ch-14 §2. The page evaluates Eq. 5 of Muennighoff et al., Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models (arXiv:2305.16264v5), with the fitted constant R*D = 15.387756 (App. A). Set the unique tokens U and the number of passes over them; the page reports the effective tokens D′, the average value of a processed token, and the value of the next increment of repetition.

D′ = U + U · R*D · (1 − e−RD/R*D). Average value = D′ / D. Marginal value = dD′/dRD ÷ U = e−RD/R*D (derived from Eq. 5). Ceiling = U(1 + R*D) = 16.39 U.

Reported observations: 8.7B model, 178B tokens, 4 passes over 44B unique tokens: validation loss 0.5% above one pass over 178B unique tokens (§6). "Meaningful gains from repeating data can be made up to around 16 epochs (R*D)" (§6). The fit "significantly underestimates the final test loss of failing models ... such as models trained for 44 epochs" (§6).

Where Eq. 5 was tested

Fitted on 182 runs of GPT-2-architecture models from 7M to 9B parameters and 1 to 500 epochs on C4 (App. A), with dropout 0.1 and weight decay 0.1 (App. S). Only uniform repetition of the whole dataset was studied (App. Q). Repeating a small subset is different: in Hernandez et al. (arXiv:2205.10487), repeating 0.1% of the data 100 times degraded an 800M model to the level of a 400M model while 90% of tokens stayed unique (Abstract); see ch-14 §3.
