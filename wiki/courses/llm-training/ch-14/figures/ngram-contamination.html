What the numbers mean

Tokens here are words after the selected normalization. Singh et al. count model tokens, so an "8-gram" in their paper is 8 tokens (§3.1) and values differ from this page. LONGEST-MATCH here uses a skip budget of 0 and counts a span only if it is at least n long, because the method extends matches from n-gram seeds.

Values the chapter quotes (normalization on): the GSM8K item has 53 tokens; the verbatim preset gives TOKEN-MATCH 53/53 = 1.0 at n = 8 and n = 13; the paraphrase preset gives 0/53 at n = 8 and n = 13, a longest shared span of 4 tokens, and 12/53 = 0.226 at n = 4. Yang et al. report 10-gram F1 = 0 on rephrased MMLU and HumanEval pairs (Tables 5-6); their rephrased items were regenerated until an n-gram check no longer matched them (Algorithm 1), so this result partly follows from how the items were built. Singh et al. find that n larger than 8 and a minimum corpus count above 1 increase false negatives (§6.1, §6.3), and that thresholds should be chosen per model-benchmark pair (§4.2).

The shared-option preset shows a false-positive pattern: two different questions share the option list "A. True, True B. False, False C. True, False D. False, True". At n = 8 the options cover 12 of 59 tokens (TOKEN-MATCH 0.203): the item is not flagged at τ = 0.5 but is flagged at τ = 0.20 or lower, and the per-benchmark thresholds Singh et al. selected range from 0.04 to 0.40 (Table 3). Olmo 3 matches full answer text instead of option labels to reduce such matches (arXiv:2512.13961v2 §3.5.3).
