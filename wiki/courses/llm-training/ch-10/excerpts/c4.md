# Excerpt: C4 — the heuristic baseline everyone copies and nobody ablates

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/papers/c4.md`
**Paper:** Raffel, Shazeer, Roberts, Lee, Narang, Matena et al. 2019, "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer" (T5).

---

## Why this source anchors ch-10

C4 is the recipe the open pretraining ecosystem has been running on, in diluted form, for six years. Ch-10 §2 calls it *"a reference point, not a recipe"* — meaning: its specific heuristics (terminal punctuation, bad-word blocklist, JavaScript-line strip) are still in active use across Gopher, Dolma, and FineWeb's pre-classifier stages, but their cumulative effect has never been ablated.

From the source (lines 7–8):

> **Core Insight:** C4 made heuristic web filtering a mainstream pretraining recipe: aggressive boilerplate removal, bad-word lists, language filtering, and document cleanup can turn a crawl into a usable text corpus fast.
>
> **Guideline:** If you need a simple baseline crawl pipeline, C4 is the canonical heuristic stack, but document its biases and exclusions because the filters are blunt.

The guideline is the tension: fast and reproducible on one hand, blunt and biased on the other. Every pipeline since has been trying to resolve that tension, either by stacking more heuristics on top (Gopher) or by replacing them with learned signals (FineWeb-Edu).

---

## The heuristic stack — what each rule actually does

From the source (lines 23–25):

> - Source is Common Crawl.
> - Main filters: English-only text, line/document cleanup, de-dup-like cleanup, code and boilerplate suppression, and bad-word / low-quality heuristics.
> - The recipe is fast and practical but much less semantically targeted than later classifier-based pipelines.

Unpacked to the individual rules (canonical sequence applied by the T5 preprocessing code):

1. Keep only lines ending in a terminal punctuation mark `.` `!` `?` `"`.
2. Drop documents with < 5 sentences.
3. Drop lines with < 5 words.
4. Drop documents whose *any* word matches the "List of Dirty, Naughty, Obscene and Otherwise Bad Words" (~400 entries).
5. Drop lines containing the word "javascript" (case-insensitive).
6. Drop pages containing a pair of curly braces `{ }` (template placeholders).
7. Drop pages containing the literal string "lorem ipsum".
8. langdetect → keep P(English) ≥ 0.99.
9. Three-sentence line-span dedup across the corpus.

Rule 1 is the single most consequential heuristic in the open-pretraining literature because it is copied *implicitly* — any pipeline whose Gopher-style "fraction of lines ending in terminal punctuation > 0.6" threshold is tuned from C4's rule is downstream of the same blind spot. Ch-10 §2 calls this out: documents whose lines are structurally valid but not prose-terminated (bullet-heavy technical docs, code-heavy explainers, poetry, song lyrics) are persistently under-kept.

## What C4 is good at — and why it stuck

- **Reproducibility.** Pin the Common Crawl snapshot (April 2019 WET), run the rules, get the same corpus. Every later pipeline that runs across multiple snapshots loses this property.
- **Speed.** No classifier, no perplexity model, no heavyweight extraction. The rules are regexes and counters. C4 runs on a small cluster overnight.
- **Legibility.** Every rule is one sentence. A reader can understand, audit, and reimplement the pipeline in an afternoon. This is why C4's heuristics are copied — they are easier to copy than they are to improve.

---

## What C4 is bad at — the Dodge et al. 2021 audit

From the source's Connections (line 30):

> Later analysis found important representation biases and unexpected removals from the blocklist-heavy design.

Dodge et al. (cited by the source as the later audit work) showed three specific costs of C4's heuristic choices:

1. **The bad-word blocklist disproportionately removes documents about LGBTQ identities, medical content, and some dialects of English.** The blocklist does not distinguish slur-as-slur from word-in-reclamation or word-in-clinical-context.
2. **The P(English) ≥ 0.99 threshold drops African-American English and code-switched prose** at higher rates than standard American English. langdetect's P-scores are not calibrated across dialects.
3. **Certain domains are silently absent.** C4's URL-level exclusion and bad-word cascade together remove a long tail of small community sites the paper did not enumerate.

These are not bugs in the blocklist or the classifier; they are the **unavoidable cost of document-level heuristic filtering without feedback**. A heuristic does not know it has a false-positive rate unless someone checks.

## Why C4 has no ablation table

Unlike [[excerpts/dolma]], C4 does not report a per-rule ablation against downstream evals. The T5 paper's focus is on the *model* (encoder-decoder, unified text-to-text formulation, scaling experiments), and the corpus is presented as one of many design choices, not a scientific claim in itself. No one has subsequently re-run T5's full training at equivalent cost with individual C4 rules toggled — it would require pretraining 8–10 T5 variants, a multi-million-dollar ablation budget.

This is the gap [[excerpts/dolma]] fills five years later, at smaller model scale (1B) but with the ablation rigor T5 lacked. The C4 → Dolma → FineWeb arc is partly the field's slow rediscovery that *pipelines must be ablated*.

---

## Why C4's influence persists

Even though the classifier era of [[excerpts/fineweb]] has demonstrably overtaken C4 on downstream benchmarks, C4's rules persist because:

- **The Gopher quality heuristics are C4-derived.** Every 2024 pipeline that uses "symbol-to-word ratio < 0.1" or "terminal-punct fraction > 0.6" is a direct descendant of C4's line-level rules.
- **The "drop the whole document" vs "redact in place" PII debate** started with C4's document-drop design. FineWeb's in-place-redact choice is an explicit rejection of C4's all-or-nothing stance.
- **The blocklist-of-words pattern** (now with classifier-generated lists instead of hand-curated ones) remains a default starting point for content filtering.

A new open pipeline that claims to be heuristic-only is, in practice, a rebundling of Gopher + C4 rules. Understanding C4 is understanding 70% of what is in the heuristic stage of any modern pipeline.

---

## What to take from C4 for ch-10

1. **Fast + reproducible is a real virtue.** The reason C4 survives as a baseline is not that its rules are good — it is that they are fixed and well-known. Any pipeline that wants to replace C4 must ship reproducibility along with its quality gains.
2. **Every heuristic is a bias commitment.** C4's bad-word list and P(English) threshold encode specific choices about who and what gets into the corpus. Classifier-based pipelines push the bias to the labeller but do not eliminate it.
3. **No ablation = no science.** C4's lack of a per-rule ablation is the specific hole Dolma fills. If your pipeline does not ablate, it is in C4's category: usable, reproducible, but not a scientific claim.
4. **The terminal-punctuation rule is the single most load-bearing heuristic in open pretraining.** Audit it first.
