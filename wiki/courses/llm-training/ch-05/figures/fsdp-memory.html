Sharding factor F: peak parameter memory and cross-host traffic

Both quantities on this page come from the two formulas printed in PyTorch FSDP (arXiv:2304.11277, §3.2.1 and §3.2.2). Gradients, optimizer state, and activations are not modelled here; the training-memory course covers the full ledger.

How to read the second term. The second term of the peak-memory formula is maxi ψi, the largest single FSDP unit, which under block-wise wrapping is one transformer block. It is not a copy of the whole model: FSDP materializes one unsharded FlatParameter at a time (§3.2.1). On the DDP side the paper reports only that DDP ran out of memory above a 2.28B T5 model on A100 80GB (§5.2); it states no general parameter-count limit.

The paper states that full sharding has 1.5× the communication overhead and volume of DDP under a bandwidth-optimal ring algorithm (§3.2.1), which is the ratio 3M(W−1)/W ÷ 2M(W−1)/W that these rows reproduce. It also notes that hybrid sharding's AllReduce runs at a smaller world size and empirically performs better than a global collective because of straggler effects and network interference (§3.2.2).

Collectives per iteration are O(N), where N is the number of FSDP units, and do not depend on F (§3.2.1). The paper's stated trade-off is the opposite one: finer-grained units lower the second memory term but add collectives, and total communication time rises rapidly once an AllGather falls below about 33M FP32 elements (§3.2.1, Figure 2b).
