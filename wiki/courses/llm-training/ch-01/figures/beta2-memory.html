Adam β₂ and update size after a gradient change

Deterministic simulation of one coordinate of Adam. The gradient is 1 before the event, so the steady-state update is exactly α. Choose a one-step spike or a gradual rise and compare how the second-moment estimate and the update size respond for different β₂.

β₂ values (β₁ = 0.9)
Top: √v̂ (the denominator of the update) and the gradient magnitude |g| (grey). Bottom: update size |m̂ / (√v̂ + ε)| in units of α; the green line marks the steady-state value 1.

Reading the one-step spike (default 10×). With β₂ = 0.999 the denominator barely moves (√v̂ = 1.048 at the spike), while the momentum m̂ jumps to 1.9, so the spike step is 1.81α and the updates stay above α through step +28. After that, v̂ remains slightly elevated for hundreds of steps, so updates are slightly smaller than α (0.96α at +50 and +100). With β₂ = 0.95 the denominator rises to 2.44 at once, the spike step is 0.78α, updates stay below α, and they are back within 1% of α at step +108.

Reading the gradual rise. When the gradient grows over several steps, a long second-moment memory lags behind the growth. With β₂ = 0.999 the peak update is 5.30α; with β₂ = 0.95 it is 1.15α. This is the case in which a long β₂ memory produces large steps.

Formulas. m_t = β₁m_{t−1} + (1 − β₁)g_t; v_t = β₂v_{t−1} + (1 − β₂)g_t²; update = α·m̂_t/(√v̂_t + ε) (Kingma & Ba, arXiv:1412.6980, Algorithm 1). The simulation starts from the steady state m = 1, v = 1, where bias correction is close to 1, and uses ε = 0 relative to a gradient of 1. Averaging timescale 1/(1 − β): 10 steps (β = 0.9), 20 (0.95), 50 (0.98), 1,000 (0.999); half-life ln 0.5 / ln β: 6.6, 13.5, 34.3, 692.8 steps.

Scope. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a measurement. β₂ = 0.95 appears in GPT-3 (arXiv:2005.14165 App. B), InstructGPT (arXiv:2203.02155 App. C), Llama 2 (arXiv:2307.09288 §2.2), DeepSeek-V3 (arXiv:2412.19437 §4.2), and the OLMo 2 7B config; none of these reports gives an ablation of β₂. In a 130M-parameter sweep, β₂ = 0.98 gave C4 loss 3.529 and 0.95 gave 3.535 (arXiv:2509.02046 Table 36). See ch-01 §4.
