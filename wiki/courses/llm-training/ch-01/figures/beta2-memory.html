What you should notice. At β₂ = 0.999, v̂ lags the spike by hundreds of steps — the effective step size stays too large long after the gradient has normalized, which is how a single outlier step causes a downstream loss spike. At β₂ = 0.95 (the Llama default), v̂ responds within ~20 steps and the adaptive denominator √v̂ + ε tracks current variance. Lower still (β₂ = 0.9) tracks faster but becomes jittery under normal noise, pushing the optimizer around. The Llama choice of 0.95 is the empirical sweet spot for the non-stationary loss landscapes of large-scale pretraining.
