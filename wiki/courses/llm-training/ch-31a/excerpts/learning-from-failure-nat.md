# Excerpt: Learning From Failure: Integrating Negative Examples when Fine-tuning Large Language Models as Agents (Wang, Li, Han, Zhang, Baldwin; LibrAI, MBZUAI, Melbourne)

Facts used by [[read]], read in the arXiv v2 PDF on 2026-09-15. The method is called Negative-Aware Training (NAT); it is unrelated to the "Negative Assistant Training" module in [[dust-into-gold-negative-distillation]].

## Method (§3.3)
- Trajectories are generated by GPT-3.5 (1106) three times per question at temperatures 0.2, 0.5, 0.7, in ReAct format with a SymPy calculator (math) or Serper search (QA). Each trajectory is labeled by comparing its answer with the ground truth.
- Negative-aware reformatting: positives get "Please generate a solution that **correctly** answers the question."; negatives get "Please generate a solution that **incorrectly** answers the question." (§3.3; the actual prompts are "slightly more complex", footnote 4).
- "The loss is computed only on the part of the text generated by the LLM." At inference, "we prompt the fine-tuned agent using the prompt for positive examples only."
- Baselines: Vanilla (positives only) and Negative-Unaware Training (NUT: negatives added without prefix or suffix).
- Training (§4.1): LLaMA-2-Chat 7B and 13B; 2 epochs; batch 64; cosine schedule, 3% warm-up; max LR 5×10⁻⁵; 4×A100, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3.

## Data (App. B)
- About 7k GSM8K training questions × 3 generations → about 9k positive and 12k negative trajectories; 5k unique positives. Main math experiments use 2k or 5k positives with 10k negatives.

## Results
- Math average over GSM8K, ASDiv, SVAMP, MultiArith (Table 2): 7B, 2k positives: Vanilla 55.90, NUT 63.39, NAT 64.64. 7B, 5k: 64.17, 64.95, 67.42. 13B, 2k: 65.18, 66.53, 67.74. 13B, 5k: 70.76, 69.10, 71.28.
- Negative quality (Table 5, 7B, 10k negatives): negatives from a fine-tuned LLaMA-2-7B (NAT-low) change the average by −3.16 (2k positives) and −6.20 (5k), versus +8.74 and +3.25 for GPT-3.5 negatives (§5.2).
- Quantity (Fig. 2): performance rises with negatives and plateaus at about 11k negatives for 2k and 5k positives. Fig. 3: the added value of 10k negatives shrinks as positives increase.
- Behavior (Table 6, GSM8K, 7B): accuracy / action error / average turns: Vanilla 35.63 / 3.58% / 3.12; NUT 44.43 / 10.47% / 3.92; NAT 46.93 / 7.90% / 3.71. Training-data action error: 4.01% (positives only), 15.33% (with negatives).
- Prompt content (Table 7): Correct/Incorrect 63.55; Incorrect/Correct 63.33; Good/Bad 63.91; A/B 63.15; Random string 1/Random string 2 64.04. The authors conclude that the gain of NAT comes from differentiating positive and negative data, not from the meaning of the prompt (§5.4).
- HotpotQA (Table 3, mean of 5 runs): 7B EM / F1 Vanilla 27.44 / 36.41, NUT 28.04 / 40.96, NAT 28.80 / 41.37, NAT-2 29.76 / 42.51. With 500 positives, NAT peaks at 500 negatives and decreases with more; NAT-2 peaks at 1k (§6.1, Fig. 5).
- StrategyQA (Table 4; 1,000 positives, 500 negatives): 7B Vanilla 55.40, NUT 62.40, NAT 65.80.
- CoT without tools (Table 8, 7B, 2k positives, 1.6k negatives): Vanilla 52.69, NUT 58.45, NAT 59.37.

## Limits stated by the source (§8)
- Requires ground-truth labels; not tested on more capable models.
