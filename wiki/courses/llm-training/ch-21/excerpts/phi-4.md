# Excerpt: Phi-4 — 50 synthetic categories and the "SFT ceiling dominates" claim

**Source library:** `wiki/raw-data/llm-training/model-reports/phi-4.md`
**Authors / Lab:** Microsoft Research (Marah Abdin et al.) — Phi-4 Dec 2024, Phi-4-reasoning Apr 2025.

---

## Why this source anchors ch-21 §4 end, and the Phi-series composition table

Ch-21 §4 includes a per-generation Phi composition table. Phi-4 is the right-most column, and the source of two specific claims the chapter makes:

1. Phi-4 uses "50 synthetic categories" of ~400B unweighted tokens. This is a flat taxonomy — not a tree, but also not the 20K-flat-topics shape of Phi-1.5; a coarser, deliberately enumerated list.
2. Phi-4-reasoning's RL stage is only 90 GRPO steps. "Most of the ceiling is set by SFT data quality" — the quote that anchors ch-21's argument for data-first synthesis.

---

## The 50-category flat taxonomy

From the source:

> **Phi-4 base:** 50 categories of synthetic data, ~400B unweighted tokens, injected into both pretraining and post-training.

Three facts:

- **Flat, not hierarchical.** Unlike GLAN's six-level tree or Phi-1.5's 20K-topic flat list, Phi-4 uses a coarse category index of 50. The axis of coverage is categories; depth within each category is delegated to the teacher.
- **Token scale dominates structure.** 400B unweighted tokens / 50 categories ≈ 8B tokens per category on average. This is textbook synthesis at *pretraining* scale, not instruction-data scale.
- **Categories list not disclosed.** From the Gaps section: "50 synthetic-category list" is withheld. The public paper describes the mechanism but not the specific categories chosen.

The ch-21 §5 "curator bias at the top" critique applies with force to Phi-4: 50 hand-picked category names, chosen by Microsoft researchers, determine the upper bound of Phi-4's synthetic coverage. Nobody outside Microsoft has audited that list.

## Pivotal-token DPO

From the source:

> **Pivotal-token DPO** — pairs constructed at tokens where the probability of final-answer correctness changes most.

Mechanically: run the base model on a reasoning prompt, look at each token position, and estimate `P(correct final answer | tokens so far)` before and after that token. If the delta is large, the token is "pivotal" — the branching point of the reasoning trace. Construct a DPO pair at that position by swapping in a token that leads toward correctness vs one that leads away.

This is a data-synthesis trick inside the preference-pair construction step (ch-19's bottom-up generator applied to preference data). It is *not* a taxonomy move; it is a fine-grained-within-generation move. Ch-21 mentions it for completeness — Phi-4's synthetic machinery includes both coarse (50 categories) and fine (pivotal-token) structure.

## Phi-4-reasoning — the 1.4M / 16B / 90 argument

From the source:

> **Phi-4-reasoning SFT:** 1.4M prompts filtered to the "boundary of base-model capability," with long reasoning traces generated by o3-mini in high-thinking mode at 32K context.
> **Phi-4-reasoning RL:** GRPO with rewards that combine correctness (+1 / −0.5) with a length-aware accuracy bonus and explicit penalties for (a) missing `<eos>`, (b) unclosed `<think>` blocks, (c) n-gram repetition with n=5.
> **RL is cheap:** only 90 GRPO steps produce >10% AIME gain; further steps yield little — SFT ceiling dominates.

The composition:

| Stage | Dataset | Teacher | Tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pretraining | Phi-4 base: 50-category synthetic + filtered web | GPT-4-class internal | ~400B unweighted synth + rest web |
| Post-SFT | 1.4M prompts, long-CoT | o3-mini (high-think, 32K) | ~16B total, ~8.3B unique |
| RL | 90 GRPO steps, rule-based verifier | — | (small) |

Ch-21's argument for "SFT dominates the ceiling":

- 16B tokens of o3-mini-distilled reasoning traces produce the bulk of Phi-4-reasoning's capability.
- 90 GRPO steps produce the last ~10% of AIME performance.
- Past 90 steps, improvement plateaus — the base model's SFT-installed distribution has been tuned as far as RL can tune it without better data.

This is the strongest published argument that, when your synthetic SFT corpus is well-curated and distilled from a strong teacher, RL is polish, not a separate data-scaling dimension. Contrast with DeepSeek-R1 (ch-20), which runs long RL from a weaker SFT base — different philosophy, different ceiling.

## The boundary-of-capability prompt filter

From the source:

> 1.4M prompts filtered to the "boundary of base-model capability," with long reasoning traces generated by o3-mini in high-thinking mode at 32K context.
> Prompts filtered to boundary of base-model competence (not too easy, not impossible).

This filter is ch-21 §7's "prompt curation is a distinct cost-bearing stage" principle operationalized. The filter:
- Drops prompts where the base model always succeeds — no teaching signal.
- Drops prompts where the base model always fails — the teacher's traces are too distant from the student's distribution for SFT to lift it.
- Keeps prompts in the middle, where the student *sometimes* gets it right on its own and the teacher's longer trace is transferable.

The mechanism of the filter itself is not disclosed (see Gaps). But the principle generalizes: the most useful SFT prompts for reasoning are the ones at the edge of the student's current competence, and a separate filter stage — with its own cost — is needed to find them.

---

## Reward shaping — what survives the 90-step RL

From the source:

> **Reward shape (Phi-4-reasoning GRPO):**
> - +1 for correct answer, −0.5 for incorrect.
> - Length-aware bonus: encourage concise outputs on correct answers; permit more think tokens on incorrect.
> - Penalty for missing EOS or unclosed `<think>` block.
> - n-gram repetition penalty with n=5 — discourages degenerate loops.

This reward is rule-based, not RM-based. That choice is coherent with the "SFT dominates" thesis — a trained RM would be a second model whose biases must be audited, and Phi-4-reasoning chose not to spend the audit budget on an RM when the SFT base was already strong enough to need only 90 steps.

The n-gram repetition penalty with n=5 is the mechanism that prevents the 90-step RL from collapsing the model into repetitive outputs — a known failure mode when RL is run against purely accuracy-based rewards on reasoning traces. The length-aware bonus ("concise when right, longer when unsure") is the mechanism that keeps the model from learning to always emit the longest possible trace.

## What the report hides

From the source's Gaps section:

> Search-surfaced summary withholds: GRPO group size G, clip ε, KL coefficient, learning rate, batch size, rollouts per prompt, AdamW betas, exact prompt-filter thresholds, RM used (if any) for boundary selection, 50 synthetic-category list, pivotal-token detection algorithm.

For ch-21's purposes, the two load-bearing omissions are:

1. **The 50-category list.** Not published. Audit impossible.
2. **The pivotal-token detection algorithm.** Not published. Reproduction impossible.

Phi-4 is therefore a *claim* about synthetic-data scaling, not a *reproducible experiment*. Ch-21 uses it as the most recent data point on the Phi trajectory, with the caveat that it is closed-data closed-recipe.
