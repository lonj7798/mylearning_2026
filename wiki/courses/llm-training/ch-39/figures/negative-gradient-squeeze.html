Where the pushed-down probability mass goes

One next-token distribution over four tokens. A negative gradient on token y⁻ changes the logits by Δz = −η(e_{y⁻} − p): the target logit falls by η(1 − p_{y⁻}) and every other logit rises by η p_j, in proportion to how likely that token already is. Adjust which token is pushed down, the step size, and whether a positive step on another token is applied at the same time. The pattern shown here is the squeezing effect proved for logistic regression in Ren and Sutherland (2024), App. E.

"Concentration" rescales the base logits: higher values make the distribution peakier, which Ren and Sutherland report makes the effect stronger. The base distribution at concentration 1.00 is (0.70, 0.20, 0.08, 0.02), the worked example in the chapter's negative-feedback section.

Reading to take away: pushing down a token that is already unlikely returns almost all of its mass to the current argmax, and tokens in the middle of the distribution lose mass as well. A positive step on another token competes with that concentration rather than cancelling it — which is the token-level counterpart of the sequence-level result in Pal et al. (2024) §3, where the DPO gradient lowers the logit of the correct token at every position after the position where the two responses differ.
