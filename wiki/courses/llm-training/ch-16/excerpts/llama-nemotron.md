# Excerpt: Llama-Nemotron — one-sided pass-rate cut and progressive batching

Used by [[read]] §1, §3.1, §5 and the Recipe table. Loci refer to the cached primary text
(`scratchpad/sources/llama-nemotron.txt`, arXiv:2505.00949v5).

## Which model gets RL (§5)

"we apply reasoning RL exclusively to LN-Ultra" — the report states that its preliminary
experiments, consistent with DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025), found RL to give suboptimal results for
smaller models compared with distillation, and that resource constraints motivated the choice.
LN-Ultra is the 253B model; the whole RL run is stated as approximately 140k H100 hours (§5.1).

## RL setup (§5.1)

- Algorithm: GRPO, NeMo-Aligner development branch (§5.2.1).
- Rollout prompt size 72, 16 responses per prompt, temperature = 1, top_p = 1.
- Global batch size 576, 2 gradient updates per rollout.
- Rewards: accuracy reward, where Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct judges whether the prediction matches the
  ground-truth answer (a number, a sentence, or a paragraph); format reward for `<think>` tags in
  "detailed thinking on" mode, and for their absence in "detailed thinking off" mode.
- Progress metric: GPQA-Diamond Avg@4 over roughly 500 training steps (Figure 5).

## Difficulty filtering (§5.1), quoted

"To ensure that the model is adequately challenged, we preprocess the data by independently
generating 8 responses per question using LN-Super, calculating the pass rate, and then
intentionally discarding prompts with a pass rate of 0.75 or higher, thereby increasing the
difficulty of the training data."

Two properties the chapter uses: the cut is one-sided (nothing is said about discarding
pass-rate-0 prompts), and the measuring model (LN-Super) is not the trained model (LN-Ultra).

## Progressive batching curriculum (§5.1), quoted in substance

A target distribution of pass rates is computed for each sequential batch, "modeled using a
Gaussian function centered on a difficulty level that progresses from high pass rates (easier
examples) for initial batches to low pass rates (harder examples) for later batches". Samples are
allocated to a batch from that target distribution, with leftover capacity filled from the pass-rate
bins that have the largest remaining pools; samples inside a batch are shuffled. Figure 6 compares
curriculum against random ordering on GPQA-Diamond Avg@4 over about 200 steps.

## Data provenance for the science domain (§4)

Science questions come from StackOverflow extracts and synthetic MCQs generated by prompting
Qwen2.5 models conditioned on topic, subtopic, and difficulty level, with variations generated
following the OpenMathInstruct-2 pipeline. All questions, real and synthetic, are decontaminated
against GPQA, MMLU, and MMLU-Pro test sets.

## Not reported

The number of RL prompts after filtering; any ablation of the 0.75 threshold; the pass-rate
distribution of the pool; whether pass-rate-0 prompts were kept or dropped.
