# Excerpt: Learning From Failure: Integrating Negative Examples when Fine-tuning Large Language Models as Agents (Wang, Li, Han, Zhang, Baldwin; LibrAI, MBZUAI, University of Melbourne)

Facts used by [[read]] §5 and the Negative samples section. Read in the arXiv v2 PDF on 2026-09-15. The method is Negative-Aware Training (NAT).

## Method (§3.3, §4.1)
- GPT-3.5 (1106) generates ReAct trajectories with a calculator (math) or Serper search (QA); each trajectory is labeled positive or negative by comparing its final answer with the ground truth.
- Reformatting: positives receive "Please generate a solution that **correctly** answers the question."; negatives receive "Please generate a solution that **incorrectly** answers the question." Footnote 4: the actual prompts are "slightly more complex".
- The loss is computed only on text generated by the LLM. At inference the fine-tuned agent receives the positive prompt only.
- Baselines: Vanilla (positives only) and NUT (negatives added without any prefix or suffix).
- Training: LLaMA-2-Chat 7B and 13B; 2 epochs; batch 64; cosine schedule with 3% warm-up; maximum LR 5 × 10⁻⁵ (§4.1).

## Data (App. B)
- About 7k GSM8K training questions × 3 generations give about 9k positive and 12k negative trajectories (5k unique positives). Main math experiments pair 2k or 5k positives with 10k negatives.

## Results
- Quantity (Figure 2): accuracy rises with the number of negatives and levels off at about 11k negatives for both 2k and 5k positives ("samples is about 11k in both cases").
- Math average over GSM8K, ASDiv, SVAMP, MultiArith (Table 2): 7B with 2k positives: Vanilla 55.90, NUT 63.39, NAT 64.64. 7B with 5k: 64.17, 64.95, 67.42. 13B with 2k: 65.18, 66.53, 67.74. 13B with 5k: 70.76, 69.10, 71.28.
- Negative quality (Table 5, 7B, 10k negatives): negatives from a fine-tuned LLaMA-2-7B (NAT-low) average 52.74 (2k positives) and 57.97 (5k), which is −3.16 and −6.20 versus Vanilla; GPT-3.5 negatives (NAT-high) give +8.74 and +3.25 (§5.2).
- Quantity: at a fixed 10k negatives, the added value of negatives shrinks as positives grow (Figure 3).
- Behavior on GSM8K, 7B (Table 6), accuracy / action error / average turns: Vanilla 35.63 / 3.58% / 3.12; NUT 44.43 / 10.47% / 3.92; NAT 46.93 / 7.90% / 3.71. Action error in training data: 4.01% (positives only) and 15.33% (with negatives).
- Prefix content (Table 7): Correct/Incorrect 63.55; Incorrect/Correct 63.33; Good/Bad 63.91; A/B 63.15; Random string 1/Random string 2 64.04. The authors conclude that the gain comes from separating positive and negative data, not from the meaning of the prompt (§5.4).

## Limits stated (§8)
Requires ground-truth labels to classify trajectories; not tested on more capable models.
