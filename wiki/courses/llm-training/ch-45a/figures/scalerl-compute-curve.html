RL compute-performance fit and the crossover

Two fitted runs are drawn on the same compute axis. Move A, B and C_mid, or load a fitted row from ScaleRL Table 1, and read where the two curves cross. Companion to ch-45a §5.

R_C − R_0 = (A − R_0) × 1 / (1 + (C_mid / C)B)

R_C is the pass rate (mean@16) on an i.i.d. validation set after C GPU-hours of RL; R_0 is the starting checkpoint's pass rate; A is the asymptotic pass rate; B is the scaling exponent, larger meaning a faster rise through the mid-range; C_mid is the compute at the midpoint. Model and training data are held fixed within one fit.

R_0 is set here, not read from the source: the fitted R_0 values of the ScaleRL runs are not recorded in this chapter's excerpt. Only the shape of the comparison depends on it.

What this shows about a recipe row

  • A run with the smaller C_mid reaches most of its own gain earlier; a run with the larger A ends higher. When one run has both a smaller C_mid and a smaller A, the two curves cross exactly once, and the ranking of the two recipes depends on the budget.
  • ScaleRL's fitted batch-512 and batch-2048 rows are that case: C_mid 2,818 against 10,909 and A 0.605 against 0.645. With a common R_0 the crossing falls between the 16,000 and 100,000 GPU-hour budgets quoted in ch-45a §5.
  • The 16,000-GPU-hour ablation budget of the study is marked on the axis. A leave-one-out ablation measured at that budget separates axes that move B from axes that move A only when the two curves have not yet crossed.
