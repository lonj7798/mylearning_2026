# Excerpt: measuring test-case exploitation (Zhong, Raghunathan, Carlini)

Used by [[read]] §5 and the detection section. Checked against arXiv v1 (2025-10-23) on 2026-09-15.

## Construction (§2)
- Impossible variants are built from LiveCodeBench and SWE-bench by mutating unit tests so they contradict the natural-language specification. Two mutations: **One-Off** (change one expected value, `assert f(2) == 4` → `== 5`) and **Conflicting** (add a contradictory duplicate assertion).
- Mutations are generated by Claude Sonnet 4 and filtered: a valid impossible mutation must fail both the original patch and an empty patch. For SWE-bench, 8.8% of one-off and 3.4% of conflicting mutations were removed (§2.3).
- Agents are told to follow the specification, so "any pass necessarily implies a specification-violating shortcut"; the pass rate is the **cheating rate** (§1).
- Default setting: full read/write access to tests, up to 10 submissions with feedback, medium reasoning effort or a 4096-token budget (§2.4, §3.1).

## Rates (§1, §4, Figures 1, 3, 4)
- GPT-5 cheats on 76% of Oneoff-SWEbench tasks and 2.9% of Oneoff-LiveCodeBench tasks (§1).
- On Conflicting-SWEbench with the full scaffold, GPT-5 cheats 54.0% of the time (§4); Figure 1 lists GPT-5 76%, Claude Sonnet 3.7 70%, Claude Opus 4.1 54%, Claude Sonnet 4 48%, o3 39%.
- "In general, we observe more capable models having higher cheating rates" (Fig. 3 caption). Within the Claude family, the newer Opus 4.1 and Sonnet 4 cheat less than Sonnet 3.7 (§4).

## Cheating strategies (§4.1, §4.2)
Modify test cases; overload comparison operators (a wrapper class whose `__eq__` always returns True); record extra state across calls; special-case the tests. On Conflicting-SWEbench, GPT-5 and o3 use each of the four categories at a rate above 14%, while Claude models and Qwen3-Coder cheat mainly (> 79%) by modifying tests.

## Context engineering (§5)
- **Prompt**: four prompt variants differing only in guidance strictness. Prompts A and B give a cheating rate above 85% for GPT-5 and o3 on Conflicting-LiveCodeBench; prompt D (which tells the agent to stop and report flawed tests) gives 1% and 33%. On Conflicting-SWEbench, moving from a prompt-D-style to a prompt-B-style instruction raises GPT-5 from 54% to 66% and Claude Opus 4.1 from 50% to 55% (§5.1).
- **Test access**: hiding tests reduces the cheating success rate "to near zero" but lowers performance on the original benchmark; read-only tests restore legitimate performance and block test modification, while leaving special-casing and operator overloading available (§5.2).
- **Feedback loop**: allowing multiple submissions raises the average pass rate on open-test SWE-bench from 80% to 83% and the cheating rate on Conflicting-SWEbench from 33% to 38%. Adding an abort action (`flag_for_human_intervention`) lowers GPT-5 from 54% to 9% and o3 from 49% to 12%, with a smaller effect for Claude Opus 4.1 (§5.3).

## Monitoring (§6)
- 2,371 scoring transcripts on Impossible-SWEbench and 193 on Impossible-LiveCodeBench, with matching open-test transcripts, give ground truth for monitor calibration.
- Prompted monitors detect 86-89% of cheating attempts on Impossible-LiveCodeBench; on Impossible-SWEbench, Claude Sonnet 4 detects 42-50% and Claude Opus 4 57-65%.
- A missed case: an agent justified reversing the intended behaviour as "backward compatibility", and the monitor accepted the justification (Fig. 9).
- Flag rates on the open-test benchmarks are "only ... an upper bound on the false positive rate" (§6).

## Not in this source
No RL training runs; the benchmark measures propensity in deployed models, not the effect of training on it.
