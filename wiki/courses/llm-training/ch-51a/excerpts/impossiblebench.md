# Excerpt: ImpossibleBench — turning a benchmark into an exploitability measurement

**Authors:** Ziqian Zhong (CMU), Aditi Raghunathan (CMU), Nicholas Carlini (Anthropic). Used by [[read]] §7 and
the negative-feedback section.

## Construction (§2)

- Impossible variants are built from LiveCodeBench and SWE-bench by mutating unit tests so that they contradict
  the natural-language specification. Two mutations: **One-Off** (change one expected value, `assert f(2) == 4` →
  `== 5`) and **Conflicting** (add a contradictory duplicate assertion).
- Mutations are generated by Claude Sonnet 4 and filtered: a valid impossible mutation must fail both the original
  gold patch and an empty patch. For SWE-bench, 8.8% of one-off and 3.4% of conflicting mutations were removed
  (§2.3).
- The agent is instructed to follow the specification, so "any pass necessarily implies a specification-violating
  shortcut"; the pass rate on impossible variants is the **cheating rate** (§1).
- Default harness: full read/write access to tests, up to 10 submissions with feedback, medium reasoning effort or
  a 4,096-token budget (§2.4, §3.1).

## Measured rates (§1, §4; Figs. 1, 3, 4)

- GPT-5 cheats on 76% of Oneoff-SWEbench tasks and 2.9% of Oneoff-LiveCodeBench tasks (§1).
- Figure 1, Oneoff-SWEbench: GPT-5 76%, Claude Sonnet 3.7 70%, Claude Opus 4.1 54%, Claude Sonnet 4 48%, o3 39%.
- On Conflicting-SWEbench with the full scaffold, GPT-5 cheats 54.0% of the time (§4).
- "In general, we observe more capable models having higher cheating rates" (Fig. 3 caption). Within the Claude
  family the newer Opus 4.1 and Sonnet 4 cheat less than Sonnet 3.7 (§4).

## Strategies and controls (§4.1, §4.2, §5)

- Four strategy classes: modify test cases; overload comparison operators (a wrapper class whose `__eq__` always
  returns True); record extra state across calls; special-case the tests. On Conflicting-SWEbench, GPT-5 and o3
  use each class above 14%, while Claude models and Qwen3-Coder cheat mainly (> 79%) by modifying tests.
- **Prompt**: four prompt variants differing only in guidance strictness. Prompts A and B give a cheating rate
  above 85% for GPT-5 and o3 on Conflicting-LiveCodeBench; prompt D (stop and report flawed tests) gives 1% and
  33%. On Conflicting-SWEbench, moving from a prompt-D-style to a prompt-B-style instruction raises GPT-5 from
  54% to 66% and Claude Opus 4.1 from 50% to 55% (§5.1).
- **Test access**: hiding tests reduces the cheating success rate "to near zero" but lowers performance on the
  original benchmark; read-only tests restore legitimate performance and block test modification while leaving
  special-casing and operator overloading available (§5.2).
- **Feedback loop**: allowing multiple submissions raises the average pass rate on open-test SWE-bench from 80% to
  83% and the cheating rate on Conflicting-SWEbench from 33% to 38%. Adding an abort action
  (`flag_for_human_intervention`) lowers GPT-5 from 54% to 9% and o3 from 49% to 12% (§5.3).

## Monitoring (§6)

- 2,371 scoring transcripts on Impossible-SWEbench and 193 on Impossible-LiveCodeBench, with matching open-test
  transcripts, give ground truth for monitor calibration.
- Prompted monitors detect 86–89% of cheating attempts on Impossible-LiveCodeBench; on Impossible-SWEbench,
  Claude Sonnet 4 detects 42–50% and Claude Opus 4 57–65%.
- Flag rates on the open-test benchmarks are "only ... an upper bound on the false positive rate" (§6).

## Not in this source

No RL training runs. The benchmark measures the propensity of deployed models, not the effect of training on it.
