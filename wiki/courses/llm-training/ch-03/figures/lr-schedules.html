Why the horizon matters (Chinchilla Figure A1). Cosine has a fixed endpoint: lr(T) = min_lr. If you stop early, the LR never really decayed — your final optimization still has high step size and settles in a sharper-than-ideal minimum. If you train past the cosine horizon, LR is pinned at min_lr and you waste compute. Hoffmann et al. measured the penalty at roughly 0.3–1% validation perplexity for a 2× horizon mismatch. Always size cosine to the token budget you will actually complete.

Why WSD is checkpoint-able. The stable phase is a flat line at peak_lr — every point on it is statistically equivalent as a training state. You can save a checkpoint at, say, 60% of T, then fork a short decay phase from it to produce a deployable model at that token count, while the main trunk keeps going. DeepSeek and MiniCPM exploit this to produce many model variants from a single trunk, a trick cosine cannot match because every step of cosine has a different LR. Inverse-sqrt scales naturally with model width via the d_model^(-0.5) factor but loses ~0.3% to cosine on a fixed budget; constant LR after warmup leaves 1–3% on the table versus a proper decay.
