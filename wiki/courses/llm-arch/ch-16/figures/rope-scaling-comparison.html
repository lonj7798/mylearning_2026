What You Are Seeing

Top chart: The effective frequency (theta_i) for each RoPE dimension after scaling. Original RoPE frequencies are shown in gray. Each method modifies these frequencies differently to accommodate longer sequences.

Bottom chart: The maximum rotation angle each dimension experiences at the extended context length L'. The dashed gray line shows the maximum angle seen during training. Dimensions whose bars exceed this line are out of distribution — the source of length generalization failure.

Drag the extension factor slider to see how each method responds to increasing context length. Notice how PI uniformly compresses all frequencies, NTK-Aware preferentially compresses low frequencies, and YaRN applies a smooth ramp that leaves high frequencies untouched.

High Frequency (short wavelength)

Wavelength < L. These dimensions complete many full rotations during training — they extrapolate naturally. PI unnecessarily compresses them, degrading local position resolution.

Medium Frequency (boundary)

Wavelength ~ L. These are at the boundary of extrapolation. YaRN applies a smooth ramp here, interpolating between "leave alone" and "fully scale."

Low Frequency (long wavelength)

Wavelength > L. These dimensions have NOT completed a full rotation during training. They encounter novel angles at L' > L and cause the perplexity cliff. All methods must scale these.
