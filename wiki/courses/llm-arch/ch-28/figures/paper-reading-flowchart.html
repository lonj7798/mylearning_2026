The Three-Pass Paper Reading Method

An efficient strategy for reading LLM architecture papers. Most papers do not survive past Pass 2.

Pass 1: Skeleton
~5 minutes
Read Abstract + Figures + Results Tables
Skip everything else. Identify the claimed bottleneck, the structural change, and the magnitude of improvement.
↓
Answer Three Questions
1. What bottleneck is claimed?
2. What structural change is proposed?
3. How large is the improvement?
↓
Can you answer all three clearly?
If the abstract is vague or figures are unclear, that is itself a negative signal.
↓
No → STOP
~80% of papers. File for reference or discard. Do not invest more time.
Yes → Proceed
~20% of papers. The contribution is clear enough to warrant deeper reading.
↓
Pass 2: Ablations
~15-20 minutes
Read Ablation Tables + Experimental Setup
Check: single-variable changes, meaningful baselines, appropriate metrics, reported variance. Skip Related Work.
↓
Apply Mental Ablation
For each proposed component: "What would happen without this?" Identify which components are structurally necessary vs. optional improvements.
↓
Do ablations support the claims?
Are the improvements real? Are baselines fair? Are effects larger than noise?
↓
No → STOP
~50% of Pass-1 survivors. Weak ablations, unfair baselines, or noisy results.
Partial → Flag Gaps
Promising but incomplete evidence. Note specific gaps for Pass 3 investigation.
Yes → Proceed
~50% of Pass-1 survivors. Ablations are clean and support the claimed contribution.
↓
Pass 3: Full Read
~30-60 minutes
Read Complete Paper
Methodology, training details, related work, appendices. Assess reproducibility: could you implement this from the paper alone?
↓
Classify the Contribution
1. New bottleneck identification
2. Novel structural solution
3. Engineering optimization
↓
Record Assessment
Contribution type, key ablation findings, reproducibility rating, open questions, relevance to your work.
