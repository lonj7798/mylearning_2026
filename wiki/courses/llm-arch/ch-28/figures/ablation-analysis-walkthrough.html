Ablation Quality Checklist

Click each item to check it off as you evaluate an ablation study. A good ablation satisfies all eight.

Single-variable changes: Each row changes exactly one component from the baseline.
Meaningful baseline: Comparison is against the strongest reasonable alternative, not a straw man.
Matched compute: All rows use the same training budget (steps, data, hardware).
Appropriate metric: The metric matches the claimed contribution (speed claim = speed metric).
Variance reported: Error bars or standard deviation across seeds are shown.
Scale appropriate: The ablation scale matches the target deployment scale, or scaling trends are shown.
Negative results included: Cases where the method does not help are reported.
Hyperparameters fair: The proposed method and baselines use equally-tuned hyperparameters.
How to Use This Checklist
Apply this checklist to every ablation table you encounter. No paper satisfies all eight criteria perfectly -- but papers that fail on 3+ criteria deserve extra skepticism.

Most common failures: Missing variance (item 5), unmatched compute (item 3), and missing negative results (item 7). These three alone account for most misleading ablations in architecture papers.
