Weight transfer: fan-out cost, sampler lag, and in-flight requests

Companion to ch-05. "Rocket p. N" refers to [[mai-rl-infrastructure]]. Inputs marked illustrative are not stated by any source; Rocket does not state its model bytes, transport, or number of selected engines (ch-05 §2.3).

A. Learner-side cost: per-replica sends vs Rocket's two-stage transfer

Per-replica: the learner sends one full copy (S bytes) to each of R replicas, R·S in total, so "learner-side communication cost grow[s] with the inference fleet size" (Rocket p. 6). Two-stage: "selected sampler engines first pull packed parameter chunks from the learner, and then distribute them to corresponding ranks across sampler replicas" (p. 6–7). Here the learner serves one copy per source group G; Rocket v1 split a 36-server fleet into 4 groups of 9 ([[mai-thinking-1]] Sec. 3.6.5).

Lower bound = bytes / (NICs × 50 GB/s). 50 GB/s is one 400 Gb/s ConnectX-7 NIC on DeepSeek's H800 platform, where internode dispatch measured 43–46 GB/s against 153 GB/s intranode ([[deepseek-open-infra]] DeepEP table). Rocket's measured result is 15 s per transfer "in our current configuration and training run" (p. 7); without the bytes it cannot be turned into a bandwidth. Fan-out inside the fleet is not counted at the learner: it runs over the replicas' own NICs and NVLink. For scale, verl moved Qwen3-30B-A3B to 30 receivers in about 7 s with NCCL on 4 × 8 H100 (verl@8357f1e checkpoint_engine/README.md L55–74).

B. Refresh every k steps: sampler lag 0 … k − 1

If the sampler is refreshed every k learner steps and the transfer is instant, the sampler serves the weights of the most recent multiple of k, so lag s − v ∈ {0, …, k − 1}. Rocket's Fig. 4 run: k = 4, refreshes at 104, 108, …, 124, "sampler lag is 0–3 steps"; transfer 15 s (Rocket p. 6–7).
