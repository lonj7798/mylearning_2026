The 1-Bit Cliff — Binary, Ternary, INT4, FP16

Perplexity gap vs FP16 at different bit budgets per weight, on a Llama-scale model (~3B params, approximate published numbers). Toggle between post-training quantization (squeeze a pretrained FP16 model) and trained from scratch (BitNet-style: the model learns to live at this precision from step 0).

training regime:
1-bit (BitNet binary) 1.58-bit (BitNet b1.58 ternary) 2-bit (AQLM) 4-bit (GPTQ / AWQ) FP16 baseline
Why the cliff is at 1.5 bits. Going from binary {−1, +1} to ternary {−1, 0, +1} adds only ~0.58 bits of capacity but unlocks a qualitatively new operation: the network can express "this weight should be inactive". With binary weights, every connection is forced to contribute either positively or negatively — you cannot prune at the weight level. Ternary brings sparsity back, and that single degree of freedom collapses the perplexity gap to near zero at 3B+ scales.

Why training regime matters. PTQ assumes you can find a discrete code close to the pretrained FP16 weight. At 4-bit that's true (~16 levels). At 2-bit it gets tight but AQLM's vector codebooks bail you out. At 1.58-bit PTQ cannot recover quality — the pretrained weights live on a continuous manifold that {−1, 0, +1} simply cannot reach. The fix is to train the model with the ternary quantizer in the forward pass from the start: the optimizer routes information through the ternary basis natively, and at sufficient scale matches FP16 perplexity. Pure 1-bit binary stays broken even with from-scratch training unless you scale aggressively — zero is too useful a state to discard.
