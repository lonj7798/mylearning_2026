The Shannon lower bound D(R) = σ²·2−2R is the floor every quantizer must live above. Real LLM-weight schemes sit at fixed bit-widths along this curve — the constant-factor gap above the line is what each method optimizes.

How to read this. The black line is the information-theoretic floor — no quantizer can achieve lower normalized distortion D/σ² at a given bit budget. Each marker is plotted at its bit-width on the bound, then nudged upward by an empirical loss-factor: NF4 beats INT4 because its non-uniform codebook is tuned for Gaussian-shaped weights; BitNet 1.58 trades a large distortion gap for huge memory savings and recovers it through quantization-aware training.
