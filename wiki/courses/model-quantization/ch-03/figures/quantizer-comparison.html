Three Quantizers on a Gaussian Source

Uniform spacing wastes levels in the tails. Lloyd-Max places levels by density. Companding warps the input through a log-like map before uniform quantization — cheap and close to optimal.

What the gap means. The Shannon bound for a Gaussian source is D(R) = σ²·2−2R, so R = log₂K gives D_min = σ²/K². Uniform sits well above the bound because it spends bits on rare tail values. Lloyd-Max converges to the locally optimal codebook for the source PDF — the centroids cluster near zero where the density is highest. Companding gets ~90% of the way there with a fixed analytic map and no training.
