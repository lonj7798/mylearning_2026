# Excerpt: bfloat16 — the FP32-exponent training default

**Authors:** Google Brain (introduced for Cloud TPU v2, 2018); popularised by NVIDIA Ampere (A100, 2020).
**Year:** 2018 (TPU v2); 2020 (Ampere).
**URLs:** see source_url; reference paper https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12322

---

## The one-box layout

```
[ s | e_7 e_6 e_5 e_4 e_3 e_2 e_1 e_0 | m_6 m_5 m_4 m_3 m_2 m_1 m_0 ]
   1            8 (bias 127)                          7
```

```math
\text{value} = (-1)^s \cdot (1 + m/2^7) \cdot 2^{e - 127} \quad\text{(normal)}
```

**Exactly the top 16 bits of FP32**: same sign, same 8-bit exponent (bias 127), mantissa truncated 23 → 7. Conversion is a bit-shift (FP32 → BF16: RNE the bottom 16 mantissa bits; BF16 → FP32: append 16 zero bits, exact and lossless).

---

## Key constants

- Smallest positive normal: `2^{−126}` ≈ 1.18e−38 (same as FP32).
- Largest finite: `(2 − 2^{−7}) · 2^{127}` ≈ 3.39e+38 (same as FP32, modulo mantissa).
- Machine epsilon `ε = 2^{−7}` ≈ 7.81e−3.
- Subnormals: defined (`e = 0, m ≠ 0`), but most hardware treats subnormals as FTZ.

---

## BF16 vs FP16 — the critical comparison

| | FP16 | BF16 |
|---|---|---|
| Sign | 1 | 1 |
| Exponent | 5 (bias 15) | **8 (bias 127)** |
| Mantissa | **10** | 7 |
| Max finite | 65504 | 3.39e+38 |
| Min positive normal | 6.10e−5 | 1.18e−38 |
| ε (relative precision) | 9.77e−4 | 7.81e−3 |

BF16 trades **~8× worse relative precision** for **~10^{33}× more dynamic range**. For transformer training the precision gap doesn't matter (SGD/Adam noise dominates); the range gap does (attention scores routinely > 65k).

---

## Why FP16 needs loss scaling and BF16 doesn't

FP16's smallest normal is 6e−5 → gradients smaller than that underflow to 0; loss scaling multiplies loss by `2^k` pre-backward and divides gradients by `2^k` post-backward to shift gradients into the representable range. BF16's smallest normal is 1e−38 → gradients essentially never underflow → no loss scaling needed.

---

## Standard mixed-precision recipe

- **FP32:** master weights, optimizer state, loss accumulator.
- **BF16:** forward activations, weights for matmul, gradients.
- **Matmul:** `BF16 × BF16 → FP32 tensor-core accumulator → BF16 output`.
- **No loss scaling. No overflow handling.**

---

## Hardware support

- **Google TPU v2+** (2018+): native BF16 matmul, FP32 accumulator.
- **NVIDIA Ampere A100 (2020) onward**: BF16 tensor cores at 312 TFLOPS.
- **Intel Cooper Lake / Sapphire Rapids**: AVX-512-BF16 (`VCVTNE2PS2BF16`, `VDPBF16PS`).
- **AMD MI100+, Apple Neural Engine, Cerebras, Graphcore**: all native BF16.

---

## Limitations

- 7-bit mantissa is sometimes insufficient for *small* models or very-end-of-training fine-tuning (gradient signal becomes precision-bounded).
- For LoRA fine-tuning at very low LR, sometimes need FP32 master copy of LoRA params.
- BF16 is *not* in IEEE-754 (no subnormal guarantees, encodings vary by vendor for FTZ).
