KV-Cache Asymmetry — K is Channel-Outlier, V is Token-Gaussian

Synthetic K and V matrices (T=256 tokens × D=64 channels). RoPE injects a position-dependent sinusoidal scaling on a handful of channels of K — those channels become persistent outliers. V has no such structure: it's just per-token mixtures with no preferred channels. Opposite outlier geometry ⇒ opposite quantization axes.

KIVI's recipe — match the quant axis to the outlier axis

K: 1 scale per channel (D scales, here D=64) — outliers contained inside their column.
V: 1 scale per token (T scales, here T=256) — each row gets its own dynamic range.
The asymmetry, made operational. Quantizing the wrong axis destroys both caches: per-token K would bundle an outlier channel with normal channels in the same scale, blowing the bit budget; per-channel V would estimate D scales from data that doesn't actually have channel structure. KIVI inverts the choice for each cache — per-channel K, per-token V — and that single asymmetry is what makes INT2 KV cache viable with negligible quality loss.
