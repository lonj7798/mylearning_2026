QuaRot vs AQLM — Two Roads to Sub-4-Bit

Both methods break the per-tensor INT4 ceiling, but they attack the problem from opposite ends. QuaRot makes activations and weights friendlier to round-to-nearest by rotation; AQLM abandons round-to-nearest entirely and encodes weight blocks as sums of codebook entries.

2.0

QuaRot W4A4 via rotation

Rotate W and the activations by a Hadamard H; the matmul is invariant ((xHᵀ)(HW) = xW) but both operands become flat. Then plain INT4 round-to-nearest works for both sides — W4A4.

AQLM additive codebooks, sub-2-bit

A weight vector w ∈ ℝᵈ is approximated as w ≈ C₁[i₁] + C₂[i₂] + … + C_M[i_M]. With M=2 codebooks of 256 vectors each over a group of d=8 weights, storage is 2·8 = 16 bits per 8 weights = 2 bits/weight.

Complementary, not competing. QuaRot fixes the basis so per-element INT4 stops wasting bits on outliers — its sweet spot is W4A4 inference (both weights and activations in 4 bits, kernels are dense INT4 matmul). AQLM fixes the code by giving up scalar quantization entirely — its sweet spot is sub-2-bit weight-only storage where scalar grids are too coarse to represent anything. Frontier post-training quantization stacks (AQLM + Hadamard, QuIP#, QTIP) combine rotation with vector quantization to get both.
