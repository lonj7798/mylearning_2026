NF4 vs INT4 vs FP4 — 4-Bit Codebooks on N(0, 1)

Three different ways to spend 16 code-words over a unit Gaussian, the dominant empirical distribution of LLM weight columns after per-block normalization.

Why NF4 wins for Gaussian weights. INT4 spaces its 16 levels uniformly over [−1, 1] — half the bits get spent on the tails where the Gaussian has almost no mass. FP4 (E2M1) is log-spaced; it concentrates levels near zero but its largest values reach ±6, again wasting resolution on the tails. NF4 picks its 16 levels as the 16-quantile points of the standard normal — by construction, each level represents an equal-probability bin of the input distribution, which is the MSE-optimal allocation for that distribution. The win is ~0.3–0.5 PPL on Llama-class models for free: no hardware change, just a different LUT.
