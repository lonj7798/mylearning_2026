# Understanding Reasoning LLMs: Methods and Strategies for Building and Refining Reasoning Models

- **Core Insight:** Four distinct approaches to reasoning exist (inference scaling, pure RL, SFT+RL, distillation), each with different costs.
- **Guideline:** Match reasoning approach to compute budget — distillation is cheapest, pure RL is most novel.

- **Author:** Sebastian Raschka, PhD
- **URL:** https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms
- **Relevant chapters:** Reasoning, RLHF, training pipelines, distillation

## Summary
A practical guide to four approaches for building reasoning LLMs: inference-time scaling, pure RL (DeepSeek-R1-Zero), SFT + RL (DeepSeek-R1), and pure SFT/distillation. Explains how reasoning emerges as a behavior from reward signals alone, how the DeepSeek training pipeline works, and the tradeoffs between approaches at different model scales.

## Key Content

### What Are Reasoning Models?

Raschka defines reasoning as "the process of answering questions that require complex, multi-step generation with intermediate steps." Two presentation formats:
1. **Explicit:** Intermediate steps visible in output
2. **Hidden:** Multiple iterations processed internally (e.g., OpenAI's o1)

**When NOT to use reasoning models:** Summarization, translation, knowledge retrieval. Reasoning models can "overthink" simple problems, introducing errors while wasting compute.

### Approach 1: Inference-Time Scaling

Improves reasoning without modifying the model:

- **Chain-of-Thought prompting:** "Think step by step"
- **Majority voting:** Generate multiple answers, select by vote
- **Beam search:** Explore multiple generation paths
- **Process reward models:** Specialized models evaluate reasoning quality
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search:** Probabilistic exploration of solutions

DeepSeek categorized explicit inference-time scaling as "unsuccessful attempts" for R1, suggesting they relied on implicit scaling through naturally longer outputs instead.

### Approach 2: Pure Reinforcement Learning

**DeepSeek-R1-Zero** demonstrated that "reasoning emerges as a behavior from pure reinforcement learning" without supervised fine-tuning.

**Setup:**
- Base model: DeepSeek-V3 (671B parameters)
- Accuracy reward: LeetCode compiler for code, deterministic checking for math
- Format reward: LLM judge for proper structure (reasoning in XML tags)

**The "Aha" Moment:** The model spontaneously began generating reasoning traces and thinking steps despite no explicit training for this. This proves reward signals alone can trigger reasoning capabilities.

**Key insight:** Traditional RLHF includes supervised fine-tuning before RL. DeepSeek's innovation was eliminating the SFT step entirely.

### Approach 3: Supervised Fine-Tuning + RL (DeepSeek-R1)

The full pipeline, improving upon R1-Zero:

1. Use R1-Zero outputs to generate cold-start SFT data
2. Instruction fine-tune on SFT data
3. Apply RL with accuracy, format, and consistency rewards
4. Collect additional SFT data: 600K CoT examples from R1 + 200K knowledge examples from V3
5. Final instruction fine-tuning on 800K examples
6. Terminal RL with rule-based rewards + human preference labels

### Approach 4: Pure SFT and Distillation

Fine-tune smaller models on datasets generated by larger systems (not classical knowledge distillation using teacher logits):

- Teacher models generate SFT data
- Smaller student models (Llama 8B/70B, Qwen 0.5B-32B) fine-tune on this data
- Same 800K examples used for DeepSeek-R1

**Results:** Distilled models underperform R1 significantly but exceed R1-Zero despite being orders of magnitude smaller.

**Critical finding:** When DeepSeek applied pure RL (R1-Zero approach) to Qwen-32B, results lagged behind distillation. Pure RL may require larger model capacity to effectively induce reasoning.

### Scale-Dependent Strategy Selection

| Scale | Best Approach |
|-------|--------------|
| Large (671B) | Pure RL can induce reasoning |
| Medium (32B) | SFT outperforms pure RL; SFT + RL likely optimal |
| Small (<10B) | Pure SFT via distillation most practical |

### Budget-Conscious Approaches

**Sky-T1:** 32B parameters, 17K SFT samples, $450, comparable to o1
**TinyZero:** 3B parameters, pure RL, <$30, emergent self-verification
**Journey Learning:** SFT with incorrect solution paths + corrections, teaching self-correction through exposure to mistakes

### DeepSeek-R1 vs OpenAI o1

| Dimension | DeepSeek-R1 | OpenAI o1 |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Base Model | DeepSeek-V3 (671B) | Unknown |
| Inference Cost | Lower per-token | Higher per-token |
| Training Details | Published | Undisclosed |
| Inference Scaling | Minimal (implicit) | Suspected extensive |
| Open-Source | Yes (MIT) | Proprietary |

Raschka hypothesizes: R1 invested more in training optimization; o1 compensates with test-time compute.

## Notable Insights
- The emergence of reasoning from pure RL (R1-Zero) is arguably the most significant finding: reasoning isn't a feature you program but a behavior that emerges from well-designed reward signals.
- Format rewards (requiring XML-tagged reasoning) proved essential — channeling computation into structured pathways improves effectiveness.
- The scale-dependent strategy selection is practical wisdom: don't try pure RL on a 32B model when distillation with quality data works better.
- TinyZero achieving emergent reasoning at 3B parameters for <$30 democratizes this research area.
- DeepSeek's open publication of their training pipeline (while OpenAI keeps o1 proprietary) has accelerated the entire field.
