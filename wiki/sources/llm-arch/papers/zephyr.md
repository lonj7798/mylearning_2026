# Zephyr: Direct Distillation of LM Alignment
- **Authors:** Lewis Tunstall, Edward Beeching, Nathan Lambert, Nazneen Rajani, Kashif Rasul, Younes Belkada, Shengyi Huang, Leandro von Werra, Clémentine Fourrier, Nathan Habib, Nathan Sarrazin, Omar Sanseviero, Alexander M. Rush, Thomas Wolf (HuggingFace H4 team)
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16944
- **Core Insight:** Alignment can be fully distilled from larger teacher models — SFT on teacher completions plus DPO on teacher-labeled preference pairs aligns a 7B student to rival 70B+ chat models, with no human feedback in the loop.
- **Guideline:** When human preference annotation is the bottleneck, distill alignment: run dSFT on teacher-generated instruction completions, then dDPO on AI-ranked preference pairs (GPT-4 as judge). Skip RLHF's reward model and PPO loop entirely.
- **Relevant chapters:** Alignment, RLHF alternatives, Preference optimization, Post-training, Small-model efficiency

## Abstract
We aim to produce a smaller language model that is aligned to user intent. Previous research has shown that applying distilled supervised fine-tuning (dSFT) on larger models significantly improves task accuracy; however, these models are unaligned, i.e. they do not respond well to natural prompts. To distill this property, we experiment with the use of preference data from AI Feedback (AIF). Starting from a dataset of outputs ranked by a teacher model, we apply distilled direct preference optimization (dDPO) to learn a chat model with significantly improved intent alignment. The approach requires only a few hours of training without any additional sampling during fine-tuning. The final result, Zephyr-7B, sets a new state-of-the-art on chat benchmarks for 7B parameter models, and requires no human annotation. In particular, results on MT-Bench show that Zephyr-7B surpasses Llama2-Chat-70B, the best open-access RLHF-based model.

## Key Contributions
- Introduced the **dSFT + dDPO** recipe: a two-stage alignment pipeline that uses only teacher-model-generated data and teacher-model-ranked preferences — no human labels, no reward model, no RL loop
- Demonstrated that Zephyr-7B-β (distilled from Mistral-7B base) beats Llama-2-Chat-70B on MT-Bench, showing that alignment quality can outweigh a 10× parameter gap when the teacher is strong enough
- Showed that combining AI-feedback preference data (UltraFeedback, GPT-4-ranked) with DPO produces stable training in a few hours on standard hardware, with no on-policy sampling required during fine-tuning
- Empirically validated that dDPO outperforms dSFT alone by a wide margin on MT-Bench and AlpacaEval — preference data, even AI-generated, is strictly complementary to supervised distillation
- Released the full training stack (TRL, datasets, checkpoints), making the distilled-alignment recipe a community default for small chat models

## Technical Details

### The two-stage pipeline

**Stage 1 — dSFT (distilled Supervised Fine-Tuning).** Starting from a strong base model (Mistral-7B), fine-tune on the UltraChat dataset: ~200k multi-turn dialogues where both user prompts and assistant responses were generated by a teacher (GPT-3.5-turbo). Unlike human-written SFT data, every turn in the corpus comes from the teacher's distribution, so the student is directly imitating the teacher's instruction-following style. Loss is masked to assistant turns only, identical to standard SFT.

**Stage 2 — dDPO (distilled Direct Preference Optimization).** Build a synthetic preference dataset (UltraFeedback): for each prompt, sample 4 completions from a diverse pool of models (GPT-4, Claude, Llama-2-Chat, Falcon, etc.), then have **GPT-4 rank them** along helpfulness, honesty, and truthfulness axes. The highest-scored completion becomes $y_w$ and a randomly-chosen lower-scored completion becomes $y_l$. Apply the standard DPO loss on these (prompt, $y_w$, $y_l$) triples with the dSFT checkpoint as both the policy initialization and the reference model $\pi_{\text{ref}}$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{dDPO}}(\pi_\theta; \pi_{\text{dSFT}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{AIF}}} \left[\log \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{dSFT}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{dSFT}}(y_l|x)}\right)\right]$$

The "d" prefix (distilled) marks that both the completions and the preference labels are AI-generated — the entire alignment signal is distilled from teacher models rather than humans.

### Training recipe

- **Base model:** Mistral-7B (chosen for strong base-model quality per parameter; see [[mistral-7b]])
- **dSFT dataset:** UltraChat (~200k filtered teacher dialogues)
- **dDPO dataset:** UltraFeedback (~64k prompts × 4 completions, GPT-4 ranked)
- **Compute:** dSFT runs ~1–3 hours on 16× A100s; dDPO runs in ~2–4 hours. Full alignment takes under a day on a single 8× A100 node
- **Hyperparameters:** dDPO uses $\beta = 0.1$, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-7}$, 3 epochs; no warmup, cosine schedule. No on-policy sampling, no reward model, no critic
- **Reference policy:** The dSFT checkpoint is frozen and used as $\pi_{\text{ref}}$. Keeping $\beta$ small prevents catastrophic drift from the teacher's style while still allowing the preference signal to sharpen response quality

### Results

On **MT-Bench** (multi-turn chat, GPT-4 judged):
- Zephyr-7B-β: **7.34**
- Llama-2-Chat-70B (RLHF): 6.86
- Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1: 6.84
- GPT-3.5-turbo: 7.94

On **AlpacaEval** (pairwise win-rate vs GPT-4-Davinci):
- Zephyr-7B-β: **90.6%**
- Llama-2-Chat-70B: 92.7%
- Mistral-7B-Instruct: 75.0%

The key finding: dDPO alone adds ~1 point on MT-Bench over dSFT, closing most of the gap to the 70B RLHF-aligned baseline at a fraction of the parameters and training cost.

### Ablations and findings

- **dSFT alone is not enough.** The dSFT-only checkpoint scores ~6.6 on MT-Bench; dDPO lifts it to 7.34. Imitation of teacher completions teaches format but not preference-level quality.
- **GPT-4 as judge is load-bearing.** Using weaker judges (GPT-3.5) for ranking degrades downstream alignment quality — the preference signal is only as good as the teacher's discrimination ability.
- **Overfitting is risk, not asset.** Unlike SFT where more epochs usually helps, dDPO benefits from *more* epochs (3 is better than 1) — the model needs many passes over preference pairs to sharpen the margin, but preference data quality must be high or overfitting dominates.
- **No RL-specific instability.** Because DPO is a supervised loss, dDPO training is as stable as SFT. No reward hacking, no KL explosion, no PPO-tuning overhead.

### Relationship to [[dpo]], [[constitutional-ai]], [[instructgpt-rlhf]]

Zephyr sits at the intersection of three lines of work:
- **DPO** ([[dpo]]) supplies the loss function — Zephyr is one of the earliest public demonstrations that DPO works at chat-model scale
- **Constitutional AI / RLAIF** ([[constitutional-ai]]) pioneered AI-generated preferences; Zephyr replaces CAI's RL loop with DPO, making the AIF pipeline drastically simpler
- **InstructGPT** ([[instructgpt-rlhf]]) established that aligned small models can beat unaligned large models; Zephyr extends this to "aligned small + distilled preferences can beat aligned large + human preferences"

## Why This Paper Matters
Zephyr demonstrated that the human-annotation bottleneck in alignment is optional. For any team with API access to a strong teacher (GPT-4, Claude), the full alignment pipeline — instruction data, preference labels, training recipe — can be synthesized from AI feedback and trained with DPO in under a day. This collapsed the cost and complexity of producing a strong open 7B chat model from "large annotation contract + RLHF infrastructure" to "API budget + two fine-tuning runs." The recipe became the de facto template for open chat models (Tulu, OpenChat, Starling, later OLMo-Instruct) and validated AI-feedback distillation as a production alignment strategy, not just a research curiosity.
