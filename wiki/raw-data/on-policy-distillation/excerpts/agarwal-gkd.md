# GKD — On-Policy Distillation of Language Models (Agarwal et al., ICLR 2024)
- **Core Insight:** Auto-regressive KD fails because the student is trained on the teacher's/ground-truth sequences but is evaluated on its OWN generations (train-inference distribution mismatch / exposure bias); GKD fixes this by distilling on the student's self-generated sequences with per-token teacher feedback.
- **Guideline:** Sample sequences from the *student* (λ→1, on-policy), score each token against the teacher, and pick the divergence to match capacity — forward KL when the student can mimic the teacher, reverse KL / generalized-JSD(β) when it cannot (mode-seeking on teacher-likely tokens).
- **Source:** Agarwal, Vieillard, Zhou, Stanczyk, Ramos, Geist, Bachem, "On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes," ICLR 2024. arXiv:2306.13649 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649, HTML v3). Abstract = verbatim; formulas/results = verbatim quotes lifted from the arXiv HTML body.
- **Relevant chapters:** on-policy distillation, KD, exposure bias / distribution mismatch, generalized divergences (fwd/rev KL, JSD), RLHF+distillation integration

## The problem (verbatim abstract)
"Knowledge distillation (KD) is widely used for compressing a teacher model to reduce its inference cost and memory footprint, by training a smaller student model. However, current KD methods for auto-regressive sequence models suffer from distribution mismatch between output sequences seen during training and those generated by the student during inference. To address this issue, we introduce Generalized Knowledge Distillation (GKD). Instead of solely relying on a fixed set of output sequences, GKD trains the student on its self-generated output sequences by leveraging feedback from the teacher on such sequences. Unlike supervised KD approaches, GKD also offers the flexibility to employ alternative loss functions between the student and teacher, which can be useful when the student lacks the expressivity to mimic the teacher's distribution. Furthermore, GKD facilitates the seamless integration of distillation with RL fine-tuning (RLHF). We demonstrate the efficacy of GKD for distilling auto-regressive language models on summarization, translation, and arithmetic reasoning tasks, and task-agnostic distillation for instruction-tuning." — Agarwal et al. (abstract, verbatim)

## The GKD objective — the λ knob (student-data fraction)
λ ∈ [0,1] mixes fixed/off-policy data (from ground truth or the teacher) with on-policy student-generated data. Verbatim from the arXiv HTML body:

"L_GKD(θ) := (1−λ) E_(x,y)∼(X,Y)[ D(p_T ∥ p_S^θ)(y|x) ] + λ E_x∼X[ E_y∼p_S(·|x)[ D(p_T ∥ p_S^θ)(y|x) ] ]"

Three named regimes:
- **λ = 0 → Supervised KD** — only the fixed dataset sequences (the failing baseline).
- **λ = 0.5 → Mixed** — equal blend of fixed and student-generated data.
- **λ = 1 → On-policy** — purely student-generated sequences (the paper's headline setting).

Token-level divergence over a sequence (verbatim):
"D(p_T ∥ p_S^θ)(y|x) := 1/L_y · Σ_{n=1}^{L_y} D( p_T(·|y_<n,x) ∥ p_S^θ(·|y_<n,x) )"

NOTE (thesis extracted, not verbatim on the exact wording): gradients do **not** backpropagate through the student's sampling step — the student generates y, then y is treated as fixed while the per-token divergence is minimized. This is what keeps on-policy GKD a supervised-style loss rather than an RL policy-gradient.

## The generalized divergence — JSD(β) interpolating forward↔reverse KL
Bounded generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence (verbatim):
"D_JSD(β)(P∥Q) = β·D_KL(P ∥ βP+(1−β)Q) + (1−β)·D_KL(Q ∥ βP+(1−β)Q)"

Limiting behavior (verbatim): "lim_{β→0} D_JSD(β)(P∥Q)/β = D_KL(P∥Q)" — so β→0 recovers **forward KL** (mean-covering, mimics full teacher distribution) and β→1 trends toward **reverse KL** (mode-seeking, focuses on teacher-likely samples — good when the student lacks expressivity). Variants tested: JSD(0.1), JSD(0.5), JSD(0.9). Task-specific choice: "JSD (0.1) is used on WMT and forward KL on other tasks" (per-task default).

## Key results — on-policy/mixed beats supervised (fixed-dataset) KD
- **XSum (summarization):** on-policy GKD with JSD(0.9) gave "2.1× relative gains" over baselines averaged across student sizes. Striking data-efficiency result (verbatim): "on-policy GKD on the 5% subsampled dataset, without any ground-truth summaries, outperforms supervised KD and ImitKD with entire training dataset with ground-truth summaries."
- **WMT (translation):** "1.7× relative gains" over baseline approaches.
- **GSM8K (arithmetic reasoning):** "1.9× relative gains" over standard KD methods.

Headline takeaway (thesis extracted, not verbatim): eliminating the train-inference mismatch by distilling on the student's *own* outputs matters more than having more ground-truth data — a small on-policy dataset beats a large off-policy one. GKD also composes with RLHF, since both operate on student-sampled sequences.

## Connections
Baselines it dethrones — supervised/word-level KD (λ=0) and ImitKD (a DAgger-style imitation baseline). This is the reference OPD paper that later "on-policy distillation" write-ups (e.g. Thinking Machines / verifier-graded RL-style distillation) cite as the LLM-specific origin of training a student on its self-generated mistakes.
