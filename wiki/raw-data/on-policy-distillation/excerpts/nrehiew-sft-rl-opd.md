# SFT, RL, and On-Policy Distillation Through a Distributional Lens (nrehiew)

- **Core Insight:** SFT, RL, and on-policy distillation are one family of distribution-matching objectives — SFT is a fixed external target via *forward* KL (mode-covering), RL is targetless reward-direction on *on-policy* samples that "learns the nearest task-solving policy," and OPD is the hybrid: teacher signal like SFT but student-sampled data like RL, minimizing *reverse* KL (mode-seeking).
- **Guideline:** When you need to add capability without forgetting, prefer on-policy data (RL or OPD) over SFT — "on-policy training" is what lets you raise capability without spending your KL budget; use OPD when you have a dense teacher signal and want the on-policy property for free.
- **Source:** https://nrehiew.github.io/blog/sft_rl_opd/ — "SFT, RL, and On-Policy Distillation Through a Distributional Lens" by wh (nrehiew). No publish date shown. Full text fetched; reverse-KL is described verbatim but the post prints only the forward-KL equation explicitly.
- **Relevant chapters:** distillation-theory (forward/reverse KL), on-policy-distillation, RL-vs-SFT trade-offs, per-token objectives, post-training pipelines

## The three objectives as one family

**SFT = fixed external target, forward KL, mode-covering.** "SFT via Cross Entropy on a fixed dataset is equivalent to minimizing forward KL divergence, up to constants," with `D_KL(p || q_θ) = Σ_x p(x) log [ p(x) / q_θ(x) ]`. It "pulls the model toward the dataset distribution" and "is a very direct pull toward an external target with little regard for the starting policy" — i.e. **no anti-forgetting regularization**. Because forward KL is mode-covering, "The mode-covering behavior of forward KL could thus lead to sacrificing previous modes (representing pre-existing capabilities) to learn the new task."

**RL = no external target, on-policy reward direction, nearest task-solving policy.** In RL "there isn't an arbitrary external distribution that the model is being pulled towards"; instead "RL learns the nearest task-solving policy." The on-policy mechanism is what makes it "nearest": "because we are training on on-policy samples generated by the current policy, this optimal distribution is the closest among all optimal policies to our current policy." Framed as reverse-KL minimization (attributed to Chen et al., 2025).

**OPD = hybrid (SFT teacher + RL sampling), reverse KL, mode-seeking.** "Like SFT, it has a teacher signal...but like RL, the data comes from the student." The update: "the gradient is pulling the model towards matching the teacher's distribution via reverse KL divergence" — giving the mode-seeking behavior of reverse KL (unlike forward-KL SFT).

## Per-token KL → per-token clipping

"style tokens have significantly higher per-token KL divergence while Math tokens have the lowest KL divergence" — i.e. discourse/style tokens (e.g. "wait", "alright") carry most of the teacher–student divergence, while the task-critical tokens are already well-matched. This motivates **per-token clipping** to prevent the loss from being dominated by (and the model collapsing toward) high-KL style tokens.

## Minimal-code-editing experiment — students beat teachers

Task: "The model is provided with a buggy corrupted function and asked to fix the bug and nothing else." Grading checks (1) whether the fix works and (2) whether unrelated parts were rewritten vs. the original. Result: "both OPD students slightly outperformed the RL teacher and significantly outperformed the SFT teacher." Reported Pass@1: **SFT teacher 0.775, RL teacher 0.792, OPD(SFT-teacher) student 0.800, OPD(RL-teacher) student 0.787.** Takeaway quote: "the source of the data (ie. via on-policy sampling) matters a lot while the teacher matters perhaps less than I expected."

## Entropy collapse

Comparing OPD vs RL training curves: "the reward increase is a lot more sudden and corresponds with a drastic collapse in entropy." (Connects to the entropy-collapse discussion in [[tm-on-policy-distillation]].)

## Pipeline framing & domain finding

Modern post-training stack: **Pretrain → SFT → RL/Expert → OPD Merging**, cited in production recipes (GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4) where per-domain expert/RL models are merged back via distillation. Domain split: "Math and Code tasks tend to favor RL" while creative and knowledge domains "benefit more from self-distillation or distillation-style methods."
