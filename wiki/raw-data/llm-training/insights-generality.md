# Insights — Generality measurement and benchmark overfitting

- A single benchmark number is one draw from a distribution, not a property of the model. Re-instantiating the same 100 GSM8K test questions from symbolic templates gives Gemma2-9B a worst-to-best gap above 12% across 50 sets ([[gsm-symbolic]]). On GSM1k, 1,205 newly written grade-school problems matched to GSM8k in difficulty, the weakest models score up to 8 percentage points lower than on GSM8k ([[gsm1k]]). **Replicated** across two independent constructions.
- Reformatting a benchmark to reduce guessing and prompt sensitivity lowers reported scores. Rebuilding MMLU with reasoning-weighted questions and ten answer options instead of four lowers accuracy by 16% to 33% ([[mmlu-pro]]). **Result (single study).**
- Training on verifiable constraints transfers weakly to constraints not seen in training: models that score well on IFEval's 25 constraint templates fall below 50% on 58 unseen verifiable constraints for GPT-4.1 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet ([[ifbench]]). Constraint following also degrades as reasoning length grows: the best of 23 reasoning models on MathIF, Qwen3-14B, satisfies all constraints in 50.71% of 420 queries ([[scaling-reasoning-losing-control-mathif]]).
- Some agentic and coding benchmark scores are partly explained by memorized repository content rather than by the ability being measured. Given only a repository name and issue text, ten OpenAI and Anthropic models named a file changed by the gold patch in 60-76% of SWE-Bench Verified instances but in under 53% of 245 held-out instances ([[swe-bench-illusion]]). Applying the Agentic Benchmark Checklist to ten agentic benchmarks found 7 with task-validity flaws, 7 with outcome-validity flaws, and reporting limitations in all 10 ([[agentic-benchmark-checklist]]).
- Measured capability gain depends on the k in pass@k. RLVR-trained models beat their base models at k=1 and are overtaken as k grows; on AIME24 at k=1024 the base model solves more problems than the RLVR model ([[rlvr-beyond-base-model]]). The controlled 100M-parameter study in [[interplay-pretraining-midtraining-rl]] finds GRPO raises pass@128 only on tasks close to what pre-training and mid-training already supported. **Interpretation** shared by both sources: RL redistributes probability mass toward paths the base model can already sample.
- Reported scores carry sampling error that is usually not reported. Treating eval questions as an independent draw from an unseen super-population yields closed-form standard errors, and clustered questions inflate them ([[adding-error-bars-evals]]); a benchmark's signal-to-noise ratio at small scale predicts how well small-model rankings transfer to 1B-scale rankings (R = 0.791 on the DataDecide comparison) ([[signal-and-noise-eval]]).
