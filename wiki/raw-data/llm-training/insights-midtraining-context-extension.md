# Insights — Mid-training, continued pretraining, and context extension

- A short curated stage after the main pretraining run produces large measured gains. Adding a second mid-training stage on Dolmino Mix 1124 raises the average of the development and held-out suites by 10.6 points for OLMo 2 7B and 10.3 points for 13B ([[olmo-2]]); Olmo 3 keeps the same shape with a 5.93T-token pretraining stage followed by a 100B-token mid-training stage ([[olmo-3]]). Mid-training on 347,770,880 tokens of skill packages raises a four-benchmark agentic average by 9.11-24.96 points across 1.6B, 3B, and 7B backbones ([[skill-pretraining]]). **Replicated.**
- Where reasoning data is placed in the pipeline changes the final model. In an 8B hybrid Mamba-Transformer pretrained on 1T tokens, models with reasoning data in pretraining stayed ahead after SFT (35.92 vs 26.62 average) and after GRPO (56.66 vs 37.92) ([[front-loading-reasoning]]); the controlled small-scale study in [[interplay-pretraining-midtraining-rl]] reaches the same ordering. Mid-training on synthetic documents that discuss a Model Spec before alignment fine-tuning lowered the average agentic-misalignment rate from 68% to 5% on Qwen2.5-32B ([[anthropic-model-spec-midtraining]]).
- Context extension is a positional-encoding choice plus a data choice, and the two are measured separately. Rescaling RoPE position indices rather than extrapolating reaches 32,768 tokens within 1000 fine-tuning steps ([[position-interpolation]]); NTK-by-parts interpolation plus an attention temperature extends Llama 2 7B/13B to 128K with 400 + 200 steps ([[yarn]]); with 10B mid-training tokens LongRoPE2 scores 82.03 on RULER at 128k against 73.40 (LongRoPE), 73.19 (NTK), and 49.39 (YaRN) under the same budget ([[longrope2]]). Raising the RoPE base frequency from 10,000 to 500,000 while continually pretraining Llama 2 on 400B tokens at 32,768-token sequences is the same lever used at production scale ([[llama-2-long]], [[long-context-llama3]]).
- Length-upsampled continued pretraining is the data-side lever and it is cheap relative to pretraining. Continual pretraining of LLaMA-2 7B on 5B tokens of per-source length-upsampled SlimPajama packed to 80K reaches 88.0 on Needle-in-a-Haystack at 128K ([[long-context-data-engineering]]); progressive pretraining of Qwen2.5-14B-1M from 32,768 to 262,144 tokens raises RULER at 128K from 37.6 to 87.6 ([[qwen-long-context-synth]]); ProLong uses 40B tokens (20B at 64K, 20B at 512K) followed by 1B tokens of short SFT ([[prolong]]). Building documents by following each meta-chunk with its top-k retrieved similar chunks is an alternative to raw long documents ([[nextlong]]).
- Long-context training measurably costs short-context ability unless it is controlled. Continual pretraining on long text alone keeps 92.5% of Llama-3-8B's original short-text average (51.00 vs 55.16) while LongReD keeps more ([[longred]]); reasoning mid-training on 35B tokens for 4 epochs degraded SmolLM3's RULER scores, and a linear merge of the preference-trained soup with a mid-training checkpoint recovered them ([[smollm-3]]). **Replicated** as a forgetting effect; merging is the reported repair in the second case.
- Attention-window scheduling and position-distribution coverage affect extension quality. At a fixed 100B-token budget, 1B models pretrained with shorter context windows score higher on nine benchmarks, and scheduling the window from short to long recovers both ([[skyladder]]); relative positions near the training length are rarely seen (distances ≥ 1536 are under 5% of position indices at 2048 tokens), which is the measured reason effective context falls short of nominal context ([[string-effective-context]]).
