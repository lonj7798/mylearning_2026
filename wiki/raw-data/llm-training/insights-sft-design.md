# Insights — SFT design, mixtures, forgetting, and merging

- Small curated SFT sets reach high scores when the base model already holds the ability. 1,000 curated prompt-response pairs (~750,000 tokens) on LLaMa-65B produce responses human annotators rate equal or better than much larger pipelines ([[lima]]); 1,000 curated traces plus a decoding rule raise Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on AIME24 from 50.0 to 56.7 ([[s1]]); 800 curated long-CoT traces raise it to 63.3% AIME24 and 95.6% MATH500 against 16.5% and 79.4% ([[limo]]). **Replicated.** The condition is a strong base model and verified traces, not small data in general.
- No single instruction mixture is best on every capability. With one SFT recipe on LLaMA 13B, the Human+GPT mixture is best on average (45.2) but best on only 2 of 6 capability evaluations ([[tulu-1-how-far-can-camels-go]]). The released Tülu 3 mixture is 939,344 samples from 18 component sets, 57% public and 43% generated ([[tulu-3-sft-mix]], [[tulu-3]]); OpenCoder reaches HumanEval 70.1 by training on a diverse stage-1 set and then a code-specific stage-2 set, against 52.4 for stage-2 alone ([[opc-synthetic-code]]).
- Loss masking, packing, and embedding noise are measurable SFT-stage levers, not defaults to accept silently. At sequence length 512 in the Wikipedia BERT corpus, 4.17 billion of 8.33 billion tokens are padding, which packing removes ([[sequence-packing]]); applying the loss to instruction tokens as well as output tokens raises the mean over 18 tasks by up to 2.46 points under the conditions tested ([[loss-masking-prompt]]); scaled uniform embedding noise raised LLaMA-2-7B's AlpacaEval win rate from 29.79% to 64.69% under a GPT-4 judge ([[neftune]]). The AlpacaEval result is judge-dependent and should be read with [[insights-evaluation-and-judges]].
- Fine-tuning removes capabilities as well as adding them, and the loss is measurable. Fine-tuning GPT-3.5 Turbo on 10 harmful pairs for 5 epochs for under $0.20 raised its harmfulness rate on a 330-prompt benchmark from 1.8% to 88.8% ([[finetuning-compromises-safety]]); continual pretraining on long text alone keeps 92.5% of Llama-3-8B's short-text average ([[longred]]); preference post-training eroded SmolLM3's long-context scores ([[smollm-3]]); fine-tuning on benign web-navigation demonstrations raised WebDojo attack success from 32.88% to 64.38% and lowered refusal from 26.03% to 6.85% ([[agentic-finetuning-misalignment]]). **Replicated** across four settings.
- Weight merging is the repair that several reports use for these losses, and it has its own evidence. A linear merge of the preference-trained soup with a mid-training checkpoint restored SmolLM3's base RULER score up to 128K ([[smollm-3]]); trimming each task vector to its top-20% magnitudes and resolving sign conflicts beats the strongest prior merging baseline by about 2 points ([[ties-merging]]); SLERP-merging a general-only and a safety-only DPO model scores 78.0% Dolly-200 win rate and −57.8% relative harm against 71.0% and −54.7% for the data-mixed alternative ([[mix-data-or-merge-models]]); weight averaging across a run or across runs is at least as accurate as the best single point in the cases studied ([[early-stopping-and-checkpointing]], [[warp]]).
- Data selection for SFT is a scoring problem with several validated scores: complexity-quality-diversity ([[deita]]), instruction-following difficulty ([[ifd]], [[cherry-llm]], [[superfiltering]]), Adam-aware influence ([[less]]), tag coverage ([[instag]]), and gradient-entropy diversity ([[prismatic-synthesis]]). Reported correlations with post-training accuracy reach Spearman ρ ≈ 0.90 for gradient-entropy diversity over 300+ runs.
