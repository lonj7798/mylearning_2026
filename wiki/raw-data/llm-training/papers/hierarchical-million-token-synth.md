# Scaling Instruction-Tuned LLMs to Million-Token Contexts via Hierarchical Synthetic Data Generation
- **Core Insight:** Fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct stepwise from 180K to 1M tokens on multi-book QA dialogues generated by Qwen-2-72B-Instruct from 4K/12K-token chunks gives 62.95% RULER accuracy at 1M tokens versus 48.81% for zero-shot RoPE scaling of the base, with MMLU 65.08 versus 68.21 for the base (App. D Tables 8-9; Table 3).
- **Guideline:** When long-context SFT data must exceed the length of available documents, concatenate documents into one multi-turn dialogue with per-document hierarchical and diverse QA plus questions that revisit earlier documents, generated by a short-context model on chunks, because the fixed hierarchical + diverse composition at 180K scored 59.45 on InfiniteBench versus 51.31 for the base (Table 7) and 7B/8B generators gave 57.22-58.65 at 180K (Table 4); also track short-context benchmarks, because the 1M model loses 3.13 MMLU points and 1.69 LongBench points (Tables 2-3).
- **Authors:** Linda He, Jue Wang, Maurice Weber, Shang Zhu, Ben Athiwaratkun, Ce Zhang (Harvard University, Together AI, University of Chicago)
- **Year:** 2025 (arXiv v1 2025-04)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12637
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** long-context SFT, synthetic QA generation, multi-document concatenation, RoPE scaling, 1M-token context, short-context retention, multi-turn long dialogues

## Abstract
Long-context reasoning is limited by quadratic attention cost and by the scarcity of annotated long-context data; the authors state that no open instruction-tuning dataset exceeds 100K tokens. They propose a post-training synthetic data strategy that extends the context window while preserving general task performance and is not limited by the length of real documents. With step-by-step RoPE scaling, their model reaches a 1M-token context, performs well on RULER and InfiniteBench, and keeps performance on general language tasks (Abstract, §1).

## Key Contributions
- Hierarchical question ordering over global summary, 12K-token sections and 4K-token chunks, so questions move from broad to detailed (§3.1, Figure 2, Algorithm 1).
- A pool of diverse, multi-hop and specific-detail question prompts applied to chunks that a short-context model can read (§3.1, App. B).
- Multi-document integration: books are concatenated and QA about earlier books is inserted later, which sets context length independently of document length (§3.2, Algorithm 2).
- Stepwise training to 180K, 350K, 650K and 1M, with ablations at 100K and 180K and a generator-size check (§4).

## Technical Details
- **Source documents:** Together Long-Data-Collections books, 7,772 books; the training data total about 1.4B tokens (§4.1, footnote 1). Derived check: 2,000 × 180K + 1,280 × 350K + 600 × 650K + 200 × 1M = 1.398B tokens.
- **Chunking and summaries:** documents are split into 12K-token sections and 4K-token chunks, and summaries are produced at each level with the prompt "Summarize the following text concisely in no more than {word_limit} words" (§3.1, App. B); the word limit is not reported.
- **Order-following selection (Algorithm 1):** the first QA uses the global summary; the next uses a random medium chunk; then the first small chunk of that medium chunk; afterwards, with probability 1/3 each, deeper content of the same small chunk, the next small chunk, or the next medium chunk (App. A).
- **Diverse question prompts:** temporal, character-based, multi-aspect complex, themes, comparison, cause and effect, hypothetical, interpretation, detail-oriented, and perspectives; a specific-detail prompt with exact answers (multiple choice allowed); and a multi-hop prompt over three chunks, described in §3.1 as spanning 2-4 chunks (App. B). Outputs are JSON question-answer pairs.
- **Per-book volume:** 25 hierarchical and 50 diverse questions per book, 582,900 QA pairs in total, generated through the Together AI inference API with 32 simultaneous requests in about two days (§4.1).
- **Multi-document assembly (§3.2, Figure 3):** after each document, N1 = 5 hierarchical and N1 = 5 diverse QA pairs; then N2 = 9 diverse QA pairs sampled from earlier documents; for each earlier document, with 60% probability, N3 = 3 hierarchical follow-up questions. §4.2 prints the same numbers with the N2 and N3 labels swapped. Each book enters a user turn followed by "Please give me a summary of the book" (App. C).
- **Loss:** computed on answers only; questions and context are masked (§4.1).
- **Evaluation:** InfiniteBench first 150 samples per task, coding tasks excluded; LongBench without coding tasks; RULER with 130 tasks per length across 13 categories (over 150M tokens); MMLU (§4.1).
- **RULER accuracy (App. D):** 1M model: 62.95 at 1M, 76.88 at 524,288, 82.05 at 131,072, 86.19 at 32,768, 95.59 at 4,096 (Table 8). 650K model: 57.24 at 650K. 350K model: 71.15 at 350K (Table 8). Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: 83.06 at 131,072, 30.34 at 262,144, 28.93 at 524,288, 88.44 at 32,768 (Table 9a; §4.2 describes input truncation above 128K). Zero-shot RoPE scaling to 1M: 48.81 at 1M, 62.53 at 524,288, 72.68 at 131,072 (Table 9b).
- **InfiniteBench average (100K):** base 51.31, Gradient-1048k 41.04, 180K 59.45, 350K 59.26, 650K 56.51, 1M 54.80 (Table 1). Retrieve.KV: 42.66, 15.60, 88.66, 92.00, 63.33, 57.33 in the same order (Table 1). The authors believe the 650K and 1M models are under-trained because of multi-node training time and NCCL overhead (footnote 3) (Interpretation).
- **Generator robustness (180K / 350K / 650K):** InfiniteBench with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as generator 57.22 / 57.48 / 52.94, with Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct 58.65 / 57.49 / 54.38 (Table 4). Table 4 lists Gradient-1048k at 42.19, while Table 1 lists 41.04.
- **180K composition ablation (InfiniteBench):** hs-hs-hs-fixed 59.45; h-h-s-fixed 58.58; hs-hs-hs-randomized 58.51; h-h-h-randomized 58.14; h-h-s-randomized 58.10; h-h-s-fixed-no-sum 58.03; h-h-randomized 56.63 (Table 7). Fixed = 6 main + 4 follow-up questions; randomized = 2-10 follow-ups (§4.4). The §4.4 text compares "hs-hs-fixed-no-sum" (58.03) with "hs-hs-hs-fixed (58.58)"; in Table 7, 58.58 belongs to h-h-s-fixed. Every hs-hs-hs-fixed value in Table 7 equals the 180K column of Table 1, although §4.2 gives 2,000 samples for the 180K stage and §4.4 gives 300-600 samples per ablation run.
- **100K single-document ablation (InfiniteBench, App. E text):** 4 hierarchies with multi-hop (20% chance a multi-hop QA follows each new QA) 54.70; diverse questions 52.41; 4 hierarchies 52.08; 4 hierarchies without order 50.69; base 51.31. The text states that both hierarchical models outperform the base, which the 50.69 value does not support. LongBench "All" differs by at most 0.56 points across configurations (48.11-48.67, Table 10).

## Findings relevant to generality, long context, distillation
- **Short-context retention:** MMLU 68.21 (base) vs 66.29 (350K), 65.80 (650K), 65.08 (1M); Gradient-1048k 60.48 (Table 3). LongBench 48.11 vs 47.34 (350K), 46.18 (650K), 46.42 (1M); Gradient-1048k 35.89 (Table 2). Table 3 prints identical MMLU subcategory scores for the 650K and 1M models. The authors attribute summarization drops to trade-offs of long-context training (§4.2) (Interpretation).
- **Below 128K:** the extended models are comparable to the base on RULER, for example 86.19 (1M model) vs 88.44 at 32,768 (Tables 8-9, §4.2).
- **Distillation details:** stage = long-context SFT data; prompt types = order-following, diverse, specific-detail and multi-hop QA plus book summaries; teacher = Qwen-2-72B-Instruct (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in §4.3); quality control = no filtering or verification of generated QA is described (§3, §4.1, App. A-B); the generator reads only chunks and summaries, not the full context (§3.1).
- **Long multi-turn format:** each training sample is one user/assistant conversation over several books, with QA about earlier books inserted after later books (App. C, Algorithm 2).
