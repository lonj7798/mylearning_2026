# Nash Learning from Human Feedback (Nash-LM / Nash-MD)
- **Core Insight:** RLHF objectives that pick the argmax of a learned reward (PPO, DPO) converge to a *single* preferred response; modeling alignment as finding the Nash equilibrium of a preference game instead yields a *stochastic* policy that is robust to non-transitive preferences — which is exactly what real human preference data is.
- **Guideline:** When preferences are intransitive (rock-paper-scissors on writing style, coding style, etc.), train Nash-MD (mirror-descent on the preference game) instead of DPO; the solution is a policy distribution, not a single mode.
- **Authors:** Rémi Munos, Michal Valko, Daniele Calandriello, Mohammad Gheshlaghi Azar, Mark Rowland, Daniel Guo, Yunhao Tang, Matthieu Geist, Thomas Mesnard, Andrea Michi, Marco Selvi, Sertan Girgin, Nikola Momchev, Olivier Bachem, Daniel J. Mankowitz, Doina Precup, Bilal Piot
- **Year:** 2024 (DeepMind)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00886
- **Relevant topics:** preference game theory, Nash equilibrium, self-play, intransitive preferences

## Abstract
Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has emerged as the main paradigm for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. Typically, RLHF involves the initial step of learning a reward model from human feedback, often expressed as preferences between pairs of text generations produced by a pre-trained LLM. Subsequently, the LLM's policy is fine-tuned by optimizing it to maximize the reward model through a reinforcement learning algorithm. However, an inherent limitation of current reward models is their inability to fully represent the richness of human preferences and their dependency on the sampling distribution. In this study, we introduce an alternative pipeline for the fine-tuning of LLMs using pairwise human feedback. Our approach entails the initial learning of a preference model, which is conditioned on two inputs given a prompt, followed by the pursuit of a policy that consistently generates responses preferred over those generated by any competing policy, thus defining the Nash equilibrium of this preference model. We term this approach Nash learning from human feedback (NLHF).

## Key Contributions
- Reframes LLM alignment as finding the **Nash equilibrium** of a two-player symmetric preference game: the equilibrium policy π* satisfies `P(π* ≻ π) ≥ 1/2` for all competing π.
- Introduces **Nash-MD** (Nash via Mirror Descent): alternate between self-play sampling and a soft-policy update that regularizes toward π_ref via KL.
- Proves convergence under mild regularity even with non-transitive preference models (where argmax-of-reward fails).
- Provides a practical token-level approximation (Nash-MD-PG) that trains via on-policy REINFORCE with a learned preference model as the opponent.

## Technical Details
- **Preference model:** `p(y > y' | x)` trained from pairwise human labels — can be non-transitive (unlike Bradley-Terry).
- **Nash objective:** find π such that `max_π E_{y ~ π, y' ~ π_opp} p(y > y' | x) = 1/2`.
- **Nash-MD update:** `π_{t+1} ∝ π_ref · exp(η · E_{y' ~ π_t} p(· > y' | x))` — soft-max with temperature η over the one-step advantage against the previous policy.
- **Practical implementation (Nash-MD-PG):** at each step, sample two responses from π_t, score both with the preference model, and run a REINFORCE gradient with the preference margin as reward.
- **Symmetric vs asymmetric:** the paper's equilibrium is symmetric (same policy for both players); Nash-MD-PG implements this by playing against a slow-moving EMA of itself.
- **Regularization:** a KL(π_t || π_ref) term mirrors the DPO/PPO reference constraint.

## Connections
- Shares the self-play structure of [[spin]] and [[self-rewarding-lm]], but the equilibrium concept is different: SPIN converges to the data distribution, Self-Rewarding to the judge's argmax, Nash-MD to a preference-game equilibrium that may be stochastic.
- The Nash-MD-PG variant is what [[trl-online-dpo]] generalizes — swap the judge for a preference model and relax the "chosen/rejected" hard pair into soft Nash-MD updates.
- Directly motivates [[ipo]]'s L2-on-margin loss: both fight the DPO failure where one mode dominates because the reward model is non-transitive or miscalibrated.
- Follow-up work extends to decentralized XPO (eXploratory Preference Optimization) which uses the same equilibrium framing to motivate optimistic exploration.
