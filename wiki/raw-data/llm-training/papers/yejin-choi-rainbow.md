# Symbolic Knowledge Distillation — From GPT-3 to Commonsense Models (Rainbow line)
- **Core Insight:** Critic-filtered knowledge distilled from a frontier LM can produce a student commonsense model that *outperforms* the teacher on the distilled skill — because a small critic model can reject the teacher's hallucinations more aggressively than the teacher rejects its own.
- **Guideline:** When distilling commonsense or reasoning from a large teacher, add a trained critic in the loop: teacher generates → critic filters → student trains. The critic doesn't need to be larger than the student; moderate critic accuracy is enough to beat teacher quality.
- **Authors:** Peter West, Chandra Bhagavatula, Jack Hessel, Jena D. Hwang, Liwei Jiang, Ronan Le Bras, Ximing Lu, Sean Welleck, Yejin Choi
- **Year:** 2022 (Allen AI + University of Washington)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07178
- **Relevant topics:** knowledge distillation, commonsense reasoning, critic models, ATOMIC, Rainbow

## Abstract
The common practice for training commonsense models has gone from–human–to–corpus–to–machine: humans author commonsense knowledge graphs in order to train commonsense models. In this work, we investigate an alternative, from–machine–to–corpus–to–machine: general language models author these commonsense knowledge graphs to train commonsense models. Our study leads to a new framework, Symbolic Knowledge Distillation. As with prior art in Knowledge Distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), our approach uses larger models to teach smaller models. A key difference is that we distill knowledge symbolically–as text–in addition to the neural model. We also distill only one aspect – the commonsense of a general language model teacher, allowing the student to be a different type, a commonsense model. Altogether, we show that careful prompt engineering and a separately trained critic model allow us to distill GPT-3 into a commonsense model that outperforms the teacher, exemplified by the student being three times smaller while matching the teacher's score on reasoning benchmarks.

## Key Contributions
- Introduced **Symbolic Knowledge Distillation (SKD)** — distill a teacher LM by generating text, filtering through a learned critic, and training the student on the filtered text.
- Built **ATOMIC-10X**: a commonsense KG 10× larger than the human-authored ATOMIC-2020, entirely generated by GPT-3 and critic-filtered.
- Trained **COMET-ATOMIC-10X** — a 1.5B student that outperforms GPT-3 (175B) on commonsense completion accuracy, despite being >100× smaller.
- Showed the *critic* is the crucial component: without it, GPT-3's noisy generations cap student quality below teacher quality.

## Technical Details
- **Teacher:** GPT-3 (davinci, 175B); prompted with 10 hand-crafted examples per relation.
- **Generation:** 6.46M (head, relation, tail) tuples across 23 ATOMIC relations; T=0.9, top-p=1.
- **Critic:** DeBERTa-v3-large fine-tuned on ~20K human-annotated acceptable/unacceptable tuples.
- **Filter threshold:** critic probability > 0.5 → keep; yielded 5.6M accepted tuples (~86% pass rate after prompt tuning).
- **Student:** GPT-2 XL (1.5B), trained with standard autoregressive loss on the filtered KG.
- **Eval:** human pairwise preference on held-out tuples; COMET-10X wins 87% vs GPT-3 teacher.

## Connections
- Precursor to [[self-instruct]] (Wang 2022) — same lab, same "teacher generates, filter, student trains" structure, applied to instructions instead of commonsense tuples.
- The critic-filtering idea anticipates [[orca]] (explanation-trace filtering) and [[let-verify]] (step-level filtering).
- Supports the broader "distillation can beat the teacher" thesis when combined with filtering — a recurring motif in the Yejin Choi lab's work, from Rainbow (2020) through ATOMIC-10X (2022) through Self-Instruct (2022).
- Directly relevant to synthetic-preference work like [[west-of-n]] — both rely on filtering to extract high-quality signal from a noisy generator.
- Establishes the empirical basis for the small-model-wins-on-narrow-task thesis that frames Phi-3, Orca-2, and the Yi-series distillation recipes.
