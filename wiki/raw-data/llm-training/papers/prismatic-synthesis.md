# Prismatic Synthesis: Gradient-based Data Diversification Boosts Generalization in LLM Reasoning
- **Core Insight:** Across more than 300 controlled fine-tuning runs, G-Vendi, the exponentiated entropy of loss gradients from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, reaches Spearman ρ = 0.899 (math) and 0.893 (NLI) with average out-of-distribution (OOD) accuracy, above embedding, n-gram, perplexity, and skill-set measures (§2.3.1, Table 1).
- **Guideline:** When scaling synthetic reasoning data from a fixed generator, cluster samples in gradient space and keep only new samples that land in sparse clusters, because vanilla few-shot and persona-guided generation saturated around 50K–100K samples while this procedure kept improving up to the 1.0M-sample PrismMath set (§3.2.2, Fig. 1 right). The correlation was measured only with data quality held fixed (App. D).
- **Authors:** Jaehun Jung, Seungju Han, Ximing Lu, Skyler Hallinan, David Acuna, Shrimai Prabhumoye, et al. (NVIDIA Research, University of Washington, University of Southern California; last author Yejin Choi)
- **Year:** 2025 (arXiv v1 2025-05; v2 2026-07)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20161
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** data diversity measures, gradient representations, synthetic data scaling, reasoning distillation, OOD generalization

## Abstract
The paper asks which kind of training-data diversity predicts generalization in LLM reasoning. In over 300 training runs controlled for data scale and quality, it introduces G-Vendi, which measures diversity as the entropy of model-induced gradients computed with a small off-the-shelf proxy model. G-Vendi correlates with OOD performance at Spearman ρ ≈ 0.9 on natural language inference (NLI) and math reasoning, and outperforms alternative measures. The authors then present Prismatic Synthesis, which generates synthetic data targeted at underrepresented regions of gradient space. Model performance keeps improving as this data is scaled, on in-distribution and OOD benchmarks. PrismMath-7B, distilled from a 32B model, outperforms R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (same base model, data generated by the 671B R1) on 6 of 7 benchmarks.

## Key Contributions
- G-Vendi: Vendi score of randomly projected, normalized loss gradients under a frozen instruction-tuned proxy (§2.1).
- An evaluation protocol for diversity measures: 300 subsets of fixed size from a 1.5M-sample synthetic pool, one fine-tuned model per subset (§2.2; App. A.1).
- Evidence that diversity can outweigh scale for OOD accuracy, while scale mainly drives in-distribution accuracy (§2.3.1, Fig. 2–3).
- Prismatic Synthesis and two datasets: Nemotron-PrismMath (1.0M problem-solution pairs) and PrismNLI (515K pairs) (§3.1).
- Code and data released with README documentation (NeurIPS checklist item 13).

## Technical Details
- **Gradient feature (Eq. 1–2, §2.1).** g_θ(x, y) = −∇log P(y|x; θ) / ‖−∇log P(y|x; θ)‖, projected as g_proj = Πᵀ g_θ with Π ∈ ℝ^{|θ|×d}, Π_ij ~ U({−1, 1}).
  - x = input, y = output, θ = frozen proxy parameters, d = projected dimension (1024 in all experiments, §2.1).
- **Score (Eq. 3, §2.1).** G-Vendi(D) = exp(−Σ_i λ_i log λ_i), where λ_i are eigenvalues of K = GGᵀ/|D| and G ∈ ℝ^{|D|×d} stacks the projected gradients.
  - G-Vendi equals |D| when all off-diagonal entries of K are 0 and 1 when they are all 1; it is computed through GᵀG in O(d²|D|) (App. C).
- **Proxy.** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct without further training (§2.1). On 10k math subsets, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct gives ρ = 0.909 with OOD performance vs 0.898 for the default; base Qwen2.5-0.5B gives 0.772 (Table 3).
- **Diversity study setup.** Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct 5-shot generates 1.5M samples per task from WANLI (NLI) and GSM8K + MATH (math) seeds; subset sizes 100k/50k/10k (math) and 50k/10k (NLI); students Llama-3.2-1B (math) and DeBERTa-v3-large (NLI); Perf = mean accuracy relative to the same model trained on the full pool, over 7 OOD benchmarks (§2.2, Eq. 5).
- **Baselines (Table 1, math OOD ρ).** Embedding Vendi 0.754 (gte-Qwen-7B-Instruct, 14× the proxy size), Embedding Dissimilarity 0.751, 2-gram Entropy 0.736, Average Perplexity −0.631, Skill-Set Entropy 0.812 (GPT-4 taxonomy, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct tagging) (§2.2–2.3.1; App. A.1).
- **Diversity vs scale.** A 10K subset with higher diversity often outperforms a 100K subset with lower diversity on OOD benchmarks (§2.3.1). Llama-3.1-8B trained on same-sized subsets differs by up to 25.4% OOD accuracy depending on diversity (§2.3.4).
- **What G-Vendi encodes.** At 10k, DiverseReason (10k seeds, 0.1k personas) gets OOD 54.77 and G-Vendi 63.22; DiversePersona (0.1k seeds, 10k personas) gets 38.15 and 51.94, yet a higher Embedding Vendi (24.49 vs 22.03) (Table 2).
- **Prismatic Synthesis loop (§3).** (1) Compute proxy gradients and run K-means. (2) Generate new samples by few-shot prompting from the current pool. (3) Add only generated samples in sparse clusters; §3 gives "the top 20% of clusters with the smallest number of members" as an example. The math-generation paragraph sets k = 1% of the pool size per iteration and keeps samples in the smallest k/2 clusters (§3.1); the NLI paragraph does not restate these values.
- **Filtering (§3.1).** No ground-truth answers are used. Majority vote over N sampled solutions with threshold τ; then 10-gram matching and LLM paraphrase detection against all test benchmarks.
- **Results (Table 4).** PrismMath-7B average 75.25 vs R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 72.80 and OpenThinker2-7B 72.36; AIME24 57.08 vs 54.66. PrismNLI average OOD accuracy 74.28 vs 66.18 for WANLI+MNLI+SNLI (943K samples).

## Findings relevant to generality, distillation
- **Generality.** OOD and in-distribution (ID) accuracy respond differently: ID accuracy on MATH and GSM8K correlates more weakly with G-Vendi (ρ = 0.780) and is dominated by dataset size (§2.3.1, Table 1, Fig. 3 left). Higher G-Vendi also predicts higher OOD accuracy for Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen2.5-0.5B students (§2.3.4).
- **Distillation.** A 32B teacher with majority-vote filtering and no ground truth produced a 7B student above baselines distilled from R1-671B with ground-truth verification on 6 of 7 benchmarks (§3.2.1, Table 4). The authors conclude that diversification can matter more than generator strength in this setting (§3.2.1).
- **Limits (App. D).** The correlation appears only under controlled data quality, so G-Vendi should not be used alone to compare datasets from different generation processes. Aggregating many benchmarks may hide effects on a new domain with few benchmarks.
