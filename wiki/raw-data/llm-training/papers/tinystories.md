# TinyStories: How Small Can Language Models Be and Still Speak Coherent English?
- **Core Insight:** Restricting a synthetic corpus to the vocabulary of a 3–4-year-old (about 1,500 words) while forcing diversity with one random verb, noun and adjective plus random story features per generation lets models below 10M parameters, or with a single transformer block, produce multi-paragraph stories with near-perfect grammar and some reasoning, which 125M models trained on web corpora rarely do (Abstract; §1; §2; Fig. 2).
- **Guideline:** When generating synthetic pretraining data with an LLM, do not rely on sampling temperature for diversity; inject explicit random constraints (sampled words and features) into each generation prompt, and check novelty of trained-model outputs with n-gram overlap against the training set (§2, §4.4).
- **Authors:** Ronen Eldan, Yuanzhi Li (Microsoft Research)
- **Year:** 2023 (arXiv v1 2023-05-12; v2 2023-05-24)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759 (dataset and models on Hugging Face under the names TinyStories, TinyStoriesInstruct, TinyStories-1M/3M/9M/28M/33M/1Layer/2Layer, per footnotes 1–2, 5)
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** synthetic pretraining data, constrained generation for diversity, small language models, LLM-as-grader evaluation, emergence ordering, memorization checks, width vs depth

## Abstract
Models around 125M parameters, such as GPT-Neo small or GPT-2 small, rarely generate coherent English beyond a few words even after extensive training. The paper asks whether coherent text requires large scale or complex architecture. It introduces TinyStories, synthetic short stories generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 using only words a typical 3–4-year-old understands. Models below 10M parameters, or with one transformer block, trained on it produce fluent, consistent, diverse stories of several paragraphs with almost perfect grammar and show reasoning. It also proposes GPT-4 grading of model completions on grammar, creativity and consistency as an evaluation method.

## Key Contributions
- Dataset construction with random word triples and random story features for diversity (§2).
- TinyStories-Instruct: stories preceded by random subsets of four instruction types (§2.1).
- GPT-Eval: GPT-4 grades completions of ~50 manually written story beginnings (§3).
- Capability ordering by size and depth: grammar first, then consistency, then creativity; depth matters more for consistency and instruction following, width more for factual knowledge (§3.1, §8).
- Diversity and memorization analysis using Rouge-k precision against the training set (§4.4).
- Small-scale scaling-law and attention-head experiments (§6, Figs. 23–24).

## Technical Details
**Generation prompt (§2).** Vocabulary of about 1,500 basic words split into nouns, verbs and adjectives. Each prompt samples one verb, one noun, one adjective, and a random subset of features (e.g., dialogue, plot twist, bad ending, moral value), and asks for a 3–5 paragraph story using only simple words. The authors state that prompting without such constraints, even at high temperature, gives a "very repetitive dataset" (§2).

**TinyStories-Instruct (§2.1).** Instruction types: (1) words to include, (2) a sentence that must appear (any sentence except the first), (3) features from {dialogue, bad ending, moral value, plot twist, foreshadowing, conflict}, (4) a 1–2 line summary generated by GPT-3.5. Each entry uses a random subset.

**GPT-Eval (§3).** ~50 manually prepared beginnings, each cut mid-sentence; 10 completions per prompt at temperature 1; GPT-4 first writes a verbal assessment, then grades grammar, creativity and consistency (and an age-group guess); scores averaged. Instruct evaluation: 50 instruction sets disjoint from training; GPT-4 also scores instruction consistency and "Plot".

**Models (§1 footnote 2; §4).** GPT-Neo architecture, window 256, context length 512; GPT-Neo tokenizer restricted to the top 10K tokens. Sizes about 1M–35M parameters, 1–8 layers; each trainable on one V100 within at most 30 hours. The largest trained model is roughly 80M parameters (§3.1).

**Observations (§3.1).** Grammar plateaus earlier than consistency and creativity; consistency emerges when hidden size grows from 64 to 128; 1-layer models struggle with instruction following, 2 layers suffice to some extent; the ~80M model reaches near-perfect grammar and consistency but stays well below GPT-4 on creativity.

**Diversity test (§4.4).** 100 training stories cut at ~40%; model completions compared with (1) the original ending (Rouge-2 precision), (2) each other (max Rouge-2 F), (3) training-set k-gram frequency, (4) the closest training story (max Rouge-2 precision). Most 4- and 5-grams in generations do not appear in the training data (Fig. 16).

**Heads (Fig. 24, hidden 768).** 2 layers: 2/4/8 heads → eval loss 1.38/1.34/1.33, consistency 7.78/8.16/8.16. 1 layer: 1.58/1.56/1.54, consistency 6.38/6.75/7.02.

## Findings relevant to generality and negative feedback
- **Generality by design is narrow.** The dataset removes breadth (facts, domains, vocabulary) to isolate fluency and basic reasoning; the paper makes no claim of general capability transfer (§1, §8).
- **Out-of-distribution composition.** A model trained on TinyStories-Instruct where "Words" and "Summary" never co-occur can still follow prompts that combine them (§4.3, Fig. 13).
- **Memorization levels.** The paper separates exact memorization, simple template matching and complex template matching, and claims evidence against the first two only; complex template matching is described as almost impossible to quantify (§4.4).
- **Evaluation risk.** All quality scores come from GPT-4 grading with one prompt template; no human-agreement check is reported (§3).
- **Scaling law.** The compute-optimal model size vs FLOPs follows a polynomial trend on few points; the authors say the data may not be conclusive (§6, Fig. 23).
