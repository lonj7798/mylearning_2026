# AlpaGasus: Training A Better Alpaca with Fewer Data
- **Core Insight:** ChatGPT rated each of the 52,002 Alpaca examples for "accuracy" on a 0–5 scale; LLaMA-7B and LLaMA-13B trained on the 9,229 examples scored ≥ 4.5 had more GPT-4-judged wins than losses against Alpaca-52k on all four test sets, and 7B training time fell from 80 to 14 minutes (§2.3, Fig. 5, Table 5).
- **Guideline:** When an instruction dataset was generated by a teacher model without response-level checks, rating each response with a strong LLM and training on the top-rated subset can improve judged instruction following at lower training cost; check per-category retention and knowledge benchmarks afterwards, because in this paper coding examples were removed at a higher rate than average and MMLU fell at both 7B and 13B (§6, Table 2).
- **Authors:** Lichang Chen, Shiyang Li, Jun Yan, Hai Wang, Kalpa Gunaratna, Vikas Yadav, et al. (University of Maryland, Samsung Research America, University of Southern California)
- **Year:** 2023 (arXiv v1 2023-07; ICLR 2024)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08701
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** instruction-data selection, LLM-as-rater filtering, Alpaca, GPT-4-as-judge evaluation, data quality versus quantity

## Abstract
Instruction fine-tuning (IFT) datasets such as Alpaca's 52k examples contain responses that are incorrect or irrelevant to their instructions. The paper proposes an automatic filter: a strong API model (ChatGPT) scores every (instruction, input, response) triplet, and triplets below a threshold are removed. AlpaGasus is LLaMA fine-tuned on the 9k examples that remain from Alpaca, with Alpaca's training script and hyperparameters. GPT-4 as judge and a human study both prefer AlpaGasus to Alpaca. The 13B variant reaches more than 90% of its teacher, text-davinci-003, on the test tasks, and 7B training time falls from 80 to 14 minutes. The authors also apply the filter to other datasets (Dolly, GPT4LLM), another base model (LLaMA-2), and another rater (Claude-2).

## Key Contributions
- A rating prompt (Fig. 3) and a selection rule S = {x ∈ V : G(x, p_G) ≥ τ} (Eq. 1). V is the IFT dataset, x a triplet, G the rating LLM, p_G the prompt, τ the threshold.
- A controlled comparison with identical training scripts: filtered 9k versus full 52k versus randomly sampled 9k, at 7B and 13B (Figs. 5–6).
- A data-size and threshold study: 3k and 6k random subsets of the filtered 9k, and a 39k subset from τ = 4.0 (Figs. 1, 7, 8).
- Transfer checks: human-written Dolly-15k, GPT-4-generated GPT4LLM, LLaMA-2 base, Claude-2 rater, and the "helpfulness" dimension (§5, App. A.2–A.5).
- A per-skill analysis that shows uneven removal rates across categories (§6, App. E, App. H).

## Technical Details
- **Rating prompt.** The system prompt presents the instruction, input, and response. The user prompt asks for a score from 0 to 5 on one named "dimension", as a single line followed by an explanation (Fig. 3). Main experiments use the dimension "accuracy" (§2.3). ChatGPT scores fall on a 0.5-point grid; Claude-2 scores fall on a 1-point grid (App. A.2).
- **Score histogram (Alpaca, 52,002 examples):** <3: 172; 3: 1,550; 3.5: 10,811; 4: 30,240; 4.5: 9,218; 5: 11 (Fig. 4). τ = 4.5 was chosen "according to the score histogram" and keeps 9,229 examples (§2.3), 17.75% of the data (§4.1).
- **Judge protocol.** GPT-4 receives both responses and scores each from 1 to 10 with the Vicuna-blog prompt (§3.3, App. C). Each comparison is run in both orders. Win = two wins, or one win and one tie. Tie = two ties, or one win and one loss. Lose = two losses, or one loss and one tie (§3.3). Generation limit 1,024 tokens; ChatGPT, Claude, and text-davinci-003 are sampled at temperature 0.0 (§3.3).
- **Test sets:** Koala 180, Vicuna 80, WizardLM 218, Self-Instruct 252 prompts (Table 1).
- **9k vs 52k (W/T/L):** 7B — Vicuna 41/15/24, Koala 70/44/66, WizardLM 83/65/70, Self-Instruct 90/86/76; 13B — 42/13/25, 67/49/64, 96/55/67, 88/81/83 (Fig. 5).
- **9k vs random 9k (W/T/L):** 7B — 42/14/24, 80/53/47, 93/72/53, 103/82/67; 13B — 38/13/29, 59/63/58, 88/74/56, 83/92/77 (Fig. 6).
- **τ = 4.0 (39k) vs 52k, 7B (W/T/L):** Vicuna 31/19/30, Koala 59/51/70, WizardLM 77/74/67, Self-Instruct 74/101/77 (Fig. 7). The text says 39k outperforms 52k on Koala and WizardLM (§4.2); the Koala bars show more losses (70) than wins (59).
- **Data size:** filtered 9k has more wins than losses against its random 6k and 3k subsets on all four sets (Fig. 8). About 6k filtered examples match Alpaca-52k (§4.2, Fig. 1).
- **Human study:** 3 participants, 40 prompts per test set (160), AlpaGasus-13B vs Alpaca-13B, majority vote: 63 wins, 64 ties, 33 losses (§4.3). The per-set bars in Fig. 29 sum to 79/44/37, which does not match §4.3.
- **Comparison with API models (13B):** 90.1% "capacity" of text-davinci-003, 81.2% of Claude, 78.4% of ChatGPT (Fig. 9). The formula for "capacity" is not stated.
- **Benchmarks (Table 2; MMLU 5-shot, BBH 3-shot, DROP 3-shot, HumanEval 0-shot, App. B.2).** Columns: 7B 9k-random / 7B 9k / 7B 52k / 13B 9k-random / 13B 9k / 13B 52k. - **Dolly-15k:** τ = 4.5 keeps 2,996 examples (§5). 7B 3k vs 15k (W/T/L): Vicuna 40/11/29, Koala 73/42/65, WizardLM 98/46/74, Self-Instruct 107/57/88 (Fig. 10).
- **Other raters and data:** Claude-2 rater, 8k subset, 7B beats 52k on 3 of 4 sets (WizardLM 69/71/78) (App. A.2, Fig. 14). Helpfulness dimension, 2k subset, beats 52k on 3 of 4 sets (WizardLM 74/74/84) (App. A.5, Fig. 19). GPT4LLM: τ = 4.5 keeps 13,721 examples; 13k vs 52k: Vicuna 35/30/15, Koala 64/44/72, WizardLM 72/80/66, Self-Instruct 84/70/98 (App. A.4, Fig. 17). - **Cost:** 7B 80 min → 14 min ($27.31 → $4.78); 13B 5.5 h → 1 h ($225.28 → $40.96), estimated from the AWS p4de.24xlarge price of $40.96/h with 8-GPU time assumed to be half of 4-GPU time (§7, Table 5, fn. 13).

## Findings relevant to generality, negative feedback, distillation
- **Narrowing by category (Result, single study).** Examples containing the keywords Java, C++, C#, or Python fell from 718 to 85, a removal rate of 88.16% against 82.25% overall. AlpaGasus-7B was better or equal on 22 of 29 WizardLM skills and not better on 7, including coding. The authors conclude that training data should stay "diverse and balanced across different categories" (§6, App. E, App. H.1).
- **Knowledge benchmark (Result, single study).** MMLU fell from 40.86 to 38.78 (7B) and from 47.89 to 46.12 (13B) with 9k filtered data instead of 52k; BBH, DROP, and HumanEval rose by 0.12–0.75 points (Table 2).
- **Negatives.** Low-rated examples are discarded (negative marginal value); none are used as content, conditioning, or gradient. Evidence that they lower quality: random 9k loses to filtered 9k (Fig. 6), and the authors report the 39k model as comparable to 52k but worse than 9k (§4.2).
- **Distillation.** Alpaca responses come from text-davinci-003; filtered GPT-4 responses (GPT4LLM, 13k) had more losses than wins against the full 52k on Koala and Self-Instruct (Fig. 17).
- **Scope limits.** Only 7B and 13B were tested (App. K). Evaluation relies mainly on GPT-4 judgment; position bias is controlled by order swapping (§3.3).
