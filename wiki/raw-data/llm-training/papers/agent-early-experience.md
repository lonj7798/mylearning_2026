# Agent Learning via Early Experience
- **Core Insight:** Training on next states produced by the agent's own non-expert actions improved over imitation learning in all 48 model-environment-method cells of Table 1 (8 environments × Llama-3.2-3B / Qwen-2.5-7B / Llama-3.1-8B × two methods) under the same step budget, for example WebShop success with Llama-3.2-3B rose from 41.8 (imitation) to 60.2 with implicit world modeling and 52.7 with self-reflection (§5.2, Table 1).
- **Guideline:** When expert demonstrations exist but the environment gives no verifiable reward, execute a few alternative actions at each expert state and train on the resulting next states (implicit world modeling, then imitation) or on rationales grounded in those states (self-reflection) within the imitation step budget, because this beat imitation in-domain (Table 1), on out-of-domain splits (Table 2), and as a GRPO starting point (Fig. 3); use K = 2-4 alternatives for self-reflection and larger K for world modeling (§6.2).
- **Authors:** Kai Zhang, Xiangchao Chen, Bo Liu, Tianci Xue, Zeyi Liao, Zhihan Liu, et al. (Meta Superintelligence Labs; The Ohio State University; FAIR at Meta)
- **Year:** 2025 (arXiv v1 2025-10; ICML 2026, PMLR 306)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08558
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** agent SFT, imitation learning, world modeling as auxiliary loss, self-reflection rationales, reward-free supervision, SFT-to-RL warm start, out-of-domain generalization

## Abstract
Language agents are mostly trained by supervised fine-tuning on expert trajectories, because many environments have no verifiable reward (websites) or need long rollouts (multi-turn tool use). Expert data cover a narrow set of states and are hard to scale. The paper proposes "early experience": interaction data produced by the agent's own actions, where the resulting future states act as supervision without any reward. Two strategies use this data: implicit world modeling, which trains the policy to predict next states, and self-reflection, which trains the policy on explanations of why the expert action is better than its own alternatives. Across eight environments and multiple model families both improve task success and out-of-domain generalization, and in environments with verifiable rewards they give a stronger starting point for reinforcement learning.

## Key Contributions
- Defines the early-experience setting: for each expert state s_i, sample K alternative actions from the initial policy, execute them, and store (s_i, a_i^j, s_i^j) as D_rollout (§4.1, Eq. 2).
- Implicit world modeling (IWM): next-state prediction with the policy's own parameters, then imitation (§4.2, Eq. 3).
- Self-reflection (SR): rationale + expert action as the target, with rationales generated from observed outcomes of expert and alternative actions (§4.3, Eq. 4; prompt in App. B.1).
- Evaluation on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, TravelPlanner, BFCLv3, Tau-Bench (retail), SearchQA, WebShop, WebArena-Lite, plus out-of-domain splits and a GRPO stage (§5).
- Ablations of demonstration amount, branching factor K, model scale up to 70B/72B, and comparisons with Long CoT, STaR-style rationales, and DPO (§6).

## Technical Details
- **Imitation loss:** L_IL(θ) = −Σ_i log π_θ(a_i | s_i) over D_expert = {(s_i, a_i)} (§3.1, Eq. 1). The paper names distribution shift and no observation of non-expert action outcomes as its limits (§3.1).
- **IWM loss:** L_IWM = −Σ log p_θ(s_i^j | s_i, a_i^j) over D_rollout; s_i^j is the next state after alternative action a_i^j; p_θ is the same model as the policy (§4.2, Eq. 3). Expert transitions (s_i, a_i, s_{i+1}) are included; stage 2 fine-tunes on D_expert with L_IL (§4.2). D_rollout "can be up to an order of magnitude larger" than D_expert (§4.2).
- **SR loss:** L_SR = −Σ log p_θ(c_i^j, a_i | s_i), where c_i^j is a chain-of-thought generated by the same model explaining why a_i is preferable to a_i^j given s_{i+1} and s_i^j; SR data are mixed with D_expert (§4.3, Eq. 4).
- **Budget control:** imitation steps are chosen per environment by lowest training loss and validation performance, then reused unchanged; IWM runs one epoch of the world-model objective and continues supervised updates up to that budget; SR trains the same number of epochs as imitation; at most 8 H100 GPUs (App. B.2).
- **Expert data sources:** provided optimal trajectories (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld); successful but possibly suboptimal human or model trajectories (WebShop, WebArena); LLM-assisted synthesis on training splits (SearchQA, TravelPlanner, BFCLv3, Tau-Bench) (App. B.2).
- **Next-state representation:** raw observations (ALFWorld), offline textual summaries of the next page (WebShop, avg. 345 characters; App. C.2), model-written summaries of retrieved documents (SearchQA; App. C.5) and of accessibility-tree observations (WebArena; App. C.8).
- **Selected results (Table 1):** TravelPlanner SR gains +12.8 to +15.0; ScienceWorld SR with Llama-3.1-8B 54.7 → 68.0; WebShop IWM +4.6 (Qwen-2.5-7B) to +18.4 (Llama-3.2-3B), while the §5.2 prose gives "+11.3 to +18.4"; smallest gains on SearchQA (+0.6 to +3.3 F1) and WebArena-Lite (4.9 → 8.5 for Llama-3.1-8B) (§5.2).
- **SearchQA reporting note:** the Table 1 SearchQA values equal the Musique column of App. Table 7, while Fig. 3(c) uses the "All" column (App. Table 7).

## Findings relevant to generality, negative feedback, agentic training, distillation
- **Out-of-domain (Table 2):** ALFWorld OOD with Llama-3.1-8B: imitation 63.3, IWM 78.1 (+14.8), SR 72.7 (+9.4). BFCLv3 OOD (average of missing function, missing argument, long context) with Llama-3.2-3B: imitation 5.3, SR 13.8 (+8.5). SearchQA OOD with Qwen-2.5-7B: 47.0 → 51.2 (SR) (§5.3). The authors report that OOD gains meet or exceed in-domain gains on ALFWorld and SearchQA. Result (single study).
- **RL warm start (Fig. 3, App. Tables 4-5):** with identical GRPO settings, WebShop success for Llama-3.2-3B after RL is 82.0 from imitation, 92.2 from IWM, 89.8 from SR; ALFWorld 92.2 / 97.7 / 99.2. GRPO from the instruction-tuned model without SFT is lowest (ALFWorld 3B: 78.9; WebShop 3B: 3.9) and the authors describe its training as unstable (§5.4).
- **Less expert data (Fig. 4a, Llama-3.1-8B):** with 1/8 of WebShop demonstrations and 1/2 of ALFWorld demonstrations, early experience exceeds imitation trained on the full set (§6.1).
- **Branching factor (Fig. 4b):** IWM improves as K grows; SR has diminishing returns at large K because some alternatives also lead to success and the model must compare many outcomes in one context (§6.2).
- **Negative actions as gradient vs as content (Table 3, Llama-3.1-8B):** DPO with expert actions as chosen and D_rollout actions as rejected reaches 53.1 (WebShop) and 82.8 (ALFWorld), below IWM (58.6, 85.9); DPO "collapses within tens of optimization steps" on both, and the reported numbers are the best pre-collapse checkpoints (§6.3). IWM and SR place non-expert actions and their outcomes in the input or target of ordinary next-token loss (§4.2-4.3). STaR-style rationales that ignore alternatives and outcomes lower performance (WebShop 47.3 → 25.0; ALFWorld 80.5 → 74.2) (Table 3).
- **Rationale quality control:** WebShop keeps only trajectories completed in fewer than 15 steps (6,235 examples); BFCLv3 drops reflections whose concluded action differs from the expert action (1,200 kept); WebArena drops explanations supporting a non-expert action (3,190 kept); TravelPlanner applies no additional filter (App. C.2, C.3, C.7, C.8).
- **Scale (§6.4, App. Table 9):** WebArena-Lite with LoRA at 70B/72B: Llama-3.3-70B imitation 13.3, IWM 16.4, SR 15.2; Qwen-2.5-72B 12.7, 17.6, 15.8.
