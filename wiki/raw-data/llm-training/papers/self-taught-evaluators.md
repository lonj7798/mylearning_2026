# Self-Taught Evaluators
- **Core Insight:** Starting from Llama-3-70B-Instruct with no human preference labels, five iterations of fine-tuning on the model's own filtered judgments over synthetic pairs raise RewardBench overall accuracy from 75.4 to 88.3 (88.7 with 32-sample majority voting), above GPT-4-0125 (84.3) and above the same seed trained as a judge on human-labeled HelpSteer2 (85.6) (Abstract; Table 1).
- **Guideline:** When human preference labels are unavailable and a capable 70B-class instruction-tuned seed exists, build rejected responses by answering a modified instruction instead of asking a model to write a worse answer, because the modified-instruction construction scored 83.8 vs 80.7 on RewardBench (§6.2); smaller seed models were not tested (Limitations).

- **Relevant topics:** LLM-as-a-Judge, generative reward model, synthetic preference pairs, hard negatives, rejection sampling, iterative self-training, position bias

## Abstract
Evaluators are used as reward models in training and as a substitute for human evaluation, and are usually trained on human preference judgments that are costly and become outdated as models improve. The paper trains an evaluator without human annotations. From unlabeled instructions, an iterative scheme generates contrasting model outputs, trains an LLM-as-a-Judge to produce reasoning traces and final verdicts, and repeats with the improved model's predictions. Without labeled preference data, Llama3-70B-Instruct improves from 75.4 to 88.3 on RewardBench (88.7 with majority vote), outperforming judges such as GPT-4 and matching top reward models trained with labeled examples (Abstract).

## Key Contributions
- Pipeline: initialization, instruction selection, response-pair construction, then iterated judgment annotation and fine-tuning (§3, Fig. 1).

## Technical Details
- Judge input: instruction x, responses y(A) and y(B), and the evaluation prompt; the judge writes a reasoning chain, then a verdict A or B (§3). The winner's position is randomized to address position bias (§3.3).
- Pair construction: an instruction-following LLM writes a baseline response y_w to x; it then writes a modified instruction x′ that is "highly relevant but not semantically identical" to x, and a high-quality response y_l to x′; y_l "would not be a good response for x", giving the pair y_w ≻ y_l (§3.3, Fig. 2). §3 describes y_l as "likely of lower quality".
- Instructions: WildChat, categorized by Mixtral 22Bx8 Instruct with a 15-category prompt (Fig. 7); 20,582 "reasoning" examples are kept; y_w and y_l are generated by Mixtral 22Bx8 Instruct (§4.1).
- Judgment annotation (rejection sampling): sample N = 15 judgments per example from model M_{i−1}; remove those whose verdict disagrees with the construction label; keep one correct judgment chosen at random; discard the example if none is correct; balance A-better and B-better labels (§3.4, §4.1). Judgments for the first iteration come from Mixtral 22Bx8 Instruct (§4.1).
- Fine-tuning: each iteration starts again from Llama3-70B-Instruct; negative log-likelihood loss is applied only to the judgment tokens; fairseq2 for training, vLLM for inference; checkpoints chosen by pairwise accuracy and position bias on a held-out set (§3.5, §4.1). The training set is expected to grow as the model finds more correct judgments; per-iteration sizes are not reported (§3).
- RewardBench overall by iteration: 83.9, 86.0, 87.5, 87.7, 88.3; majority vote with 32 samples 88.7. Seed → iteration 5: Chat 97.6 → 96.6, Chat Hard 58.9 → 84.2, Safety 69.2 → 91.5, Reasoning 78.5 → 81.0 (Table 1).
- Baselines (Table 1): HelpSteer2-trained Llama-3-70B judge 85.6; nvidia Llama3-70B classifier RM on HelpSteer2 88.8; Gemini 1.5 Pro 0514 88.1; Llama3.1-405B-Instruct 83.7; Llama3.1-70B-Instruct 82.2.
- MT-Bench agreement with humans on non-tie pairs: seed 77.8, iteration 5 78.9, majority vote 79.5, GPT4-0125 79.1 (Table 2).
- HelpSteer2 validation, average accuracy over two orders and three seeds: seed 65.5, iteration 4 71.4, iteration 5 71.0; position-consistent accuracy 56.5 → 60.6 (Table 3).
- Order effects at iteration 5: winner always first 85.5, loser always first 91.1, average 88.3; random-order seeds 88.3-88.9 (App. A.3, Table 9).
- Data sources (Table 4): hh_rlhf safety 79.6, GSM8K math 79.3, WildChat coding 79.4, WildChat reasoning 83.5; sources improve the RewardBench categories related to their distribution (§6.1).
- Direct "rewrite the answer to be worse" prompt (Fig. 10): 80.7 vs 83.8 for the proposed construction (§6.2).
- Iterative training on labeled HelpSteer2: 85.6, 86.3, 87.0, 87.0 (Table 5). Starting from a HelpSteer2-fine-tuned judge: 87.0, 86.6 (Table 12). Synthetic:HelpSteer2 mixing ratios from 1:0 to 5:1 give overall 0.833-0.858 (Table 6).
- Generator and judge choice: Mixtral judging Mixtral responses 83.9; Llama3.0 judging Llama3.0 81.4; Llama3.0 judging Mixtral 80.0 (App. A.4, Table 10).

## Findings relevant to generality and negative feedback
- Negatives here are "negative as content" (term 2 of the course negative-feedback standard): y_l is placed in the judge's input and the judge is trained with cross-entropy on its judgment; incorrect sampled judgments are discarded, not pushed down (§3.4, §4.1). No false-label rate for the construction is reported.
- Generality: training on reasoning prompts only raised Chat Hard, Safety, and Reasoning but lowered Chat (97.6 → 96.6); the authors suggest the harder training data as the cause (§5.1, Interpretation). MT-Bench agreement moved from 77.8 to 79.5 (Table 2).
- Measurement: RewardBench accuracy differs by 5.6 points between the two fixed response orders (85.5 vs 91.1, Table 9; difference derived); the authors recommend reporting the average of both orders (App. A.3).
- Limitations stated: 70B models only; pairwise judgments only; the seed must already produce reasonable evaluations; compute not studied (Limitations).

- Audit claims not found in the source: "the judge cannot separate pairs by surface quality and must reason about instruction fit" and "must learn content-level discrimination rather than surface quality" (not stated); "the preference label is guaranteed" (§3 says the losing response is "likely of lower quality"); "the model trains on the rest" of the sampled judgments (one correct judgment per example is kept, §3.4).
