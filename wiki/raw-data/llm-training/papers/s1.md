# s1: Simple test-time scaling
- **Core Insight:** Supervised finetuning Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on 1,000 curated question/trace pairs, plus a decoding rule that suppresses the end-of-thinking token and appends "Wait", raises AIME24 from 50.0 to 56.7 and MATH500 from 92.6 to 93.0 without changing weights (Table 1, Table 5).
- **Guideline:** When a strong instruction-tuned base model is available and the distillation budget is small, select the SFT set by quality, difficulty, and diversity jointly, because each criterion used alone loses 16.7 to 23.3 AIME24 points relative to s1K (Table 2). Adding the full 59K pool instead gives 53.3 vs 50.0 with a 95% interval of [-13.3%, +20.0%], so it is not separable from noise at this sample size.
- **Authors:** Niklas Muennighoff, Zitong Yang, Weijia Shi, Xiang Lisa Li, Li Fei-Fei, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, et al.
- **Year:** 2025 (arXiv v1 2025-01; v3 2025-03-01)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** reasoning-trace distillation, long-CoT, test-time scaling, SFT data selection, budget forcing

## Abstract
The paper asks for the simplest recipe that produces both test-time scaling and strong reasoning performance. It curates s1K, 1,000 questions paired with reasoning traces distilled from the Google Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking Experimental API, selected from a 59,029-question pool by three criteria validated in ablations: difficulty, diversity, and quality (§2). It then introduces budget forcing, a decoding intervention that either terminates thinking by emitting the end-of-thinking delimiter, or extends it by suppressing that delimiter and appending "Wait" (§3). After SFT of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on s1K, the resulting s1-32B exceeds o1-preview on competition math by up to 27% and extrapolates from 50% to 57% on AIME24 through budget forcing alone (Abstract).

## Key Contributions
- **s1K:** 1,000 question/trace/solution triples selected from 59,029 collected questions drawn from 16 sources (§2.1, Table 7).
- **Three-stage selection:** quality filtering (API errors and formatting), difficulty filtering by two-model solvability, and diversity sampling over Mathematics Subject Classification domains (§2.2).
- **Budget forcing:** a decoding-time control with 100% controllability and the best measured scaling slope among six compared methods (§3.1, Table 3).
- **Data ablations:** single-criterion subsets and the full 59K pool, each with paired bootstrap confidence intervals (§5.1, Table 2).
- **s1K-1.1:** the same 1,000 questions with traces regenerated by DeepSeek r1, released seven days after s1 (§A).

## Technical Details
- **Pool:** 59,029 questions from 16 sources (§2.1). Largest sources are NuminaMATH 30,660, MATH 11,999, OlympicArena 4,250, OmniMath 4,238, AGIEval 2,385 (Table 7). Two datasets are original: s1-prob, 182 questions from Stanford Statistics PhD qualifying exams, and s1-teasers, 23 quantitative-trading brain teasers at difficulty "Hard" (§2.1).
- **Trace generation:** all traces and solutions come from the Google Gemini Flash Thinking API, yielding 59K question/trace/solution triples (§2.1).
- **Quality filter:** removing API errors leaves 54,116; removing formatting defects (ASCII art, missing image references, inconsistent numbering) leaves 51,581; 384 samples from sources judged high quality are taken directly into the final 1,000 (§2.2).
- **Difficulty filter:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct each attempt every question, with correctness graded by Claude 3.5 Sonnet against the reference solution; any question either model solves is removed, leaving 24,496 (§2.2, §C.3). Reasoning-trace token length, measured with the Qwen2.5 tokenizer, is the second difficulty indicator.
- **Diversity sampling:** Claude 3.5 Sonnet assigns each question a Mathematics Subject Classification domain; one domain is drawn uniformly, then one problem from that domain under a distribution favoring longer traces, repeated to 1,000 samples spanning 50 domains (§2.2; Table 6 in §C.1 lists 51 domain rows including an "All domains" row).
- **Decontamination:** 8-gram overlap filtering of all samples against MATH500, GPQA Diamond, and AIME24; AIME 2022–2023 (90 questions) is also excluded because it was used during development (§C.5, Table 7 caption).
- **Trace correctness:** the grader deems 53.6% of s1K traces correct and 63.0% of s1K-1.1 traces correct; incorrect traces are kept deliberately (§2.2).
- **Budget forcing mechanics:** maximum enforced by appending the end-of-thinking delimiter and optionally "Final Answer:"; minimum enforced by suppressing that delimiter and appending "Wait" (§3.1).
- **Results (Table 1):** s1-32B 56.7 AIME24 / 93.0 MATH500 / 59.6 GPQA Diamond; s1 without budget forcing 50.0 / 92.6 / 56.6; base Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct 26.7 / 84.0 / 49.0; o1-preview 44.6 / 85.5 / 73.3; r1-distill (800K examples) 72.6 / 94.3 / 62.1.
- **Scaling limit:** AIME24 gains from repeated "Wait" flatten at six suppressions; further suppression drives repetitive loops (§4.2, Figure 4a).
- **Method comparison (Table 3):** budget forcing 100% control, slope 15, 56.7 AIME24; token-conditional control 40% control, slope −24, 40.0; rejection sampling 100% control, slope −35, 40.0.
- **s1K-1.1 (§A):** traces regenerated with DeepSeek r1, same training procedure. s1.1 without budget forcing reaches 94.4 MATH500 / 60.6 GPQA / 56.7 AIME24 / 50.0 AIME 2025, against s1 at 92.6 / 56.6 / 50.0 / 26.7 (Table 5). Distilling from Claude 3.7 was tried and gave worse performance than r1; those numbers are not reported (§A).
- **Compute comparison:** s1-32B required 7 H100 GPU hours; finetuning on the 59K pool required 394 H100 GPU hours (§5.1).
- **Evaluation determinism:** with vLLM, identical seeds and greedy decoding still produce different scores across batch sizes and tensor-parallel settings; final evaluations are run in full precision (§B).

## Findings relevant to generality and distillation
- **Distillation is teacher-bounded and teacher-swappable.** Holding the 1,000 questions and the training procedure fixed and regenerating traces with DeepSeek r1 instead of Gemini Flash Thinking raises AIME 2025 from 26.7 to 50.0 and GPQA from 56.6 to 60.6 (Table 5, §A). This is a controlled teacher swap on fixed prompts. Claude 3.7 as teacher was worse than r1, without numbers (§A).
- **Breadth of the training set is narrow.** All 59K source questions are competition math, olympiad science, standardized-test, puzzle, or code problems (Table 7); evaluation is restricted to AIME24, MATH500, and GPQA Diamond (§4.1). The paper reports no non-reasoning or instruction-following evaluation, so transfer outside this distribution is not measured.
- **Selection ablations are underpowered.** Only the 1K-random AIME24 interval [-26.7%, -3.3%], the 1K-diverse interval [-40.0%, -10.0%], and the 1K-longest MATH500 interval [-5.0%, -0.2%] exclude zero; the 59K-full comparison does not (Table 2).
- **Test-time scaling saturates.** Budget forcing extrapolates AIME24 from 50 to 57 but flattens at six "Wait" insertions and then degrades into repetition (§4.2).
- **Incorrect traces are retained.** 46.4% of s1K traces are graded incorrect and are still trained on as positive targets, on the stated ground that the reasoning process rather than the final answer is the target (§2.2).
