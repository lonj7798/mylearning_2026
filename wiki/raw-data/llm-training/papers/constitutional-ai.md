# Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback
- **Core Insight:** A natural-language "constitution" of ~16 principles plus an AI critique-and-revise loop can replace almost all human harmlessness labels without sacrificing helpfulness.
- **Guideline:** Structure safety post-training as two passes — SL-CAI (self-critique + self-revise on red-team prompts, then SFT on the revisions) followed by RL-CAI (AI labels pairwise preferences with chain-of-thought → preference model → PPO).
- **Authors:** Yuntao Bai, Saurav Kadavath, Sandipan Kundu, Amanda Askell, et al. (Anthropic; ~50 authors)
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073
- **Relevant topics:** RLAIF, self-critique, self-revision, chain-of-thought preference labeling, harmlessness vs helpfulness Pareto, red-teaming

## Abstract
Constitutional AI (CAI) trains a helpful-and-harmless assistant using a fixed written "constitution" and only ~a few thousand human helpfulness preferences — harmlessness preferences are generated by the AI itself. The method has two stages. In SL-CAI, a helpful-only RLHF model is red-teamed; for each harmful response it is prompted to critique its own answer against a randomly selected principle and then revise it; these revisions are used for standard SFT. In RL-CAI, the SL-CAI model labels pairs of responses with an explanation of which is better according to the constitution; the resulting preference dataset trains a preference model, which is then used as a reward for PPO. The final model is less evasive than pure-RLHF harmlessness models while being equally safe.

## Key Contributions
- **Constitution:** ~16 short written principles (e.g. "please choose the response that is most harmless, ethical, polite..."). The constitution is sampled from, not concatenated — each critique uses one principle.
- **Critique-Revise loop (SL-CAI):** `(prompt, harmful_response) → critique_prompt(principle) → critique → revise_prompt(principle) → revised_response`. Then SFT on `(prompt, revised_response)` pairs.
- **AI preference labeling (RL-CAI):** given two responses, prompt the AI "which response is more {principle}?" with chain-of-thought; take the log-odds of "(A)" vs "(B)" as a soft label.
- **RLAIF pipeline:** preference model trained on mixed human-helpfulness + AI-harmlessness labels → used as reward in PPO → produces the final Helpful+Harmless model.
- **Result:** CAI Pareto-dominates pure RLHF harmlessness on the helpfulness/harmlessness front; CAI models are much less evasive (they explain refusals instead of stonewalling).
- **Scaling observation:** harmlessness of AI labels improves with the size of the labeling model — Constitutional methods scale with capability.

## Technical Details
- **Red-team prompt set:** ~180K prompts drawn from internal red-teaming corpora.
- **Critique prompt template:** "Identify specific ways in which the assistant's last response is harmful, unethical, racist, sexist, toxic, dangerous, or illegal." then "Rewrite the assistant's response to remove any and all harmful, unethical, ... content."
- **CoT preference prompt:** asks the labeler to reason step-by-step before answering "A" or "B".
- **Label smoothing:** soft labels are clipped to `[0.25, 0.75]` when converted from log-probs.
- **PPO details:** essentially InstructGPT's pipeline — same KL-to-SFT penalty, same β range (see **[[kl-control-rlhf]]**).
- **Human data:** ~135K helpfulness comparisons; zero harmlessness comparisons in the final runs beyond seed data.
- **Evaluation:** crowdworker Elo on helpfulness and harmlessness, plus automated harmfulness classifier.
