# D4: Improving LLM Pretraining via Document De-Duplication and Diversification
- **Core Insight:** For a 6.7B OPT-architecture model trained on 100B tokens, documents selected with D4 at selection ratio R = 0.25 reach the baseline perplexity with about 20% fewer updates and end with a 2.04% higher mean 0-shot accuracy over 16 NLP tasks than randomly selected documents (Abstract, §4.1, Fig. 1).
- **Guideline:** When the source pool of MinHash-deduplicated web documents is larger than the token budget, select documents by D4 instead of at random, because this improved Instruct + Answers perplexity at 1.3B (Fig. 2) and non-web perplexity, Instruct + Answers perplexity, and mean accuracy at 6.7B (Fig. 1), while perplexity on web-snapshot validation sets got worse (§4.4.1); when the pool is smaller than the budget, repeat a D4-selected subset, because 2 epochs over it beat one pass over random documents at 1.3B (Table 1).
- **Authors:** Kushal Tirumala, Daniel Simig, Armen Aghajanyan, Ari S. Morcos (Meta AI Research)
- **Year:** 2023 (arXiv v1 2023-08; only arXiv version; the PDF is marked "Preprint. Under review.")
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12284
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** pre-training data selection, semantic deduplication, embedding clustering, data repetition, validation-set proximity

## Abstract
LLMs are usually pre-trained with one pass over tokens sampled at random from web corpora, and data selection beyond simple de-duplication such as MinHash has received little study. The paper selects documents with pre-trained model embeddings, on top of already de-duplicated data. At 6.7B scale this speeds up training by about 20% and raises average downstream accuracy on 16 NLP tasks by up to 2%. Repeating an intelligently selected subset outperforms baseline training, while repeating a random subset performs worse than baseline. The authors state that this questions the practice of training for a single epoch on as much data as possible.

## Key Contributions
- The D4 selection strategy: SemDeDup, then re-clustering, then SSL Prototypes pruning (§3.4).
- A fixed-compute comparison of random selection, SemDeDup, SSL Prototypes, and D4 at 1.3B parameters and 40B tokens with 3 seeds, confirmed at 6.7B and 100B tokens (§4.1, Fig. 1, Fig. 2).
- A fixed-data comparison showing that repeated D4-selected tokens outperform new random tokens (§4.2, Table 1, App. A.6).
- A cost account of the selection step against the training compute it saves (§4.3, Fig. 4).
- An analysis of why selection worsens perplexity on web-snapshot validation sets, and an ablation of re-clustering (§4.4, Fig. 5–7).

## Technical Details
- **Source data (CC-dedup):** 5 CommonCrawl dumps from 2017–2020, processed with the CCNet pipeline used for LLaMA, followed by document-level MinHash de-duplication (§3.1, App. A.1.2). It holds about 600 million documents (App. A.1.3).
- **Selection ratio R:** the fraction of source documents kept (§3). In the fixed-compute setting the source dataset grows as R shrinks, so every run sees the same token budget (§4.1, Fig. 2 caption).
- **Embedding:** each document is passed through a 125M OPT model, and the last-layer embedding of the last token is used (§3.4). Embeddings are normalized to L2 norm 1.0 (App. A.1.3).
- **Clustering:** faiss spherical K-Means with 11,000 clusters and 20 iterations, fit on a random sample of about 100M documents (App. A.1.3). At 125M scale, 1K to 1M clusters gave no significant difference (App. A.1.3, Fig. A1).
- **SemDeDup (Abbas et al., arXiv:2303.09540):** K-Means clustering, then removal of points that lie within epsilon-balls of one another inside a cluster; used without modification (§3.4). This paper does not state the epsilon value.
- **SSL Prototypes (Sorscher et al. 2022):** K-Means clustering, then removal of points in increasing order of distance to the nearest centroid, so the most prototypical points go first (§3.4).
- **D4 steps (§3.4):** (1) apply SemDeDup with ratio R_dedup to dataset D, giving D′; (2) re-cluster D′ with K-Means; (3) apply SSL Prototypes to D′ with ratio R_proto. The overall ratio is R = R_dedup × R_proto. R_dedup is 0.75 in all main experiments (§3.4, App. A.1.3).
- **Motivation:** MinHash leaves dense "duplicate-driven clusters" of templated text in embedding space (§3.4, App. A.9). Such clusters can be found as clusters whose standard deviation of cosine distance to the centroid is below 0.03 (App. A.9).
- **Evaluation groups (§3.3, App. A.1.4):** web-snapshot perplexity (C4, CC-dedup, Pile CommonCrawl); non-web-snapshot perplexity (other Pile subsets, Reddit, and others); "Instruct + Answers" perplexity on OPT-IML instruction data, which is never trained on; mean 0-shot accuracy on the 16 OPT evaluation tasks.
- **6.7B, 100B tokens, R = 0.25 (Fig. 1):** 22.18% faster on non-web-snapshot perplexity, 18.08% faster on Instruct + Answers perplexity, and 2.04% higher mean 0-shot accuracy at the end of training.
- **1.3B, 40B tokens (§4.1, Fig. 2):** all three selection methods improve Instruct + Answers perplexity. D4 improves it most, with the largest gap when the source dataset is about 4× the target.
- **Selection cost (§4.3):** 20% fewer updates at 6.7B saves about 4,300 GPU hours. Embedding 400B tokens with the 125M model takes about 888 A100 GPU hours, and a CPU step on one 96-core machine takes about one day. The net saving is about 3,412 GPU hours. By extrapolation the authors estimate overall gains of 20% for LLaMA-65B and 22% for OPT-175B (§4.3, Fig. 4).
- **Re-clustering ablation:** without step (2), perplexity is worse on web, non-web, and Instruct + Answers sets (§4.4.2, Fig. 7).
- **Embedding choice:** SentenceTransformer all-mini-LM-v6 embeddings outperform OPT-125M embeddings at 8M, 125M, and 1.3B scale; at 6.7B the OPT embeddings outperform all-mini-LM-v6 (App. A.7, Fig. A17). The last-token OPT embedding groups documents by how they end (App. A.7).

## Findings relevant to generality
- **Web-snapshot validation sets:** data selection always worsens perplexity on C4, CC-dedup, and CommonCrawl validation sets (§4.4.1). These sets lie closest to the training set in embedding space, and their low-perplexity points are the most affected (§4.4.1, Fig. 5).
- **Which metric tracks ability:** across 1.3B/40B-token runs, lower web-snapshot perplexity goes with slightly worse Instruct + Answers perplexity (Pearson −0.368) and slightly lower accuracy (−0.188); lower Instruct + Answers perplexity goes with higher accuracy (0.298) (Fig. 6). The authors conclude that validation sets close to the training data may not indicate generalization (§4.4.1, Interpretation).
- **Repetition (1.3B, 40B tokens, 3 seeds, Table 1):** random 40B tokens for 1 epoch: non-web perplexity 16.27, Instruct + Answers 14.19. Random 20B for 2 epochs: 16.39 and 14.37. D4 20B for 2 epochs: 16.10 and 13.85. D4 repetition also beat random new tokens at 125M and 6.7B and at R = 0.5 and 0.25 (App. A.6, Fig. A15). For a fixed selection method, new tokens are usually better than repeated ones (App. A.6, Fig. A13).
- **Variance:** accuracy varies considerably across the 16 individual tasks, so only the mean is reported (App. A.3, Fig. A3).
- **Limits (§5):** one data distribution only, not mixtures of sources; the largest model is 6.7B trained on 100B tokens.
