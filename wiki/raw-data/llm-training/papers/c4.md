# Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer
- **Core Insight:** C4 is one April 2019 Common Crawl snapshot passed through line-, page-, and span-level heuristic filters plus English identification (langdetect probability ≥ 0.99), giving about 750 GB of text (§2.2); a T5 baseline pre-trained on unfiltered C4 (6.1 TB) scored lower than on C4 (745 GB) on 7 of 7 reported metrics, e.g. SuperGLUE 68.04 vs 71.36 (§3.4.1, Table 8).
- **Guideline:** When the pre-training token budget is much larger than a candidate in-domain corpus, use a large filtered web corpus, because the smaller T5 variants (16–35 GB) beat C4 on some tasks at 2^35 tokens (Table 8) but repeating a data set 1,024 or more times lowers scores (Table 9), and the authors kept C4 for 1T-token training for this reason (§3.7); when the budget is small enough that an in-domain corpus is not repeated, in-domain data raised matching-task scores (e.g. ReCoRD exact match 68.16 → 73.72 with RealNews-like data, §3.4.1).
- **Authors:** Colin Raffel, Noam Shazeer, Adam Roberts, Katherine Lee, Sharan Narang, Michael Matena, et al. (Google)
- **Year:** 2019 (arXiv v1 2019-10; JMLR 21, 2020)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** web filtering, heuristic cleaning, deduplication, language identification, T5 pre-training data ablations, data repetition

## Abstract
The paper converts text-based language problems into a text-to-text format and compares pre-training objectives, architectures, unlabeled data sets, transfer approaches, and scale on dozens of language-understanding tasks. It introduces the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus (C4) as the unlabeled pre-training source and, combining the study's findings with scale, reports state-of-the-art results on benchmarks covering summarization, question answering, and text classification. The data set, pre-trained models, and code are released (Abstract).

## Key Contributions
- The C4 filtering rules applied to Common Crawl "web extracted text" and the released data set in TensorFlow Datasets (§2.2).
- A controlled comparison of six pre-training data sets with the same 220M baseline model: C4, unfiltered C4, RealNews-like, WebText-like, Wikipedia, and Wikipedia + Toronto Books Corpus (§3.4.1, Table 8).
- A data-repetition experiment that truncates C4 to 2^29–2^23 tokens while keeping 2^35 training tokens (§3.4.2, Table 9).
- Release of all C4 variants used in §3.4 through TensorFlow Datasets (§3.4).

## Technical Details
- **Source.** Common Crawl produces about 20 TB of scraped text per month, mostly not natural language (§2.2). C4 uses the web extracted text from April 2019 (§2.2).
- **Line rules.** Keep only lines ending in a terminal punctuation mark (period, exclamation mark, question mark, end quotation mark); keep only lines with at least 5 words; remove lines containing the word "Javascript"; remove lines with "terms of use", "privacy policy", "cookie policy", "uses cookies", "use of cookies", or "use cookies"; remove Wikipedia-style citation markers such as [1] and [citation needed] (§2.2).
- **Page rules.** Discard pages with fewer than 3 sentences; remove pages containing any word on the "List of Dirty, Naughty, Obscene or Otherwise Bad Words"; remove pages containing "lorem ipsum"; remove pages containing a curly bracket "{" (§2.2).
- **Deduplication.** Discard all but one of any three-sentence span occurring more than once in the data set (§2.2).
- **Language.** Keep pages classified as English by langdetect with probability at least 0.99 (§2.2).
- **Size.** About 750 GB (§2.2); 745 GB in Table 8. Unfiltered C4 (langdetect only) is 6.1 TB (Table 8).
- **Variants.** RealNews-like keeps C4 pages from RealNews domains, 35 GB. WebText-like uses 12 months of Common Crawl (August 2018 to July 2019), applies the C4 filters, then keeps URLs with Reddit score ≥ 3, 17 GB; the single-month version was about 2 GB (§3.4.1, Table 8).
- **Release code (separate artifact, consistent).** TensorFlow Datasets `tensorflow_datasets/text/c4_utils.py` (master, fetched 2026-09-14) sets `_MIN_WORDS_PER_LINE = 5`, `_MIN_NUM_SENTENCES = 3`, and `detect_english(..., min_probability=0.99)`.

## Findings relevant to generality
- **Filtering (Result, single study).** Table 8, baseline model, 2^35 tokens: C4 vs unfiltered C4 = GLUE 83.28 vs 81.46, CNN/DM 19.24 vs 19.14, SQuAD 80.88 vs 78.78, SuperGLUE 71.36 vs 68.04, EnDe 26.98 vs 26.55, EnFr 39.82 vs 39.34, EnRo 27.65 vs 27.21 (§3.4.1). The text says unfiltered C4 is "the worst in every task"; in Table 8 it is lowest in 6 of 7 columns, and Wikipedia is lowest on SuperGLUE (68.01) (derived from Table 8).
- **In-domain data.** Wikipedia + TBC raises SuperGLUE to 73.24, "almost entirely" from MultiRC exact match 25.78 → 50.93 (Table 16); RealNews-like raises ReCoRD exact match 68.16 → 73.72 (§3.4.1). The authors call this "unsatisfying" when the goal is fast adaptation to tasks from arbitrary domains (§3.4.1).
- **Size cost of domain filters.** WebText-like is about 40× smaller than C4 while drawing on 12× more Common Crawl data (§3.4.1).
- **Repetition.** With 2^35 training tokens, SuperGLUE is 72.03 at 2^29 tokens (64 repeats), 69.97 at 2^27 (256), 64.76 at 2^25 (1,024), 59.29 at 2^23 (4,096); training loss falls as the data set shrinks, which the authors read as memorization (§3.4.2, Table 9, Fig. 6). Effects are "limited" at 64 repeats (§3.4.2).
- **Language coverage.** C4 keeps English only, and the shared vocabulary restricts the model to a fixed set of languages (§2.2; §3.1.3).
- **Not measured in the paper.** The effect of each individual filter, and which content or populations the filters remove.

## Connections
- [[ccnet]] — concurrent Common Crawl pipeline; CCNet §2 describes Raffel et al. (2019) as concurrent work.
- [[fineweb]] — §3.5 re-applies C4 filters to the 2019-18 crawl: the terminal-punctuation filter gives the largest HellaSwag gain but removes about 30% of tokens; curly-bracket and word-length filters remove 2.8% and 4.3%; lorem-ipsum, javascript, and policy rules each remove < 0.5%; "all but terminal punct" removes about 7% and beats terminal punctuation alone. FineWeb keeps all C4 filters except terminal punctuation. §3.7 lists C4 at 172B tokens.
- [[dolma]] — §5.2 reaches the opposite choice with 1.2B ablation models: the single C4 rule that removes paragraphs not ending in punctuation ("C4 NoPunc", 22.73% of UTF-8 characters tagged for removal) outperforms the full C4 rule set and Gopher rules; Dolma combines Gopher rules with C4 NoPunc. The two sources conflict on the most useful C4 rule.
- Dodge et al., "Documenting Large Webtext Corpora: A Case Study on the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus" (arXiv:2104.08758v2, no library card) — separate audit of the released C4.EN: 365M documents, 156B SpaCy tokens, 305 GB; without the blocklist 395M / 198B / 380 GB; without cleaning 1.1B / 1.4T / 2.3 TB (Table 1). Of 100,000 blocklist-excluded documents clustered into 50 clusters, 16 clusters (31% of excluded documents) are largely sexual; other clusters cover science, medicine, health, legal, and political text (§5.1). Mentions of sexual orientations have the highest likelihood of removal; in 50 sampled documents mentioning "lesbian" and "gay", 22% and 36% are non-offensive or non-sexual (§5.2). Documents classified as African American English and Hispanic-aligned English are removed by the blocklist at 42% and 32%, against 6.2% (White-aligned) and 7.2% (other); 97.8% of C4.EN is White-aligned, 0.07% AAE, 0.09% Hispanic-aligned (§5.3). It also reports patents.google.com as the most represented website, machine-translated patent text, and benchmark contamination (§3–§4). Dodge §2 states the line and page thresholds as "fewer than three words" and "less than five sentences", which does not match the T5 paper and the TFDS code (5 words, 3 sentences).
