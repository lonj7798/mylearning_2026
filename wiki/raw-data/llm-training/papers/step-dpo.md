# Step-DPO: Step-wise Preference Optimization for Long-chain Reasoning of LLMs
- **Core Insight:** Treating one reasoning step as the preference unit, rather than the whole answer, raises MATH accuracy by about 3 points on models above 70B parameters with 10K preference pairs and fewer than 500 training steps; Qwen2-72B-Instruct goes from 69.4% to 70.8% on MATH and 92.4% to 94.0% on GSM8K (Abstract; §4.2, Table 1).
- **Guideline:** When applying DPO to long chain-of-thought reasoning, locate the first erroneous step in a wrong trajectory and build the pair at that step, and draw the preferred step from the policy's own samples rather than from a human or GPT-4 rewrite, because in-distribution preferred steps scored 55.8% on MATH against 55.1% for out-of-distribution ones on Qwen2-7B-SFT (§4.3, Table 4).
- **Authors:** Xin Lai, Zhuotao Tian, Yukang Chen, Senqiao Yang, Xiangru Peng, Jiaya Jia (The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen; SmartMore)
- **Year:** 2024 (arXiv v1 2024-06)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18629
- **Source type:** paper
- **Relevant topics:** preference optimization, mathematical reasoning, step-level DPO, in-distribution preference data

## Abstract
The paper argues that DPO gives limited benefit on long-chain mathematical reasoning because a rejected
answer usually contains correct steps before the first error, so rejecting the full answer also pushes
down those correct steps and adds noise (§3.1). Step-DPO keeps the DPO functional form but conditions on
a prefix of correct steps and applies the preference between a single correct next step and a single
incorrect next step. The authors build a three-stage pipeline (error collection, step localization,
rectification) that yields about 10K step-wise preference pairs, and report that the preferred step must
be generated by the reference model itself: human- or GPT-4-written corrections are out of distribution
for the policy and are less effective (§3.2, Table 4).

## Key Contributions
- Step-DPO objective: the preference is over the next reasoning step `s_win` against `s_lose`, given the
  problem `x` and the preceding correct steps `s_{1..k-1}` (Eq. 2).
- A three-stage data pipeline in which humans or GPT-4 only locate the first error and rank answers; they
  do not write the corrected step (§3.2). About 10K pairs, released with code and models (Abstract).
- An in-distribution against out-of-distribution ablation isolating who writes the preferred step
  (§4.3, Table 4).
- Results on MATH, GSM8K, AIME 2024, and Odyssey-MATH across 7B to 72B models (§4.2, Tables 1-2).

## Technical Details
- Objective (Eq. 2): `L(θ) = −E log σ( β log [π_θ(s_win|x; s_{1..k-1}) / π_ref(s_win|x; s_{1..k-1})]
  − β log [π_θ(s_lose|x; s_{1..k-1}) / π_ref(s_lose|x; s_{1..k-1})] )`, where `x` is the problem,
  `s_{1..k-1}` the preceding correct steps, `s_win` the correct next step, `s_lose` the incorrect next
  step, `π_θ` the policy, `π_ref` the fixed reference model, `σ` the sigmoid, and `β` the strength of the
  constraint toward `π_ref` (§3.1, Eq. 1-2).
- Error collection: problems are prompted with `"Let's think step by step. Step 1:"` so completions are
  segmented as `Step i:`; completions whose final answer differs from the ground truth are kept (§3.2).
- Step localization: steps are checked in order until the first error at index `k`; `s_k` becomes `s_lose`.
- Rectification: multiple continuations are sampled from `π_ref` given `x` and `s_{1..k-1}`; those whose
  final answer matches the ground truth are kept, and the first step of such a continuation becomes
  `s_win`. Samples whose `s_win` is itself wrong may be filtered manually or with GPT-4 (§3.2).
- Data: MetaMath and MMIQC problems answered step by step with DeepSeek-Math; after removing wrong final
  answers, 374K samples remain, 299K used for SFT and the rest, plus a subset of AQuA, processed by the
  pipeline into 10K Step-DPO pairs (§4.1).
- Results, MATH / GSM8K (§4.2, Table 1): DeepSeekMath-RL 7B 51.7 / 88.2 → 53.2 / 88.7; Qwen2-7B-SFT
  54.8 / 88.2 → 55.8 / 88.5; Qwen1.5-32B-SFT 54.9 / 90.0 → 56.9 / 90.9; Llama-3-70B-SFT 56.9 / 92.2 →
  59.5 / 93.3; Qwen2-72B-SFT 61.7 / 92.9 → 64.7 / 93.9; Qwen2-72B-Instruct (reproduced prompt)
  69.4 / 92.4 → 70.8 / 94.0.
- Competition sets (§4.2, Table 2): Qwen2-72B-SFT AIME 2024 1/30 → 3/30 and Odyssey-MATH 44.2 → 47.0;
  Qwen2-72B-Instruct AIME 5/30 → 4/30 and Odyssey-MATH 47.0 → 50.1. The same 10K pairs were used.
- Matched-budget comparison at 5K pairs on MATH (§4.3, Table 3): Qwen2-7B-SFT 54.8, +DPO 55.0,
  +Step-DPO 55.8; Qwen2-72B-SFT 61.7, +DPO 62.5, +Step-DPO 64.1.
- In-distribution ablation on MATH (§4.3, Table 4): Qwen2-7B-SFT 54.8; +Step-DPO with GPT-4-corrected
  (out-of-distribution) steps 55.1; +Step-DPO with self-generated steps 55.8.
- Evaluation sets: MATH test 5000 problems; GSM8K test 1319 problems (§4.1).

## Findings relevant to negative feedback
- The paper's reason for localizing the negative: in long reasoning answers the first error usually
  appears midway, so rejecting the entire answer also rejects the correct preceding steps and
  "introduc[es] significant noise" (§3.1). This is a negative used as gradient, in the rejected term of DPO.
- Diagnostics reported: validation accuracy at judging preferred against undesirable outputs, and the
  reward margin between them, tracked over training steps; vanilla DPO has a lower margin that plateaus
  (§1, Fig. 2; 5K training and 3K evaluation pairs, §4.3).
- The introduction cites Hong et al. (2024) for the observation that as the probability of preferred
  outputs increases so does the probability of undesirable ones (§1).
- The weakness of out-of-distribution preferred steps is attributed to the low log-probability of
  `s_ood_win` under `π_ref` and to "gradient decay issues" (§3.2; derivation deferred to the appendix).

## Findings relevant to generality
- The 10K pairs built from MATH/GSM8K-style problems also improved AIME 2024 and Odyssey-MATH without
  any competition-specific training data, which the authors call generalization (§4.2). No non-math
  benchmark is evaluated, so transfer outside mathematics is not measured.
- Gains grow with model size (about +3 points on MATH at 70B and above against +1.0 at 7B); the authors
  offer untapped capacity as a hypothesis, not a measured cause (§4.2, Interpretation).
