# Sampling Temperature Schedules in RL Rollouts for LLMs
- **Core Insight:** The rollout temperature is an exploration knob that is independent of the entropy bonus and the KL penalty — raising temperature expands the support of rollouts without changing the gradient's bias, and modern reasoning RL recipes explicitly anneal or oscillate it.
- **Guideline:** Start RL rollouts at `T = 1.0` (or slightly higher, `T = 1.1–1.2`), keep temperature fixed during pure-RLVR pretraining rounds, and anneal only in later "polish" rounds when you want the policy to commit; if entropy is collapsing, raising rollout temperature buys you exploration budget much faster than retuning the entropy coefficient.
- **Authors:** various — Tülu 3 (Lambert 2024), DeepSeek-R1 (2025), OpenRLHF / verl documentation, Ahmadian et al. "Back to Basics: REINFORCE for LLM" (2024)
- **Year:** 2024–2025
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14740
- **Relevant topics:** rollout temperature, top-p / top-k truncation, nucleus sampling, annealing, exploration-exploitation

## Abstract (synthesis)
Unlike classical RL, LLM RL uses a separate sampling process for rollouts (autoregressive, with temperature, top-p, and top-k). That sampling distribution is **not the same** as the policy being optimized — temperature `T` rescales logits by `1/T` at sample time without changing the parameters. Several 2024–2025 papers (Tülu 3, DeepSeek-R1, "Back to Basics" REINFORCE-for-LLM, OpenRLHF practitioner notes) describe rollout-temperature choices as a first-class hyperparameter: use `T ≈ 1.0` for on-policy unbiasedness, raise `T` to widen exploration, and avoid top-k / top-p truncation during training (they introduce off-policy bias).

## Key Contributions / Observations
- **Default `T = 1.0`:** matches the policy distribution, keeps the importance-sampling ratio `π_new/π_old` well-defined.
- **Raising `T` for exploration:** `T ∈ [1.0, 1.5]` increases rollout diversity. Because rollouts are generated by `π_samp(a) ∝ π(a)^{1/T}`, this is off-policy; correct with importance weights or accept the bias if rollouts are still treated as the behavior policy.
- **Top-p / top-k during training: avoid or be explicit:** truncation introduces support bias that PPO's clipping does not absorb; DeepSeek-R1 and Tülu 3 both use `top_p = 1.0` (full support) during training rollouts.
- **Annealing schedule (Tülu 3 style):** `T = 1.0` for the verifiable-reward pretraining stage; `T = 0.7` for the final DPO polish; corresponds to widen-then-sharpen.
- **Re-warming after collapse (OpenRLHF practice):** if per-token entropy crashes, bump `T` by 0.2 for the next N rollouts to reinject diversity; a blunt but effective intervention.
- **R1-Zero:** reports `T = 1.0` with `top_p = 0.95` and a rollout budget of 32k tokens — the long context is itself an exploration mechanism, not just a capability mechanism.

## Technical Details
- **Tempered sampling:** `P_T(a|s) = softmax(z(s) / T)` where `z` are the pre-softmax logits. Entropy increases monotonically with `T`.
- **Top-p (nucleus):** restrict to smallest token set whose cumulative probability ≥ `p`; introduces hard truncation that is non-differentiable w.r.t. logits.
- **Top-k:** keep top-k tokens; same concerns as top-p.
- **Importance correction for off-policy rollouts:**
  `IS_ratio_t = π_θ(a_t|s_t) / π_samp(a_t|s_t)`; PPO's ratio `π_new/π_old` becomes `π_new/π_samp` if you insist on a cold sampler.
- **Common recipe:** sampler `T = 1.0`, `top_p = 1.0` during training; evaluation at `T = 0` (greedy) or `T = 0.6` with `top_p = 0.95` (DeepSeek-R1 default).
- **Budget note:** longer `max_new_tokens` interacts with `T` — short rollouts at high `T` may not commit to a reasoning path long enough to receive meaningful reward.
