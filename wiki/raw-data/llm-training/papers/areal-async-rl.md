# AReaL: A Large-Scale Asynchronous Reinforcement Learning System for Language Reasoning
- **Core Insight:** Fully decoupling generation from training reduces end-to-end training time by up to 2.77× at matched benchmark accuracy (1.5B math: 14.8 h vs 41.0 h for the synchronous variant, 42.2 vs 42.0 AIME24, Table 1), but only when staleness is bounded by a parameter η and the PPO objective separates the behavior policy from the proximal policy: with naive PPO at η = 4 the 1.5B math run scores AIME24 23.3 against a 42.0 synchronous oracle, and with the decoupled objective 42.2 (Table 2).
- **Guideline:** When generation dominates step time and the trainer is idle waiting for the longest response, decouple generation from training and set an explicit maximum staleness η, because η ≤ 8 with the decoupled objective stayed within about 1 point of the synchronous oracle on four math benchmarks while raising effective training throughput from 128.7k to 371.7k tokens/s (Fig. 5c). When the objective is standard PPO, keep η ≤ 2, because naive PPO loses 2.0 AIME24 points at η = 2 and 18.7 at η = 4 (Table 2). When staleness is left unbounded (η → ∞), expect a loss even with the decoupled objective: 36.9 vs 42.0 AIME24 (Table 2).

## Abstract
Synchronous RL systems alternate generation and training in a batch setting, so each batch is generated by the same or latest model. Generation must wait for the longest output in the batch before the model is updated, which underutilizes inference GPUs. AReaL is a fully asynchronous RL system: rollout workers generate continuously without waiting, and trainer workers update the model whenever a batch has been collected. To keep training stable, AReaL balances the workload of rollout and training workers so that data staleness is controlled, and adopts a staleness-enhanced PPO variant for outdated samples. On math and code reasoning benchmarks it reports up to 2.77× training speedup over synchronous systems using the same number of GPUs, with matched or improved final performance.

## Key Contributions
- A four-component asynchronous architecture: interruptible rollout workers, a reward service, trainer workers reading a replay buffer, and a rollout controller (§4.1, Fig. 2).
- Interruptible generation: an `update_weights` request interrupts ongoing generations, the workers discard KV caches computed under the old weights, recompute them under the new weights, and continue decoding the unfinished sequences (§4.1).
- A staleness admission rule with hyperparameter η that the rollout controller enforces by rejecting generation requests (§5.1, Eq. 3).
- A decoupled PPO objective that separates the behavior policy π_behav from the proximal policy π_prox used as the trust-region center (§5.2, Eqs. 4–5), with Proposition 1 stating that a trajectory made of segments from several policy versions is equivalent to sampling from one behavior policy (§5.2, proof in App. D).
- System optimizations: padding-free sequence packing with dynamic micro-batch allocation (§6, Alg. 1), and CPU/GPU decoupling of reward computation and data transfer (§6).

## Technical Details
1. **Staleness rule.** With current policy version i, total generated trajectories N_r and training batch size B prompts, new generation requests are admitted only while ⌊(N_r − 1)/B⌋ ≤ i + η; older trajectories are prioritized when a batch is formed (§5.1, Eq. 3). η = 0 degenerates to synchronous RL (§5.1).
2. **Default staleness.** η = 4 for coding and η = 8 for math unless stated otherwise (§7.1).
3. **Decoupled objective.** The ratio inside the clip is u_t^prox(θ) = π_θ(a_t|s_t)/π_prox(a_t|s_t), and the outer factor is π_prox/π_behav; π_prox is the parameters immediately before the current update step, and token probabilities are recomputed when the global batch arrives (§5.2, Eq. 5 and Practical Remark).
4. **End-to-end results (Table 1).** 1.5B math, 16 nodes, 250 PPO steps: AIME24 42.2 in 14.8 h for AReaL vs 42.0 in 41.0 h for Sync. AReaL and 43.1 in 33.6 h for the verl-trained DeepScaleR reference. 7B math, 24 nodes, 250 steps: 63.1 in 25.4 h vs 63.0 in 57.7 h. 14B code, 32 nodes, 80 steps: LiveCodeBench 58.1 in 21.9 h vs 56.7 in 48.8 h and 57.9 in 44.4 h for DeepCoder. 32B code, 48 nodes, 60 steps: 61.0 in 31.1 h vs 61.2 in 51.1 h. The 2.77× figure is the 1.5B ratio 41.0/14.8 (§7.2).
5. **Staleness sweep (Table 2, 1.5B math, 250 steps).** AIME24 without/with the decoupled objective: η = 1 → 41.8/42.1; η = 2 → 40.0/41.8; η = 4 → 23.3/42.2; η = 8 → 35.7/41.0; η = 16 → 35.8/38.7; η = ∞ → 34.0/36.9. Oracle (η = 0) is 42.0. MATH-500 at η = 4 is 66.9 without and 89.5 with, against an 89.2 oracle. AIME25 and AMC23 are in the same table.
6. **Throughput vs staleness (Fig. 5c).** Effective training throughput rises with η: 128.7k (η = 0), 269.3k (1), 356.6k (2 and 4), 371.7k (8), 382.4k (16), 396.8k (∞) tokens/s.
7. **Scaling (§7.3, Fig. 4).** Strong scaling is measured against verl at 16k and 32k context, from 32 to 512 GPUs depending on model size; AReaL scales approximately linearly and reaches at most 2.5× the baseline throughput, while verl OOMs for the 32B model at 32k context (§7.3, Fig. 4 caption).
8. **System ablations (§7.5).** Dynamic batching gives an average 30% throughput improvement across 1B/7B/32B (Fig. 6a, 32 micro-batches for the standard setting vs a 32,768-token budget per micro-batch). Interruptible generation gives 12% (1.5B) and 17% (7B) higher generation throughput on 4 nodes (Fig. 6b: 231k vs 207k, and 130k vs 111k tokens/s).
9. **Hardware and models.** H800 cluster of 64 nodes × 8 GPUs, NVLink intra-node and 3.2 Tbps RoCE inter-node; minimum 16 nodes per experiment, 48 nodes for the 32B model; three quarters of devices are allocated to inference, chosen over a 50-50 split in early experiments (§7.1). Base models are R1-Distilled-Qwen 1.5B–32B; math data from DeepScaleR, code data from DeepCoder (§7.1, App. B.2).
10. **Implementation.** ReaLHF framework, SGLang v0.4.6 for generation (vLLM v0.8.4 where SGLang errored, i.e. 32B or 64 nodes), Megatron-Core v0.11.0 or FSDP for training, SLURM scheduling (§6, App. B.4). The verl baseline is the main branch of 2025-05-07 (App. B.4).
11. **PPO configuration.** Critic and reference model are disabled; GAE λ = 1 and γ = 1; reward is +5 at the final token for a correct answer and −5 otherwise; advantage normalization over the global batch (App. B.1).

## Findings relevant to generality
- Transfer across model families: the same configuration as the Qwen 7B math run, applied to DeepSeek-Distilled-Llama-8B, gives AIME24 58.4 (η = 4) and 57.2 (η = 8) against a 50.4 base, AMC23 92.3/91.5 vs 84.2, MATH-500 92.2/91.9 vs 89.1, AIME25 42.6/41.6 vs 23.3 (App. C.2, Table 6). **Result (single study).**
- Broader evaluation of the released runs: 1.5B math AReaL vs synchronous reaches AIME25 32.0 vs 32.9 and AMC23 85.1 vs 84.4; 7B reaches AIME25 47.3 vs 50.0 (App. C.1, Table 4). Coding models are also evaluated on Codeforces rating and CodeContests (Table 5).
- Scope limit: all training and evaluation is math and code reasoning. The paper reports no multi-turn agent, tool-use, instruction-following, or safety evaluation, so it provides no evidence about breadth of capability outside these two domains.
- Small-scale replication (8 GPUs, 8k context, batch 64 × 16): the staleness-throughput direction holds, and RLOO advantage estimation shows slightly higher tolerance to staleness than vanilla PPO (App. C.3–C.4, Tables 7–8). **Result (single study).**
