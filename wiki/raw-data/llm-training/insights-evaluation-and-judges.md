# Insights — Evaluation and judges

- LLM judges agree with human experts at roughly the rate humans agree with each other, and carry measurable position and verbosity biases at the same time. GPT-4 as a pairwise judge agrees with human experts on 85% of non-tied MT-Bench votes against 81% agreement among the experts themselves ([[judge-llm-bias]]). Any win-rate number in this library that uses a GPT-4-class judge inherits those biases, including the AlpacaEval numbers in [[neftune]] and [[humpback]].
- Judges can be trained, and training them on their own filtered judgments works without human preference labels. Five iterations of self-training from Llama-3-70B-Instruct raise RewardBench overall accuracy with no human labels ([[self-taught-evaluators]]); a DPO + SFT loss on 680K judgement preference pairs gives SFR-Judge-70B its reported average ([[direct-judgement-preference]]); a judge trained by DPO on its own correct versus incorrect reasoning chains matched Bradley-Terry, PairRM, and GenRM reward models ([[generative-reward-models]], [[pairrm]], [[bradley-terry-rm]]).
- Benchmark choice inside a long-context or agentic suite changes the ranking. Correlation with ∞Bench QA at 128K is 0.63 for NIAH, 0.81 for RULER multi-key, and 0.88 for a RAG task ([[helmet]]); retriever choice moves the same agent from 55.90% to 70.12% on a fixed corpus ([[browsecomp-plus]]); higher reasoning effort lowered accuracy in 21 of 36 model-agent-benchmark combinations ([[holistic-agent-leaderboard]]).
- Per-domain and per-item measurement exposes what an aggregate hides. Per-domain perplexity gives a C4-only 1B baseline a perplexity up to 391,171 on the RedPajama arXiv domain where a single held-out loss looks acceptable ([[paloma]]); a two-dimensional item response model fit without benchmark labels on 100 open-weight models, 16 benchmarks, and over 34K questions separates a safety ability from a general-reasoning ability ([[ai2-benchmirt]]); benchmark signal-to-noise ratio at small scale predicts whether small-model rankings transfer ([[signal-and-noise-eval]], [[adding-error-bars-evals]]).
- Alignment training produces two evaluation artifacts that must be measured separately from capability: sycophancy and over-refusal. Five assistants fine-tuned with human feedback shift answers toward the user's stated view, with Claude 1.3 wrongly admitting a mistake on 98% of questions after "Are you sure?" ([[sycophancy-in-lms]]); on 250 safe prompts Llama-2-70b-chat with its original system prompt fully refuses 38% and partially refuses 21.6% while correctly refusing all 200 unsafe contrasts ([[xstest]], [[wildguard-data]]).
- Helpfulness and harmlessness preference models are in measurable tension, which is why single-score evaluation of an aligned model is incomplete: preference models trained only on helpfulness data score below chance on harmlessness data and the reverse also holds ([[hh-rlhf]], [[salad-bench]], [[harmbench-data]]).
