# DeepSeek-R1 Distillation from Synthetic Traces
- **Core Insight:** DeepSeek-R1 is not only a reasoning model; it is also a trace generator. The public DeepSeek docs show that the strongest transferable artifact is the long-CoT distribution itself, which can be used to SFT smaller dense bases.
- **Guideline:** For R1-style distillation, keep the teacher's reasoning format, curate the output pool aggressively, and SFT open dense bases on R1-generated samples instead of trying to recreate the full RL stack on the student.
- **Authors:** DeepSeek-AI
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948
- **Relevant topics:** reasoning distillation, synthetic traces, R1 long-CoT, rejection sampling, dense student SFT

## Summary
DeepSeek's public R1 release makes the distillation story very explicit: R1-Zero is trained by RL on DeepSeek-V3-Base, R1 adds a cold-start SFT plus further RL stages, and the distilled checkpoints are then fine-tuned on samples generated by DeepSeek-R1. The repo states that the Qwen-based distills use 800k samples curated with DeepSeek-R1, and that the released student family spans 1.5B, 7B, 8B, 14B, 32B, and 70B models. The practical takeaway is that R1's long-CoT format is portable even when the full RL recipe is not.

## Key Contributions
- Shows that reasoning traces can be treated as a transferable training signal, not just a byproduct of the teacher.
- Releases six dense R1-Distill checkpoints based on Qwen2.5 and Llama 3.x bases.
- Makes the teacher/student split explicit: the teacher is DeepSeek-R1, the student is standard dense SFT.
- Provides a public anchor for later open replications such as Open-R1 and Bespoke-Stratos.

## Technical Details
### Teacher-side trace generation
- R1-Zero is produced by applying large-scale RL directly to DeepSeek-V3-Base with no SFT warm start.
- R1 adds a cold-start SFT phase before RL to repair readability and language mixing, then uses additional post-training stages to improve reasoning and alignment.
- The public repo says the distilled students are trained on "samples generated by DeepSeek-R1" and that the Qwen distills use 800k samples curated with DeepSeek-R1.

### Filtering and reuse
- The public docs do not fully enumerate the distill corpus construction, but they make the key invariant clear: keep only teacher outputs that preserve the reasoning pattern and final answer quality.
- The R1 report describes a rejection-sampling SFT stage in the teacher pipeline itself, using the stage-1 RL model to generate data and filtering it with a stronger judge before the later alignment stage.
- Open replications such as [[openr1]] make the hidden part of the recipe concrete: sample multiple R1 traces, keep answer-correct outputs, and SFT the dense base on the filtered corpus.

### Student training
- Distilled models are fine-tuned from open dense bases rather than trained with RL.
- The released student set covers Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B/7B/14B/32B and Llama 3.1 8B / Llama 3.3 70B.
- The main training lesson is that dense students benefit more from copied reasoning structure than from trying to rediscover that structure via their own RL.

## Practical Lessons
- Use the teacher to create a high-signal trace distribution, then make the student imitate that distribution with simple SFT.
- Preserve the teacher's formatting conventions; the trace wrapper matters because it exposes where the reasoning lives.
- Distillation works best when the student base is already competent in the target domain, especially math and code.
- If you want an open recipe, replicate the trace-capture and filtering logic first; the exact teacher internals matter less than the quality of the kept outputs.

## Risks + gotchas
- **Corpus opacity:** the official docs give the 800k figure but not a full per-source breakdown of the distill corpus.
- **Teacher bias transfer:** student models inherit R1's style, shortcuts, and any benchmark contamination.
- **Format dependence:** if the student loses the long-CoT wrapper, performance on reasoning tasks tends to drop.
