# DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning — distillation component
- **Core Insight:** Fine-tuning open dense base models on 800K samples generated by DeepSeek-R1, with SFT only and no RL stage, produces students that outperform large-scale RL applied directly to a base model of the same size: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B reaches 72.6 pass@1 on AIME 2024 against 47.0 for Qwen2.5-32B-Zero, which was RL-trained for over 10K steps from Qwen2.5-32B-Base (Table 16).
- **Guideline:** When the goal is a strong small reasoning model and a strong teacher is available, distil by SFT on rejection-sampled correct teacher traces rather than running large-scale RL on the small base, because the paper reports the RL route requires enormous computational power and may still not reach distillation performance (§F.1). Reserve large-scale RL for advancing the strongest model.
- **Authors:** DeepSeek-AI (corresponding: research@deepseek.com)

- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948 (paper); https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1 (release repo)

## Summary
This card extracts only the distillation part of the DeepSeek-R1 release: Appendix B.3.3 (construction of the 800K supervised dataset), Appendix B.4.3 (distillation hyper-parameters), and Appendix F (distilled-model results and the distillation-versus-RL comparison), plus the repo README's model table. The full card for the release, including R1-Zero and the RL pipeline, is [[deepseek-r1]].

The 800K corpus is built in two parts. Reasoning data is produced by rejection sampling from the checkpoint of the first-stage RL training: multiple responses are sampled per prompt and only correct ones are retained, where correctness is judged by rules for rule-verifiable data and by feeding the ground truth and the model prediction to DeepSeek-V3 as a generative reward model otherwise. Chains of thought with mixed languages, long paragraphs, or code blocks are filtered out. This yields about 600k reasoning samples. Non-reasoning data reuses parts of the DeepSeek-V3 SFT dataset and adds software-engineering data; DeepSeek-V3 is prompted to produce a chain of thought before answering, except for simple queries such as "hello". This yields about 200k samples. Human annotators verify the accuracy of the elicited thinking processes.

Six dense students are fine-tuned on this corpus with SFT only. The paper states that no RL stage is applied to the students, and that demonstrating the effectiveness of distillation is the goal, leaving the RL stage on students to the community (§F).

## Key Contributions
- A disclosed construction procedure for the 800K distillation corpus: rejection sampling from a first-stage RL checkpoint, correctness filtering by rules or by a DeepSeek-V3 generative judge (prompt given verbatim in Listing 4), plus readability filters (§B.3.3).
- A per-domain breakdown of the corpus (Table 5) and the statement that the majority of it is single-turn, which the paper names as a limit on R1's multi-turn conversational ability (§B.3.3).
- Six released dense students with their exact base models and initial learning rates (Table 6, repo README model table).
- A controlled comparison of distillation against large-scale RL at 32B under the paper's own RL recipe (Table 16, §F.1).

## Technical Details
- Corpus totals: 804,745 samples, average 1.0 rounds and 5,355.3 tokens per sample; by domain, Math 395,285, Code 211,129, General 177,812, Logic 10,395, STEM 10,124 (Table 5). The paper describes this as "approximately 800,000 supervised samples".
- Reasoning share: about 600k reasoning-related samples from rejection sampling; about 200k non-reasoning samples (§B.3.3).
- Filters on reasoning data: retain only correct responses; remove chains of thought with mixed languages, long paragraphs, or code blocks (§B.3.3).
- Student base models (Table 6; identical in the repo README table): DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B from Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B; Qwen-7B from Qwen2.5-Math-7B; Qwen-14B from Qwen2.5-14B; Qwen-32B from Qwen2.5-32B; Llama-8B from Llama-3.1-8B; Llama-70B from Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. Only the 1.5B and 7B students use Qwen2.5-Math bases.
- Student results, AIME 2024 pass@1 / MATH-500 pass@1 / GPQA Diamond pass@1 / LiveCodeBench pass@1 / CodeForces rating (Table 15): 1.5B 28.9 / 83.9 / 33.8 / 16.9 / 954; 7B 55.5 / 92.8 / 49.1 / 37.6 / 1189; 14B 69.7 / 93.9 / 59.1 / 53.1 / 1481; 32B 72.6 / 94.3 / 62.1 / 57.2 / 1691; Llama-8B 50.4 / 89.1 / 49.0 / 39.6 / 1205; Llama-70B 70.0 / 94.5 / 65.2 / 57.5 / 1633. Baselines in the same table: GPT-4o-0513 9.3 on AIME 2024, Claude-3.5-Sonnet-1022 16.0.
- Distillation versus RL at 32B (Table 16): Qwen2.5-32B-Zero, produced by RL on Qwen2.5-32B-Base with math, code and STEM data for over 10K steps, scores 47.0 AIME 2024 pass@1, 91.6 MATH-500, 55.0 GPQA Diamond, 40.2 LiveCodeBench; DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B scores 72.6 / 94.3 / 62.1 / 57.2. QwQ-32B-Preview scores 50.0 / 90.6 / 54.5 / 41.9.
- The repo README states the licensing position that the Qwen-based distills are "finetuned with 800k samples curated with DeepSeek-R1", and that DeepSeek-R1 series models allow distillation for training other LLMs.

## Findings relevant to distillation
- The paper's conclusion from §F.1 has two parts: distilling a more powerful model into a smaller one works well, whereas relying on the large-scale RL of this paper at small scale requires enormous computational power and may not reach distillation performance; and advancing beyond the boundaries of human intelligence may still require more powerful base models and larger-scale RL.
- Distilled outputs beat human-generated data: the paper states that models distilled from high-quality teacher outputs consistently outperform those trained directly on human-generated data, citing Busbridge et al. (2025) (§F).
- Reported limit of the corpus: the data is predominantly single-turn, which the paper says may limit DeepSeek-R1's multi-turn conversational ability; expansion to multi-turn data is left as future work (§B.3.3).

## Findings relevant to negative feedback
- Negatives in this stage are discarded, not trained on: for each prompt multiple responses are sampled and only the correct ones are retained (§B.3.3). This is negative marginal value, not negative gradient. The paper reports no false-negative rate for the DeepSeek-V3 judge.
