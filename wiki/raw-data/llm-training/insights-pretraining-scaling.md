# Insights — Pretraining scaling and data

- At a fixed token budget, corpus selection changes downstream scores by several points. For a 1.71B model on 350B tokens, FineWeb-Edu raises MMLU from 33% to 37% against FineWeb ([[fineweb]]); domain weights found with a 280M proxy raise an 8B model's one-shot average from 20.03 to 26.56 on The Pile ([[doremi]]); sampling by a predicted topic × format mixture raises a 1B model's 9-task average from 51.6 to 54.6 ([[weborganizer]]); D4 selection reaches baseline perplexity with about 20% fewer updates at 6.7B ([[d4]]). **Replicated** across four independent pipelines.
- Repeating data is close to free for a small number of epochs and deduplication mainly changes memorization. Up to 4 epochs of repetition gives test loss almost equal to unique data (an 8.7B model ends 0.5% higher in validation loss) ([[data-constrained-scaling]]). Near-duplicate removal lowers the share of generated tokens copied from training data from 1.926% to 0.189% for a 1.5B model trained one epoch on C4 ([[deduplicating-training-data]]).
- Loss predicts downstream accuracy only for part of the task set. Re-classifying 46 downstream tasks, 18 (39%) improve smoothly and predictably with validation loss and 28 do not ([[scaling-laws-unreliable-downstream]]); on ARC-Challenge about 90% of samples have a score-compute correlation above 0.75 under log-likelihood scoring but only 40% under accuracy ([[predicting-downstream-elusive]]). Chaining a size-and-token power law to a task-loss-to-accuracy sigmoid is the fitting procedure that does work at ladder scale ([[task-scaling-model-ladders]], [[emergence-loss-perspective]]).
- Small-scale proxy runs can select data and expose instabilities before a large run. Ranking 25 pretraining corpora with 150M models picks the 1B-scale winner in about 80% of pairwise comparisons ([[datadecide]]); qk-layernorm and z-loss remove the attention-logit growth and output-logit divergence that appear at high learning rates in 2.4M-1.2B models ([[small-scale-proxies-instabilities]]). Per-domain perplexity exposes gaps a single held-out loss hides: a C4-only 1B baseline reaches perplexity up to 391,171 on the RedPajama arXiv domain ([[paloma]]).
- Published scaling exponents depend on accounting choices. Reproducing the Kaplan setup on RefinedWeb gives a compute-optimal model-size exponent of 0.835; counting the head's FLOPs lowers it to 0.706 and scaling warmup to the token count lowers it to 0.602 ([[resolving-scaling-discrepancies]]). Adding inference cost to the loss law moves the optimum toward smaller models trained on more tokens ([[beyond-chinchilla-inference-scaling]]). Data quality enters the Chinchilla form as a separate exponent fitted over 126 controlled runs ([[scaling-laws-data-quality]]).
- Parameter count bounds stored knowledge. In controlled synthetic-knowledge experiments, GPT2 models from 1M to 0.5B parameters that see each fact 1000 times store at least 2 bits of knowledge per parameter and none exceeds 2.3 ([[physics-of-lm-3]]).
