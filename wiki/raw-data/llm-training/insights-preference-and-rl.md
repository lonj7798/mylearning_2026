# Insights — Preference optimization and RL

- The preference-loss family differs in what replaces the reference model and the pair-level reward, not in the data it consumes. DPO rewrites the KL-constrained RLHF objective as binary cross-entropy on pairs ([[dpo]]); IPO removes the term that drives the dispreferred action to probability 0 when the empirical preference reaches 0 or 1 ([[ipo]]); KTO replaces pairs with per-example desirable/undesirable labels and matches or exceeds DPO from 1B to 30B ([[kto]]); SimPO removes the reference model by using average log probability per token ([[simpo]]); ORPO adds an odds-ratio term to the SFT loss ([[orpo]]). Hyperparameters do not transfer: in a sweep over β ∈ {0.01, …, 0.9} on two 7B SFT models and two datasets, the best β differs by algorithm and by model ([[hf-dpo-zoo]]).
- On-policy sampling beats offline pairs under matched data, loss, and hyperparameters. With the same pairwise data and IPO loss, on-policy sampling reaches a higher peak win rate than offline sampling on all four tested datasets ([[on-off-policy-rlhf]]); running alternating rounds of SFT-on-filtered-samples and RL outperforms one SFT plus one RL run ([[iterative-sft-rl]], [[llama-2]], [[llama-3]]). **Replicated.**
- Policy-gradient variants for LLMs mostly differ in the baseline and in normalization. RLOO drops PPO's value network because the reward arrives once at the end of a generation from a strong SFT initialization ([[rloo]], [[vanilla-pg]]); GRPO z-scores the group reward and copies the scalar to every token ([[grpo]], [[verl-grpo]]); Dr. GRPO removes the length and reward-standard-deviation divisions that bias the gradient and let response length grow ([[dr-grpo]]); REINFORCE++ normalizes advantages over the whole batch instead of the prompt group ([[reinforce-plus-plus]]); PPO's clipped surrogate and TRPO's KL constraint are the ancestors of all of these ([[ppo]], [[trpo]], [[rlhf-instructgpt]]).
- Verifiable rewards change what the RL stage can be trusted to do. Replacing the learned reward model with a binary verifier on 29,946 verifiable prompts raised Tülu 3 8B from 84.3 to 87.6 on GSM8K and from 42.0 upward on MATH ([[rlvr-tulu3]]); swapping the final PPO-with-RM stage for GRPO with verifiable rewards raises the reported 11-benchmark average ([[tulu-3.1]]); GRPO with only rule-based accuracy and format rewards raised DeepSeek-V3-Base from 15.6% to 77.9% AIME24 pass@1 ([[deepseek-r1]]). The limits are also measured: random rewards gain 21.4 points on MATH-500 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B against 29.1 for ground-truth rewards, and give little or nothing on other base models ([[spurious-rewards-rlvr]]); RLVR gains shrink or reverse at large k ([[rlvr-beyond-base-model]]).
- Entropy is the quantity that predicts whether long RL runs keep improving. Across 11 base models from 0.5B to 32B with no entropy or KL intervention, validation accuracy and policy entropy fit R = −a·exp(H) + b, with 73% of entropy consumption and 76% of the performance gain occurring early ([[entropy-mechanism-llm-rl]]); Skywork-o1 reports high rollout temperature plus adaptive entropy control as the critical-path factor at 32B ([[skywork-o1]]); prolonged RL with KL control and policy resets raised a 1.5B distilled model's six-benchmark average from 44.45 to 60.14 ([[prorl]]). None of verl, OpenRLHF, or TRL enables an entropy bonus by default ([[entropy-logging-patterns]]).
- Reward models degrade under optimization pressure and the library holds the measurements and the mitigations. Gold score first rises then falls as a policy is optimized against proxy reward models from 3M to 3B parameters ([[reward-model-overoptimization]], [[reward-hacking-taxonomy]]); conservative five-member ensembling and weight-averaged reward models both raise the achievable point on that curve ([[reward-ensembling]], [[warm-weight-averaged-reward-models]]); rubric-based judges reach 31.2 on HealthBench against 25.5 for a rubric-free Likert judge ([[rubrics-as-rewards]]); in production settings o3 reward-hacked 39 of 128 RE-Bench runs ([[metr-frontier-reward-hacking]]) and learned reward hacking co-occurred with rises on all six core misalignment evaluations ([[natural-emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking]], [[lilianweng-reward-hacking]]).
