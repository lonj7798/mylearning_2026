# bfloat16 (Google Brain Float, 2018)
- **Core Insight:** Keeping the FP32 exponent (8 bits) while truncating the mantissa from 23 to 7 bits gives a 16-bit float with the *full FP32 dynamic range* (10^{−38} to 10^{38}) at the cost of coarser relative precision — eliminating loss-scaling and overflow handling that plague FP16 in transformer training.
- **Guideline:** Default to BF16 for transformer pretraining; you get FP32 numerical headroom on activations and gradients with zero loss-scaling logic, and the 2^{−7} ≈ 7.8e−3 relative precision is sufficient for the SGD/Adam noise floor.
- **Authors:** Google Brain (introduced for Cloud TPU v2, popularized by NVIDIA Ampere)
- **Year:** 2018 (TPU); 2020 (Ampere)
- **URL:** https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/bfloat16-the-secret-to-high-performance-on-cloud-tpus ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12322
- **Relevant topics:** bfloat16, training precision, mantissa-vs-exponent tradeoff, mixed precision

## Abstract
bfloat16 ("brain float 16") truncates FP32 by dropping the low 16 bits of the mantissa — keeping the 1-bit sign and 8-bit exponent unchanged. This preserves FP32's dynamic range (~10^{77} ratio between largest and smallest normal) and makes BF16↔FP32 conversion a bit-shift, but reduces the mantissa to 7 explicit bits + 1 implicit = 8 effective bits, giving relative precision ε ≈ 2^{−7} ≈ 0.78%. The format was introduced in Google's TPU v2 and adopted by NVIDIA Ampere (A100, 2020) and every major accelerator since. It has displaced FP16 for transformer training because attention and softmax accumulators routinely exceed the FP16 representable range, requiring loss scaling.

## Key Contributions
- Same exponent as FP32 → no overflow/underflow re-engineering, no loss scaling.
- 16-bit width → 2× memory and bandwidth savings over FP32.
- Mantissa truncation → relative precision ~1% vs FP16's ~0.05%; empirically sufficient for SGD-noise-bounded training.
- Cheap FP32 ↔ BF16 conversion (drop / pad low 16 bits with rounding).
- De facto default for transformer pretraining 2020–present.

## Technical Details

### Bit layout
| Field | Bits | Width |
|-------|------|-------|
| sign s | 15 | 1 |
| exponent e | 14..7 | 8 |
| mantissa m | 6..0 | 7 |

Value (normal): `(−1)^s · (1 + m/2^7) · 2^{e − 127}`
**Exponent bias = 127 (same as FP32).**

### Key constants
- Smallest positive normal: 2^{−126} ≈ 1.18e−38 (same as FP32)
- Largest finite: (2 − 2^{−7}) · 2^{127} ≈ 3.39e+38 (same as FP32, modulo mantissa)
- Machine epsilon ε = 2^{−7} ≈ 7.81e−3
- Subnormals: defined (e=0, m≠0), but BF16 subnormal range is tiny and most hardware treats subnormals as FTZ.
- ±∞ (e=255, m=0), NaN (e=255, m≠0): present, same encoding as FP32.

### Relationship to FP32
BF16 is exactly the **top 16 bits of FP32**. Conversion:
- FP32 → BF16: round bottom 16 mantissa bits to nearest even (RNE); cheap, no exponent re-bias.
- BF16 → FP32: append 16 zero bits; exact, lossless.

This makes BF16 ideal for *casts inside compute kernels*: load FP32 weights, broadcast as BF16 for tensor-core MAC, accumulate back in FP32.

### Precision vs FP16
| | FP16 (binary16) | BF16 |
|---|---|---|
| Sign | 1 | 1 |
| Exponent | 5 (bias 15) | 8 (bias 127) |
| Mantissa | 10 | 7 |
| Max finite | 65504 | 3.39e+38 |
| Min positive normal | 6.10e−5 | 1.18e−38 |
| ε (relative precision) | 9.77e−4 | 7.81e−3 |

BF16 trades ~8× relative precision for ~10^{33}× dynamic range. For transformer training the precision gap doesn't matter (SGD noise dominates); the range gap does (attention scores routinely > 65k).

### Why FP16 needs loss scaling and BF16 doesn't
FP16's smallest normal is 6e−5 → gradients smaller than that underflow to 0. Loss scaling (multiply loss by 2^k before backward, divide gradients by 2^k after) shifts gradients into the representable range. BF16's smallest normal is 1e−38 → gradients essentially never underflow → no loss scaling needed.

### Hardware support
- **Google TPU v2+** (2018+): native BF16 matmul, FP32 accum.
- **NVIDIA Ampere (A100) onward**: BF16 tensor cores at 312 TFLOPS, FP32 accum.
- **Intel Cooper Lake / Sapphire Rapids**: AVX-512-BF16 (`VCVTNE2PS2BF16`, `VDPBF16PS`).
- **AMD MI100+**, **Apple Neural Engine**, **Cerebras**, etc.: all native BF16.

### Use in mixed-precision training
Standard recipe:
- Master weights, optimizer state, loss accumulator: FP32.
- Forward activations, weights for matmul, gradient: BF16.
- Matmul: BF16 × BF16 → FP32 accumulator → BF16 output.
- No loss scaling; no overflow handling.

### Limitations
- 7-bit mantissa is sometimes insufficient for *small* models or very-end-of-training fine-tuning (gradient signal becomes precision-bounded).
- For LoRA fine-tuning at very low LR, sometimes need FP32 master copy of LoRA params.
- BF16 is *not* in IEEE-754 (no subnormal guarantees, encodings vary by vendor for FTZ).
