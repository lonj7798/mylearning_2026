# realtime_voice vs Pipecat — the capstone question is "keep or replace", not "how to port"
<!-- slug: rtv-vs-pipecat-gap · type: boson · source: packages/realtime_voice/ (3,886 L src + 1,504 L tests) vs pipecat-src/ (168,847 L src + 92,538 L tests) -->

**Core Insight.** The learner has already shipped a working Pipecat-equivalent. That reframes the course: this is not a porting exercise, it is a **build-vs-buy decision on an artifact that already exists and already passes 60 tests**. And the honest reading is that realtime_voice *wins on the two things Lina TMR actually sells* — Korean phrase chunking and audible-prefix interruption accounting — while losing on everything that is breadth: providers (2 vs 62), transports (1 vs 12), telephony (0 vs 6 serializers), observability (an unread counter vs a whole observer plane), and streaming STT (it has none).

**Guideline.** Decide per-layer, not per-framework. The layers where Pipecat's 43× larger codebase buys you something are transport breadth, provider breadth, and instrumentation. The layers where boson's 3,886 lines encode Korean-dental-specific knowledge — `KoreanPhraseChunker`, `AudioTextPlayoutLedger`, `GatewayConversationAgent` — are exactly the layers Pipecat does not have opinions about, so a hybrid ("Pipecat transport + our chunker/ledger as custom processors") is a real option, not a cop-out.

## Technical Details

### Feature-by-feature

|---|---|---|---|

| Processor abstraction | **No.** One `VoiceSession` (561 L) with a fixed VAD→ASR→agent→TTS order; 5 swappable `Protocol` slots | Yes. `FrameProcessor` doubly-linked via `link()`, two tasks/two queues each, `Pipeline`/`ParallelPipeline` ([[frame-processor]]) | boson has no `FrameDirection`, no upstream push, no mid-pipeline insertion |

### Test coverage as maturity evidence

`packages/realtime_voice/tests/` = **6 files, 1,504 L, 60 test functions** against 3,886 L of source (0.39 test:src ratio; `asyncio_mode = "auto"`). The names are the tell — these are *contract* tests, not smoke tests:

- `test_session_pipeline.py` (483 L, 15 tests): `test_confirmed_barge_in_purges_server_audio_and_cancels_old_work`, `test_barge_in_event_precedes_slow_provider_cancellation`, `test_late_stale_asr_final_is_dropped`, `test_playout_acknowledgement_returns_only_heard_prefix`, `test_late_playout_ack_cannot_extend_interrupted_audible_prefix`, `test_next_turn_after_completed_playout_is_not_an_interruption`, `test_each_generation_has_exactly_one_terminal_event`, `test_ingress_overflow_rejects_frame_instead_of_growing_latency`, `test_two_sessions_do_not_share_generation_or_provider_state`, `test_interim_asr_is_observable_but_never_calls_agent`.
- `test_chunking_and_ledger.py` (270 L, 22 tests): `test_safety_split_never_breaks_a_long_latin_token`, `test_dots_inside_ascii_identifier_are_not_sentence_boundaries`, `test_overlong_token_waits_across_streamed_deltas_for_whitespace`, `test_playout_ledgers_are_generation_isolated`.
- `test_webrtc_transport.py` (257 L, 6 tests) includes a real `test_aiortc_loopback_negotiates_ordered_control_channel` and `test_outbound_track_underflow_is_explicit_zero_pcm_silence` (the PyAV-uninitialized-memory bug, pinned).
- `realtime_voice/testing/fakes.py` (271 L) ships `ScriptedVAD`, `FakeStreamingASR`, `FakeStreamingConversationAgent`, `FakeStreamingTTS`, `FakeVoiceTransport`, `FakeMonotonicClock` as **public API** (exported from `realtime_voice.testing`) — determinism was designed in, not retrofitted.

Every line of `CLAUDE.md`'s "Contracts That Must Survive" has a named test. For scale: Pipecat is 226 test files / 92,538 L / 3,959 test functions (0.55 ratio). boson's suite is small but not thin — it is precisely aimed at the eight invariants that break voice UX.

- **Migration angle:** The capstone question is **"which do we keep?"**, and the layered answer the evidence supports is: (1) **Replace** the VAD layer — Pipecat's is strictly better and boson's `threshold=0.5`, 2-state, 16-kHz-only analyzer is the sharpest correctness risk in the stack. (2) **Replace or wrap** the ASR layer regardless of framework — unary ASR is the P50 killer, and this is true whether or not Pipecat is adopted. (3) **Keep** `KoreanPhraseChunker` and `AudioTextPlayoutLedger` — Pipecat has no equivalent and they encode Korean-dental knowledge. (4) **Keep** `WebRTCSessionManager` + `ControlEvent` — Pipecat has no equivalent and they are the auth/protocol contract. (5) The **irreducible collision is the agent boundary**: adopting Pipecat's pipeline means Pipecat owns the LLM call and the tool loop, which contradicts `CLAUDE.md`'s "Basement and Gateway must not import provider-specific audio code / keep business logic text-native." Wrapping Gateway as a single custom `FrameProcessor` that consumes `LLMContextFrame` and emits `LLMTextFrame` is the only way both survive — and that is the design question worth spending the capstone on.
